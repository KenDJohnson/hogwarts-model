## Kitchens

The Hogwarts kitchens are one enormous high-ceilinged stone room in the basement, directly beneath the Great Hall and equal to it in size, so the floor shares the Hall’s modelling minimum of about 1,200 m². Four long wooden tables stand exactly under the four house tables above, and Harry supposes that food rises from them to the matching tables overhead. Brass pots and pans are heaped around the walls and a large brick fireplace fills the far end. At least a hundred house-elves work here. The way in starts at the door to the right of the marble staircase, descends one stone flight to a broad, bright corridor hung with pictures of food, and passes through a painting of a silver fruit bowl, opened by tickling its pear. The same basement corridor serves the Hufflepuffs.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** An enormous room with a high ceiling, matching the Great Hall overhead in size. (B04-108)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 1,200 m² of floor, for example 50 m by 24 m, the same as the Great Hall. Because: The text equates the two rooms, so the Great Hall’s minimum carries straight over: about 100 tables × about 12 seats × an assumed 1 m² per seat = about 1,200 m², fitted to a span of fifty yards (46 m). The four long tables here lie under the house tables, so the plan has to match the Hall’s as well. Anything smaller could not keep those tables in line with the ones above. (B04-108, B04-109, B04-124, B07-129)
- **Explicit.** At least a hundred house-elves stood in the room at once in 1994, all wearing Hogwarts-crested tea-towels. (B04-109)
- **Reported.** Nearly Headless Nick says the school has over a hundred house-elves, more than any other dwelling in Britain, mostly in the kitchens by day and out cleaning at night. (B04-016)
- **Explicit.** The 1994 Christmas lunch needed a hundred or more turkeys. (B04-245)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** In the basement, directly under the Great Hall, on the same corridor that serves the Hufflepuffs. (B04-108, B05-115, B06-129)
- **Inference.** The floor lies one storey below the ground floor, at the same depth as the dungeons. Because: The kitchens sit under the Great Hall, the way in is one flight of stone steps below the Entrance Hall, and the narrator expects that flight to end in a passage like the one to Snape’s dungeon. The dungeons are eight floors below the seventh floor, which in British numbering is one level under the ground floor. (B04-106, B04-108, B05-110)
- **Inference.** Harry’s 1991 belief that he was five floors above the kitchens would put that dark corridor near the library on the fourth floor. Because: One level below ground plus five floors is the fourth floor in British numbering. It is only Harry’s guess, made in the dark in a corridor he did not recognise. (B01-081, B05-110)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Stone walls and a stone-flagged floor, with a large brick fireplace at the far end, its brickwork blackened by smoke, and a stool beside it. (B04-108, B04-179)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Way in: the door to the right of the marble staircase (left when coming down it), then a flight of stone steps to a broad, brightly torch-lit stone corridor hung with food paintings. (B04-062, B04-106)
- **Explicit.** Partway along the corridor a painting of a giant silver fruit bowl hides the door: tickling its green pear turns the pear into a door handle. (B04-107)
- **Reported.** Pupils can get in: Fred and George report repeated visits for food, and the elves press snacks on visitors. (B01-090, B04-040, B04-109)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Four long wooden tables stand exactly under the four house tables, bare after dinner. Brass pots and pans lie heaped around the walls. (B04-108, B04-109)
- **Reported.** Harry remembers armour standing close to the kitchens. (B01-081)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** Harry supposes food is laid on these tables, then rises through the ceiling to the matching tables in the Hall. No one sees this happen. (B04-110)
- **Reported.** Nick reports that Peeves threw the pots and pans about and flooded the room with soup before the feast on 1 September 1994. (B04-015)
- **Reported.** Kreacher was sent to work here in summer 1996. (B06-002)
- **Explicit.** May 1998: the door leading here from the Entrance Hall was blasted off its hinges as the house-elves charged out with kitchen knives. (B07-128, B07-079)

### Open questions

- The room is high-ceilinged yet only one flight of steps below the Entrance Hall, with the Great Hall floor over it. Decide how that works: a long flight, or a kitchen floor sunk below corridor level.
- No ovens, ranges, sinks, stores or windows are described, and nothing says which end of the Hall the fireplace lies under.
