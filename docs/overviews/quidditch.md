# Quidditch grounds and tournament arenas

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/quidditch.md)

Walking down the sloping lawns from the front steps, with the Forest on the right, the stadium comes up as a landmark seen from much of the castle. A tree marks about the halfway point. At the foot of the slope stand the changing rooms, at the pitch's edge, with a separate broom shed across the grass. A gap in the stands opens on to the pitch, a flat grass ground with three hoops at each end, fifty feet high. The banked stands around it take the whole school, so model at least 800 to 1,000 seats, the top of 'hundreds', probably in separate blocks. The pitch is at least about 200 yards long on one line, as the 1995 maze shows: twenty-foot hedges filled it, with fifty yards to the first fork and a hundred-yard run to the Cup at the centre. The first-task arena lay elsewhere, a long way round the Forest edge behind a clump of trees, out of sight of the castle and the lake and about ten hurried minutes from the front doors. Its conjured seats ringed a plank-fenced field that had held four dragons.

## Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Hoops are fifty feet high; the maze hedge that later filled the pitch was twenty feet high. (B01-054, B03-032, B04-210)
- **Inference.** The pitch is at least about 200 yards long on one line. Because: The Cup, at the centre, was seen a hundred yards away from inside a maze whose outer hedge followed the pitch's edge. (B04-186, B04-210, B04-218)
- **Estimate.** Seat the stadium for at least 800 to 1,000, the whole school plus staff; this stays within the text's 'hundreds'. Because: A crowd of about 800 follows from 200 people in green being the quarter not in scarlet. Over a hundred pupils in one exam year gives 700 or more over seven years. Assumes one seat each. (B01-156, B02-170, B03-171, B06-220, B01-054, B03-106, B05-219)

- **Explicit.** The first-task arena lay out of sight of castle and lake, about ten hurried minutes from the front doors. (B04-078)

## How it changes

- **Explicit.** Dated installations: conjured stands at the dragon enclosure (November 1994), the same seats by the lake (February 1995), and the maze on the pitch (May to June 1995). (B04-092, B04-158, B04-186, B04-210)

## Quidditch pitch

The Quidditch pitch is the school's flying field, a flat grass ground inside the stadium, set downhill from the castle at the foot of sloping lawns. Three hoops on golden poles stand at each end, fifty feet high. No dimensions are stated, but the maze of 1995 shows how large it is: the Cup stood at the centre with a straight hundred-yard run to it, and the outer hedge followed the pitch's edge, so the pitch is at least about 200 yards long on that line. The castle overlooks it from a distance, with players visible but not recognisable from the third floor, and from the pitch the front doors appear far off. The Forest lies to the right of anyone walking down, and Hagrid's cabin is the nearest shelter. Compass clues put it west or north-west of the castle. The ground turns to mud in rain and freezes hard in winter.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Three goal hoops stand at each end, six in all, each a golden pole ending in a ring. (B01-054, B02-001, B05-068, B05-123, B06-044)
- **Explicit.** The hoops are fifty feet high, a figure given twice for Hogwarts and again for the World Cup. (B01-054, B03-032, B04-257)
- **Inference.** The pitch is at least about 200 yards (180 m) long on one line. Because: The Cup was to stand at the centre of a maze filling the pitch, with the outer hedge at the pitch's edge. Harry saw it a hundred yards off from inside the maze, so centre to edge is at least that. (B04-210, B04-186, B04-218)
- **Explicit.** Play uses the full height: Harry climbs to about fifty feet in practice, and Fred puts a fall at about fifty feet. (B03-082, B03-053)
- **Estimate.** Keep the stadium at least about 300 yards (270 m) from the castle. Because: Figures at the hoops cannot be told apart from the third floor, which needs a few hundred yards, and the front doors look distant from the pitch. Wood's fifteen minutes, dressing included, sets an upper limit of perhaps 600 yards of lawn. (B05-058, B03-105, B02-171)
- **Reported.** Wood allows Harry fifteen minutes to dress and get from the dormitory down to the pitch, which caps the walk from Gryffindor Tower. (B02-171)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It lies downhill of the castle: sloping lawns lead down from the front steps, and every return is uphill. (B01-055, B02-025, B05-066, B05-122, B05-125, B06-043, B05-103)
- **Explicit.** Walking down to it, the Forest is on the right and the Owlery tower stands in view. (B05-066)
- **Explicit.** The stadium is passed on the way from the front doors to the drive and gates. (B05-175)
- **Explicit.** Castle windows overlook it from far off: upstairs windows, McGonagall's office, the Headmaster's office and a corridor near the hospital wing. (B01-061, B03-048, B05-291, B05-301)
- **Explicit.** From Umbridge's third-floor office the figures at the hoops are visible but cannot be told apart. (B05-058)
- **Explicit.** From the pitch the castle's front doors can be seen opening in the distance. (B03-105)
- **Explicit.** A tree stands about halfway to the castle, on the left going up. (B03-083)
- **Reported.** Harry calls Hagrid's cabin the nearest refuge, and Hagrid can follow a match from it with binoculars. A walk from the stadium towards the Forest passes it. (B02-028, B01-067, B04-187, B06-045)
- **Explicit.** From the air over the pitch, heavy rain on the lake is audible at a distance. (B05-103)
- **Inference.** The pitch probably lies west or north-west of the castle. Because: Inside the maze entrance Harry needed north-west for the centre, so champions coming from the castle entered on the south-east. From the broom shed the castle windows glow red at sunset, which needs a viewer on the sunset side. (B04-212, B04-210, B01-087)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** It is smooth flat grass when in normal order. (B04-186, B03-082)
- **Explicit.** Its plan is not stated. The only Quidditch pitch given a shape, at the World Cup, is a long oval. (B04-257)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The way in is an opening between stands, and teams walk straight out from the changing rooms. (B04-185, B01-065, B03-051, B05-123)
- **Explicit.** Use is by booking: captains reserve time, and in 1995 Snape books it repeatedly for Slytherin. (B05-054, B05-120)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** The surface changes with the weather: frost and hard ground in cold spells, deep mud at the centre in rain. (B01-085, B02-065, B03-032, B03-105, B05-103, B05-124, B06-072)
- **Explicit.** May to June 1995: it carries the third-task maze, first as low hedges, then twenty feet high. It is back in normal use by September 1995. (B04-186, B04-210, B05-068)
- **Explicit.** November 1993: at least a hundred Dementors stand on the ground during a match. (B03-053)

### Open questions

- Plan shape and width. Only one long dimension can be inferred. A long oval fits the game, but Hogwarts is never described in plan.
- Distance from the castle. It is always called distant, and no figure is given.

## Quidditch stands

The stands are the raised spectator seating around the Quidditch pitch, built up in banked benches with a front row, a top row and rows between. The text says hundreds of seats, yet the whole school sits here for matches, and the 1994 final implies a crowd of about 800, so model the seats at the very top of 'hundreds': at least 800 to 1,000. They rise high enough for a clear view, though many pupils still bring binoculars. A barrier separates seats from pitch, and stairs lead down to the lawn. The stands have edges and gaps, and one person crosses from stand to stand during a match, so they read as separate blocks rather than a closed ring. A wooden commentator's podium stands among them. Supporters gather at opposite ends. The text mentions no roofs or towers.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Hundreds of seats stand raised round the pitch, and hundreds of footsteps are heard climbing the benches before a match. (B01-054, B05-123)
- **Explicit.** The whole school turns out for matches in any weather, and the stadium is described as packed. (B01-156, B02-170, B03-171, B06-220, B03-051)
- **Inference.** The crowd at the 1994 final was about 800. Because: Three-quarters of the crowd wore scarlet, and 200 people behind the Slytherin goal wore green. If those 200 were the remaining quarter, the crowd was about 800. (B03-106)
- **Estimate.** Seat at least 800 to 1,000 people, the whole school plus staff. That stays within 'hundreds', at the top end; do not build for two or three hundred. Because: Crowd arithmetic gives about 800. Over a hundred pupils sat one O.W.L. exam, so seven years is 700 or more. About 100 coaches carrying four or five older pupils gives 400 to 500 at the least, plus first-years. Assumes one seat each. (B01-156, B02-170, B03-171, B06-220, B03-106, B05-219, B03-006, B04-004, B05-008)
- **Estimate.** At 0.5 m a person, 800 to 1,000 people need 400 to 500 m of bench, for example ten rows of 40 to 50 m. Because: Bench width is an assumption and no row count is given. This also shows that many rows in a closed ring round a 200-yard pitch would seat thousands, not hundreds. (B01-054, B05-123)
- **Explicit.** Many pupils carry binoculars, play can still be hard to follow from the seats, and Ron uses them to watch a teacher in the stand opposite. (B01-156, B02-170, B03-171, B06-220, B01-065)
- **Explicit.** Named tiers include the front row, a few rows up, halfway up, the second-highest row and the top row. (B03-106, B05-179, B05-068, B05-242, B01-065, B03-052)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The stands surround the pitch close to its edge, with one stand directly across from another and supporters massed at the two ends. (B01-054, B01-065, B06-044, B06-072, B03-106)
- **Explicit.** The stadium is a landmark: the Headmaster's window shows it in the distance, the walk to the gates passes it, and it comes into view at the end of a ten-minute Forest path. (B05-291, B05-175, B05-250)
- **Explicit.** From the end of a stand the stretch of lawn to Hagrid's cabin can be checked. (B05-242)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Seating is banked benches raised high in the air, with people able to pass along behind a row. (B05-123, B01-065, B01-156, B02-170, B03-171, B06-220, B05-242)
- **Inference.** The stands probably form separate blocks, not a closed ring. Because: One pupil crosses to the stand where a teacher sits, there is a gap through to the pitch, and the stands have an edge to look round. (B01-065, B04-185, B05-242)
- **Explicit.** A barrier runs between seats and pitch, low enough to vault and to pour over. (B03-082, B03-106)
- **Explicit.** Sound carries: jeers from halfway up echo round the empty stadium, one camera's clicks sound loud, and commentary echoes over the grounds. (B02-027, B05-068, B06-114)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Stairs lead from the rows down to the lawn, and rows are tight enough that people must stand to let others past. (B05-242, B06-043)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** A wooden commentator's podium stands within sight of the play, used with a magical megaphone. (B06-073, B06-074, B06-114, B03-106)
- **Explicit.** Banners, flags and rosettes are brought by supporters for a match, not fixed decoration. (B01-066, B03-106, B06-072)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** June 1995: the stands stay in place around the maze, and hundreds of students file into them for the third task. (B04-210, B04-223)
- **Explicit.** Autumn 1996: a collision reduces the podium to wooden wreckage. A podium is in use again by March 1997. (B06-074, B06-114)

### Conflicts

- Class-size clues point to a much smaller school: twenty brooms for a two-house first-year class and about twenty earmuffs for a two-house Herbology class suggest under 300 pupils, against a crowd of about 800 and over a hundred pupils in one exam year. (B01-041, B02-020, B03-106, B05-219)

### Open questions

- Layout of the stands: how many blocks and rows. Separate blocks fit hundreds of seats around so large a pitch; a closed ring does not.
- Support structure and height. The text gives no towers, roofs or canopies, so do not add them on assumption.

## Quidditch changing rooms and broom shed

The changing rooms stand beside the Quidditch pitch, at the foot of the sloping lawns. They form a real building with a roof, a window and doors, and teams walk straight out of it on to the pitch. Inside are benches facing each other, cloak pegs, a cupboard for the balls and a separate Captain's office; showers are mentioned once. A room takes a team of seven with space for a captain to stand and brief them. The text names a Gryffindor changing room and has the opposing team coming from the far side of the pitch, so there are probably at least two rooms. A separate broom shed with a wooden door stands across the damp grass, with a view up to the castle's windows and front steps.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** A room seats a team of seven on opposite sides, one side against a wall, with space for a captain and his boards. (B02-026, B03-032)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** They stand beside the pitch, at the foot of the sloping lawns. (B03-032, B02-025, B05-122)
- **Explicit.** Players file straight out of them on to the pitch. (B01-065, B05-123)
- **Inference.** There are probably at least two rooms, on opposite sides of the pitch. Because: A room is named as Gryffindor's, and in a match the opposing team walks out from the other side. (B03-172, B05-320, B03-051)
- **Explicit.** The broom shed stands apart, a walk across damp grass from the changing room, and has a wooden door. (B01-087)
- **Explicit.** From the shed the castle's windows and front steps can be seen up the slope. (B01-087)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** It is a true building, warm and lit, with a roof that rain drums on, a door and a window; wind can be heard around it. (B05-102, B06-069)

### Getting there

### What is inside

## First-task arena

The first-task arena was the dragon enclosure of Book 4 as fitted out for 24 November 1994: a plank-fenced field at the Forest edge with conjured stands around it. It lay a long way round the Forest perimeter, behind a clump of trees, so that neither castle nor lake could be seen from it, about ten hurried minutes from the front doors. It had held four fully grown dragons and at least thirty keepers at once, so it was large, and the stands took hundreds and hundreds of spectators. Champions entered through a gap in the fence at one end. The nest lay at the far end, where five judges also sat in raised gold-draped seats. Before the gap stood the champions' tent, and outside the fence a first-aid tent divided into canvas cubicles. The seats were moved to the lake for the next task.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** It had held four fully grown dragons at once, each as big as a small hill, with at least thirty keepers. (B04-079)
- **Explicit.** Rearing dragons brought their mouths to fifty feet. Keepers warn of fire reaching twenty feet, and forty for the Horntail. (B04-079, B04-081)
- **Explicit.** Many hundreds of faces looked down from the stands, and the crowd's feet were heard passing the tent on the way in. (B04-092, B04-091)
- **Estimate.** Seat at least 800 to 1,000: the school, staff and guests. Because: All pupils were released to attend, and the same seats were later crammed full by the lake. The 1994 stadium crowd was about 800. Assumes one seat each. (B04-092, B04-158, B03-106)
- **Estimate.** Allow an enclosure about 100 yards long at the least. Because: Four dragons taken as about 50 feet long, kept 40 feet out of each other's fire, need about 120 yards in a line, or a block about 60 yards square. Harry also has to squint up the field to read the judges' marks. (B04-079, B04-081, B04-096)
- **Explicit.** From Harry's height the crowd's faces shrink to specks and the Horntail looks dog-sized. (B04-093)
- **Explicit.** The Horntail's spread wings match a small aeroplane, and her tail cuts yard-long gouges in hard ground. (B04-093, B04-092)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It is the same spot as the dragon holding enclosure: the stands were conjured where Harry had stood on his earlier visit. (B04-092, B04-079)
- **Explicit.** It lies so far round the Forest perimeter that neither castle nor lake is in view. (B04-078)
- **Explicit.** A clump of trees screens it. The champions' tent stands in front of those trees, facing the approach. (B04-091, B04-097)
- **Explicit.** Going back, Harry had just under fifteen minutes in hand on leaving and under five at the front doors: about ten hurried minutes, with one stop. (B04-078)
- **Explicit.** The first-aid tent stands outside the enclosure. (B04-094)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** The field is fenced with thick wooden planks, with a gap as the entrance. (B04-079, B04-092)
- **Explicit.** Stands rise above the entrant and ringed the enclosure; the ground is hard and has at least one rock. (B04-092, B04-095, B04-158)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** From the castle the way is down the front steps and round the Forest edge. Lessons stop at midday so that everyone can get there. (B04-091)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Five judges sit in gold-draped raised seats at the far end. (B04-096)
- **Explicit.** The champions' tent has low wooden stools and room to pace. The first-aid tent is split into cubicles by canvas. (B04-091, B04-094)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** The stands appeared between Harry's night visit and 24 November 1994. By February 1995 the same seats had been moved to the lake. (B04-092, B04-158)
- **Reported.** Ron reports that Krum's dragon crushed about half its real eggs during the task. (B04-095)

### Open questions

- Bearing from the castle. The text gives only a long way round the Forest edge.

## Third-task maze

The third-task maze was a temporary hedge maze grown over the whole Quidditch pitch for 24 June 1995. A hedge twenty feet high ran round the pitch's edge with one entrance gap, and the hedges inside had been low growths a month before. The text calls it vast. The first fork lay about fifty yards in, and near the end a straight run of about a hundred yards led to the Triwizard Cup on a plinth at the centre, which makes the maze at least about 200 yards across. The centre lay north-west of the entrance. Paths were wide enough for a ten-foot Skrewt and a sphinx, and the thick hedges cut off the noise of the crowd in the stands around it. Four staff patrolled the outside. Creatures and spells blocked the way.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The hedges are twenty feet high: planned in May and seen at that height in June. (B04-186, B04-210)
- **Explicit.** The maze fills the pitch, with the outer hedge running right round its edge, and the text calls it vast. (B04-210)
- **Explicit.** The first fork is about fifty yards in from the entrance. (B04-211)
- **Explicit.** From the last fork the Cup is seen on its plinth a hundred yards away, down a straight path. (B04-218)
- **Inference.** With the Cup at the centre, the maze is at least about 200 yards across on that line. Because: Bagman says the Cup goes in the centre, and the outer hedge is at the pitch's edge, so the centre is at least the hundred yards of that last straight from the edge. (B04-186, B04-218, B04-210)
- **Explicit.** Creatures show the clearance needed: a ten-foot Skrewt, a lion-bodied sphinx, a twelve-foot Dementor shape, and a spider visible above a hedge. (B04-215, B04-217, B04-213, B04-218)
- **Estimate.** Allow paths at least 6 to 10 feet (2 to 3 m) wide. Because: The sphinx paces from side to side of a path, a ten-foot Skrewt charges along one, and a spider's legs fill another. Widths assumed from those bodies. (B04-215, B04-217, B04-218)
- **Explicit.** Harry spends ten minutes among dead ends in one stretch and a few minutes on another path; the total time is not given. (B04-215, B04-216)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It fills the Quidditch pitch inside the spectator stands, and the return to the castle runs over the grass by the lake and the ship. (B04-210, B04-223, B04-224)
- **Explicit.** From just inside, the centre lies north-west, and later Harry finds he has strayed too far east. (B04-212)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Hedges are dense with brambles. Getting through one takes a blasted hole and hard kicking. (B04-216)
- **Explicit.** Grass paths include forks, dead ends, parallel runs, crossings and long straights. (B04-212, B04-216, B04-217, B04-218)
- **Explicit.** Crowd noise vanishes on entry, whether from hedge or enchantment, though Bagman's whistle and a scream still carry. (B04-211, B04-214)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** One gap faces those arriving on the pitch. Champions go in at whistle blasts, in order of points. (B04-210, B04-211, B04-186)
- **Explicit.** Four staff marked with glowing red stars patrol the outside, and red sparks bring rescue. (B04-210)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** The Cup stands on a plinth at the centre, and that day it has been made into a Portkey. (B04-218, B04-219, B04-221, B04-222, B04-232)
- **Explicit.** Set obstacles are a Boggart, golden mist that swaps ground and sky, Blast-Ended Skrewts, a sphinx and a giant spider. (B04-213, B04-214, B04-215, B04-217, B04-218)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** May 1995: the hedges are low enough to climb over. A month later they stand twenty feet high. (B04-186, B04-210)
- **Explicit.** Damage during the task: one hedge holed, and another flattened by the falling spider. (B04-216, B04-218)
- **Explicit.** Bagman promises the pitch back afterwards, and it is in ordinary use by September 1995. (B04-186, B05-068)
- **Reported.** The impostor Moody says he cleared obstacles from the patrol line, so the route was emptier than designed. (B04-229)

### Conflicts

- Narration returns Harry to the maze edge below the stands; Hermione later speaks of mid-lawn. Use the narrated position. (B04-223, B05-046)

### Open questions

- Full plan of the maze. The route fixes only local forks and turns.
- Whether the goal hoops stayed up during the maze. The text does not mention them.
