# Hogsmeade connections and secret tunnels

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/hogsmeade-and-tunnels.md)

Hogsmeade lies outside the Hogwarts gates, downhill from the castle at the foot of a mountain, and everything in this area joins the school to it. The ordinary way is the road: through the winged-boar gates, left, and down a twisting lane to the High Street with its shops, the Three Broomsticks, Honeydukes and, up a side street, the Hog’s Head. Past the Three Broomsticks a slope climbs to the Shrieking Shack. Hidden tunnels make the same journey underground. From the third floor, the one-eyed witch passage drops by stone chute, runs low and winding for what feels like over an hour, and climbs two hundred steps and more into Honeydukes’ cellar. From the middle of the grounds, under the Whomping Willow, a still lower tunnel of similar felt length reaches the ground floor of the Shack. Those two belong to the seven passages on the Marauder’s Map. From spring 1998 a lamplit passage joins the Room of Requirement to the Hog’s Head. Treat the two older tunnels as works a kilometre or more long. The village core, by contrast, is tiny, with a minute between its two pubs.

## Size and numbers

- **Reported.** Seven mapped passages lead out of the castle, and the Room-made Hog’s Head passage of 1998 makes an eighth. (B03-062, B07-045)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: Honeydukes tunnel about 2 km, Willow tunnel about 1 km, Hog’s Head passage over 400 m. None is stated; each comes from felt or narrated walking time. Because: See each passage’s own estimate. Taken at face value, the Honeydukes walk gives 3.5 km. (B03-064, B03-119, B07-044)
- **Inference.** Only one route carried a crowd: hundreds of pupils left by the Hog’s Head passage in 1998, while the Willow and Honeydukes tunnels are tight for one person. Because: The older tunnels are narrow and low, while the 1998 passage is lamplit and walked upright. (B07-069, B03-063, B03-133)

## Where it is

- **Explicit.** The village lies downhill of the castle, and its High Street is reached from the gates by road or twisting lane. (B05-078, B04-135, B06-054)
- **Explicit.** The tunnels start from three different levels: the third floor, ground level in mid-grounds, and an upper room of a village inn, ending at a cellar, a ground-floor room and the Room of Requirement. (B03-059, B03-118, B07-039, B07-044)

## How it changes

- **Reported.** The links open and close: Dementors patrol the village in 1993–94, passages are guarded from 1996, sealed by 1997–98, and a new one appears in spring 1998. (B03-066, B06-193, B07-045, B07-050)

## Open questions

- The real distance from the castle to the village. All surface and tunnel lengths depend on it, and no figure is given.

## Hogsmeade village

Hogsmeade is a small all-wizard village outside the Hogwarts gates, at the foot of a mountain, and by report the only settlement of its kind in Britain. It is the weekend destination for pupils from third year. From the gates a road, hedged and twisting, runs downhill to the High Street, a curving main street of little thatched cottages and shops with side streets off it. Named premises are Honeydukes, Zonko’s, Dervish and Banges, Scrivenshaft’s, Gladrags, the post office, Madam Puddifoot’s, the Three Broomsticks and the Hog’s Head. The core is compact, a minute from one pub to the other’s corner, yet the post office alone holds hundreds of owls and the street can absorb the upper school on a visit day. Past Dervish and Banges the street becomes a winding lane with sparser cottages, ending at a stile below the mountain. The Shrieking Shack stands on a hill a little above the houses. The distance from the castle is never stated.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** Reported to be Britain’s only entirely non-Muggle settlement. (B03-004, B04-075)
- **Explicit.** The core is tight: a minute from the Three Broomsticks to the Hog’s Head corner, and only minutes to the pub from Honeydukes or Madam Puddifoot’s. (B06-170, B03-067, B05-177)
- **Inference.** From the shops to the stile at the far end is no more than a half-hour walk. Because: The pupils set off at half past one for a meeting at the stile at two o’clock. (B04-175)
- **Explicit.** The post office holds at least three hundred owls on shelves, from Great Greys to palm-sized Scops owls kept for local post. (B03-093, B03-038)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: 90 m of perch shelving in the post office, at 0.3 m an owl. On five tiers that is about 18 m of wall. Because: Three hundred owls and shelves are stated; the spacing per owl and the five tiers are assumptions. (B03-093, B03-038)
- **Explicit.** Visit days fill the place: pupils crowd the road and the High Street, and Zonko’s is so packed that moving is hard. (B05-175, B03-093, B06-056)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Outside the Hogwarts gates: turn left between the winged-boar pillars on to the village road. (B05-078, B05-175, B06-170)
- **Explicit.** It lies downhill of the castle: the way back climbs a bending lane with a hedgerow, up to the gates. (B04-135, B04-178, B06-054)
- **Explicit.** It sits at the foot of a mountain that throws its shadow over it, with black mountains in view beyond the street. (B04-175, B07-035, B05-287)
- **Explicit.** It lies far enough north that the main street is still dark after sunrise at Shell Cottage. (B07-031)
- **Explicit.** The sky over Hogwarts can be seen from the High Street: in June 1997 the Dark Mark above the school is in plain view. (B06-173)
- **Explicit.** Flying to the castle means crossing the twisting lane, then the boundary wall, into the school grounds. (B06-175)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Little thatched cottages and shops, with trees along the street that carry enchanted candles at Christmas. (B03-067)
- **Explicit.** The High Street is the spine. It curves towards the Hogwarts road, and side streets leave it, some narrow. (B07-035, B05-079, B05-177)
- **Explicit.** A few streetlamps light it at night, and shops have rooms above them with windows over the street. (B06-173, B06-170)
- **Explicit.** Past Dervish and Banges the street becomes a winding lane: cottages thin out, gardens grow, and it stops at a stile below the mountain. (B04-175)

## Three Broomsticks

A pub and inn on Hogsmeade’s High Street, the usual meeting place for visiting pupils and staff. It has a bar with a mirror behind it, tables packed into its corners, a fireplace and a window at the back, and at least one interior pillar. Madam Rosmerta sleeps upstairs, with a window over the street. The text calls it both tiny and large, so model a modest building whose bar is still big by village standards and is nearly always crowded.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Almost always packed: pupils, warlocks and others such as hags crowd it, and crossing the room means squeezing between tables. (B03-068, B04-075, B05-083, B05-177)
- **Inference.** It seats dozens at once: on one 1994 visit at least five separate parties sit in it, including a large group of Ravenclaws, in a room called packed. Because: Harry, Hermione, Ron’s group, Ernie and Hannah, Cho’s group and Hagrid with Moody are all placed there together. (B04-075)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** On the High Street, close to Honeydukes, and on the way to the slope that climbs to the Shrieking Shack. (B04-074, B06-056, B03-094)
- **Explicit.** Within minutes of Madam Puddifoot’s side road, and a minute from the corner of the Hog’s Head street. (B05-177, B06-170)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** The bar is long enough for a private word at the end away from Rosmerta, and a mirror behind it shows the shadowy corner tables. (B04-137)
- **Explicit.** A window and a fireplace stand towards the rear of the room, with small tables nearby. (B03-068)
- **Explicit.** An interior pillar stands among the tables. (B06-057)
- **Reported.** A ladies’ bathroom opens somewhere off the pub; its position is not given. (B06-057)
- **Reported.** Rosmerta’s bedroom is upstairs, with curtains and a view of the High Street. (B06-173)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The front door opens straight on to the street. (B03-068, B06-174)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Rosmerta keeps two brooms behind the bar; Summoned in 1997, they burst out through the front door. (B06-174)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** December 1993: a large Christmas tree stands beside the fireplace, and Rosmerta says Dementors have searched the pub twice. (B03-068)
- **Explicit.** May 1998: a dozen hooded Death Eaters rush out of its door when the alarm sounds; it is lit while the rest of the street is dark. (B07-035, B07-036)

### Conflicts

## Hog’s Head

A small, dirty inn at the top of a side street off Hogsmeade’s main road, much quieter than the Three Broomsticks. Downstairs is a single small bar room with grimy bay windows, rough tables, a stone floor under years of dirt, a counter and a back room. A doorway behind the counter leads to a rickety wooden stair and the upper floor, with rooms for guests and Aberforth Dumbledore’s sitting room, where in 1998 the portrait of Ariana hides a tunnel to Hogwarts. Small as it is, the bar seated 28 pupils and its regulars in 1995, and hundreds of evacuees poured through the building in May 1998.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The bar is one small, dingy room that smells of goats. (B05-081)
- **Explicit.** In 1995 that room seats 28 pupils on pulled-up chairs, 25 of them arriving at once, while other customers stay. (B05-082)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: about 40 m² of bar, say 6.5 m square. Twenty-eight pupils, four other customers and the barman need at least 1.2 m² each, seated, with tables. Because: The head count comes from the 1995 scene; the area per person is an assumption. It stops a room called small being modelled too small for that crowd. (B05-081, B05-082)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** At the top of a side street, reached past Zonko’s and the post office; Hermione notes it is not on the main road. (B05-079, B05-083)
- **Explicit.** A minute’s walk from the Three Broomsticks, round a corner; its sign can be glimpsed down the side street from the main road. (B06-170, B05-160)
- **Explicit.** A grimy upstairs window looks down on the side street. (B07-038, B07-039)
- **Reported.** Hagrid names it as one of the village pubs, where he met a hooded stranger in 1992. (B01-095, B01-119)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** A small inn. Its battered sign hangs from a rusty bracket above the door, showing a severed boar’s head bleeding on to white cloth; it creaks in the wind. (B05-079, B05-080, B06-170)
- **Explicit.** Bay windows so crusted with grime that little daylight enters; a table stands in at least one of them. (B05-081, B05-082)
- **Explicit.** The floor is stone under so much old dirt it looks like packed earth; in 1998 it has sawdust on it. (B05-081, B07-038)
- **Explicit.** A second doorway behind the counter leads to a rickety wooden stair that comes out directly in an upstairs sitting room. (B07-038)
- **Reported.** It has guest rooms: sixteen years before 1996 Trelawney stayed upstairs over the bar, and Snape, caught outside, claimed to have climbed the wrong stairs. (B05-296, B06-171)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** A bolted street door on the narrow side street leads directly into the bar. (B07-038)
- **Reported.** Flitwick confirms that pupils are allowed in, by Hermione’s account. (B05-083)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Rough wooden tables lit by candle stubs, and a fireplace with a shadowy corner beside it. One flickering candle is all that lights it in 1998. (B05-081, B05-082, B07-038)
- **Explicit.** A counter with dusty bottles stored beneath it, an old wooden till with a self-opening drawer, and a back room behind. (B05-082)
- **Explicit.** That sitting room has a threadbare carpet, a small fireplace, a table and a wobbly chair. Ariana’s large portrait hangs over the mantelpiece as the only picture, with a small rectangular mirror below it. (B07-039)

### How it changes

## Honeydukes

A sweetshop on Hogsmeade’s High Street, with a stock cellar below that hides the village end of the secret passage from the one-eyed witch on Hogwarts’ third floor. The shop holds a crush of pupils among walls of shelves and barrels, with Unusual Tastes in its furthest corner. A door behind the counter leads down wooden stairs to the crate-filled cellar. The owners are said to live above.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Packed on visit days: the crush of pupils is dense enough for Harry to go unnoticed, and he has to squeeze through it. (B03-065, B03-066, B06-056)
- **Explicit.** Shelf after shelf of sweets, including hundreds of kinds of chocolate set out in rows. (B03-066)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** On the High Street, near enough to the Three Broomsticks that people leaving the pub walk past its wall. (B04-074, B06-056)

- **Reported.** Ron says the owners live over the shop. (B03-066)

### Getting there

## Shrieking Shack

A crumbling house of at least two storeys on a hill just above Hogsmeade, reputedly the most haunted building in Britain and in fact built for Remus Lupin’s monthly transformations. Its windows are boarded, its garden overgrown and fenced, and every outside entrance is said to be sealed. The only known way in is the tunnel from the Whomping Willow, which comes up through a small opening into a wrecked ground-floor room. From there a door leads to a hall, a crumbling staircase and a dark landing, where one door opens on a bedroom with a grand four-poster. It seems an ordinary-sized house. In May 1998 one ground-floor window is unboarded and looks towards the castle.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** At least two storeys: a ground-floor room and hall, a staircase, a landing with several doors, and an upper room. (B03-120, B03-121)
- **Inference.** An ordinary house, not a mansion: Harry gets across the upstairs bedroom to the door in three strides. Because: The strides start from his position at the time, so they show a modest room rather than an exact width. (B03-131)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Stands slightly higher than the other houses, on a hill reached by a slope past the Three Broomsticks. (B03-094)
- **Explicit.** A second path climbs from the opposite side of the hill, muddy and puddled. (B03-094)
- **Explicit.** It looks out towards Hogwarts: in May 1998 an unboarded window shows distant flashes where the castle stands. (B07-098)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Outside it is a crumbling house with boarded windows, a dank overgrown garden and a fence pupils can lean on. (B03-094)
- **Explicit.** The ground-floor room has peeling wallpaper, a stained floor, boarded windows and smashed furniture, including a chair with chunks torn out and one leg gone. (B03-120)
- **Explicit.** An open door to the right of the arrival point leads to a shadowy hall and a crumbling staircase, thick with dust. (B03-120, B03-121)
- **Explicit.** On the dark landing only one door stood open in 1994. Do not infer the number of closed rooms. (B03-121)

## Whomping Willow

A violent, thick-trunked willow standing by itself near the centre of the Hogwarts grounds, below the front doors and in view from the greenhouses and Gryffindor Tower. It was planted the year Remus Lupin came to school, over the mouth of the tunnel to the Shrieking Shack, to keep people away. Its boughs, some as thick as a python or a battering ram, strike anything within reach, and its trunk can bend almost double. A large gap among the roots at the foot of the trunk leads underground. A single knot low on the trunk freezes the whole tree when touched, whether by a paw, a long stick or a levitated twig. Its height is never stated.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Branches run as thick as a python and as a battering ram on a trunk described as thick. (B02-007, B03-118)
- **Explicit.** Its blows dent and cave in a car and smash a broomstick into a dozen splinters. (B02-007, B03-054)
- **Explicit.** The trunk can bend almost double, and the roots creak as it strains after a target. (B02-007)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: a danger radius of about 8 m. Its boughs reach every part of a car, and the pupils need seconds to run to the trunk once it stops. Because: A two-second dash at an assumed 4–5 m/s is about 8–10 m. The text states no reach or height. (B02-007, B03-118)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It is lower than the entrance: from near it a grassy slope climbs up to the front steps. (B02-009)
- **Explicit.** Flying out from the castle wall the order is greenhouses, vegetable patch, lawns, then the tree. (B02-006)
- **Explicit.** It can be seen in the distance from outside the greenhouses, and from a boys’ dormitory window in Gryffindor Tower. (B02-018, B03-104)

## Willow tunnel

A low, narrow earth tunnel from the roots of the Whomping Willow, in the middle of the Hogwarts grounds, to a ground-floor room of the Shrieking Shack above Hogsmeade. It was built, with the Shack, so that Remus Lupin could be taken out of the castle for his transformations. It runs under the school boundary for a long way: to Harry in 1994 it seemed no shorter than the Honeydukes tunnel, itself a walk of over an hour. The ceiling is so low that a thirteen-year-old goes bent almost double, and by 1998 the trio have to crawl. No length is given.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** To Harry it seems no shorter than the Honeydukes tunnel. That is an impression formed while running, not a measure. (B03-119)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: 1 km. Half the Honeydukes minimum, allowing for how long a tunnel feels when run at a crouch. Because: The Honeydukes estimate is at least 2 km; halving it is a deliberately cautious assumption. The tunnel must span from mid-grounds to the village hill. (B03-119)
- **Explicit.** Very low: bent almost double in June 1994, and a crawl by May 1998 when the same three people are grown. (B03-119, B07-097)
- **Inference.** The ceiling is roughly waist to chest height on a teenager. Because: A thirteen-year-old walking bent almost double, and crawling at seventeen, cannot have much more headroom than that. (B03-119, B07-097)
- **Explicit.** Narrow: a group goes single file, and three chained people turn sideways at the Shack end. (B03-133)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Runs from the gap in the Willow’s roots to a ground-floor room of the Shrieking Shack. (B03-119, B07-097)
- **Inference.** It must pass under the edge of the grounds and on to the hill above Hogsmeade. Because: The Willow is inside the grounds and the Shack is beyond the boundary wall, so the tunnel crosses that line. (B03-119, B03-124, B06-175)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Earth-sided, with rock jutting from the ceiling in places; Harry cracks his head on one piece. (B03-119, B03-133)
- **Explicit.** Near the Shack the floor rises, twists, and ends at a small opening into the room. (B03-119, B07-097)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** At the Willow end the entrant crawls in headfirst and slides down an earth slope to the tunnel floor; leaving means climbing up through the hole. (B03-119, B03-133)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** No lighting and no fittings are mentioned; users rely on wands. (B03-119, B07-097)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** Reported history: built with the Shack for Lupin’s use, and the scene of Snape’s near-disaster as a pupil. (B03-124, B03-125, B07-109)
- **Reported.** It is drawn on the Marauder’s Map but runs off the sheet’s edge. Snape reads Lupin’s dot along it and out of sight. (B03-119, B03-129)
- **Explicit.** May 1998: no barrier stops the trio, but a crate sits against the Shack opening. They crawl back the same way. (B07-097, B07-101)

## One-eyed witch passage

A secret passage from Hogwarts to the cellar of Honeydukes in Hogsmeade, entered through the hump of a stone one-eyed witch about halfway along a third-floor corridor. A stone chute slides a long way down through the castle to cold earth; then a very low, narrow, twisting tunnel runs on for what feels like an hour, begins to rise, and after ten more minutes reaches worn stone steps. Two hundred steps and more climb to a trapdoor in the cellar floor. The whole route falls from the third floor to well below the village and climbs back, over a length of kilometres rather than metres. The Weasley twins used it often; it went unguarded in 1994 and was sealed by 1997.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** A stone chute slides a considerable way down, ending on cold, damp earth. (B03-063)
- **Inference.** The chute must drop at least three storeys inside the castle, since it starts on the third floor and ends on earth. Because: Bare earth cannot lie above ground level, so the chute clears the second, first and ground floors, inside walls or a hidden shaft. (B03-059, B03-063)
- **Explicit.** The earth passage is very narrow and low, unlit, with an uneven floor. (B03-063, B03-064)
- **Reported.** Walking fast, Harry goes for what feels like an hour before the floor begins to rise. That is his impression, not a timed figure. (B03-064)
- **Explicit.** Ten minutes after the rise begins he reaches the foot of the stairs. (B03-064)
- **Explicit.** Worn stone steps then climb out of sight: he passes two hundred before losing count, and his head hits the trapdoor. (B03-064)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: about 2 km of passage, and about 3.5 km if the hour is taken at face value. Because: Seventy minutes at an assumed 3 km/h in a low tunnel is 3.5 km. Halving the felt hour gives 40 minutes, or about 2 km. (B03-064)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: 36 m of stair rise, at 0.18 m a step, before counting any further steps. That is about the height of the castle from ground to seventh floor. Because: Two hundred steps is the stated minimum; the riser height and the assumed 4–5 m a storey are not from the text. (B03-064)
- **Inference.** Tunnel and road are comparable journeys: Harry sprinting back from Honeydukes emerges just as Snape arrives, sent by Malfoy who ran from the Shack. Because: Malfoy ran by road to the dungeons and Snape then climbed to the third floor in about the time Harry took, so the tunnel is not many times longer than the road. (B03-173, B03-096)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The statue stands about halfway along a third-floor corridor, on a route from the marble staircase up towards Gryffindor Tower. (B03-059, B03-098)
- **Explicit.** An empty classroom lies to its left, and doorways stand on either side nearby. (B03-059, B03-090)
- **Inference.** The tunnel at the stair foot lies far below the village street, and far below the castle’s ground floor too. Because: At least two hundred steps and a ten-minute rise lead up to a cellar in the village, and the village is downhill of the castle. (B03-064, B03-065, B04-135)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** A stone one-eyed witch with a hump, standing clear enough of the wall for boys to hide behind. (B03-059, B03-063, B03-090)
- **Explicit.** It is a sloping chute rather than a sheer drop: Harry climbs back up it, hands slipping on its sides. (B03-096)
- **Explicit.** It twists and turns like an animal burrow. (B03-064)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Tapped with a wand and told Dissendium, the hump opens just wide enough for a fairly thin person, who goes in headfirst. (B03-063)
- **Explicit.** It also works from within: a tap on the inside of the hump opens it, and it closes after the user climbs out. (B03-096)
- **Explicit.** The far end is a trapdoor in the cellar floor of Honeydukes. (B03-064, B03-065, B03-062)
- **Explicit.** It works in both directions, and pupils can run the whole way; the return felt almost instant to a distracted Harry. (B03-069, B03-096)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** A shadowy corner at its foot is deep enough to leave a Cloak in. (B03-096)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** The twins say they have used it often and that Filch does not know it. In early 1994 it stays unboarded and unguarded. (B03-062, B03-090)
- **Reported.** Sealed by the start of 1997–98, with curses and guards, as part of the seven old passages. (B07-045)

### Open questions

- Where the corridor with this statue lies relative to the forbidden corridor of 1991–92.
- How the chute is hidden as it passes the lower floors.

## Hog’s Head passage

A lamplit tunnel between the upstairs sitting room of the Hog’s Head and the Room of Requirement, created by the Room in spring 1998 and absent from the Marauder’s Map. Its village end is the portrait of Ariana above the fireplace, which swings out as a door, so users climb on to the mantelpiece. Stone steps lie just inside, then a worn earth floor where people walk upright. After a long stretch the passage climbs steeply, turns a corner and ends at a short flight of steps and a small matching door into the refuge. No length is given. Hundreds of pupils were evacuated through it on the night of the battle, before Mrs Longbottom sealed it.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** People walk upright and talk freely, and Neville walks backwards to face the others, so it is roomier than the older tunnels. Voices echo along it. Because: No width or height is given, but nobody stoops or crawls, in contrast with the Willow and Honeydukes tunnels. (B07-044, B07-045)
- **Explicit.** Seen in the portrait it is a long tunnel: Ariana’s figure shrinks into the dark and comes back as a tiny white dot. (B07-042)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: 400 m. About 1,100 words of talk pass as they walk, some seven minutes of speech, at an assumed 4 km/h. Because: Word count of the walking conversation at 150 words a minute. It sets only a floor; the span from village to castle should be longer. (B07-044)
- **Explicit.** The far stretch slopes upward and then climbs so steeply that Neville pants while talking. (B07-044)
- **Reported.** It carried hundreds of pupils on the night of the evacuation, and Aberforth complains of them thundering through his pub. (B07-059, B07-069)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** One end is in the upstairs sitting room of the Hog’s Head, behind Ariana’s portrait over the fireplace. (B07-039, B07-042)
- **Explicit.** It ends round a corner at a short flight of steps and a small door like the portrait, opening into the Room of Requirement’s refuge. (B07-044)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Smooth stone steps lie just inside, then a worn, smooth earth floor, with brass lamps hanging on the walls. (B07-044)
- **Inference.** The Room end is a hole in the wall, apparently above floor level. Because: People climb through it, and Percy overbalances and falls on arriving. (B07-048, B07-064)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The whole picture swings out on the wall as a small door, and users climb on to the mantelpiece to get through. (B07-042, B07-044)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** Reported origin: the Room made it once Neville had hidden there some thirty-six hours and wanted food, nearly two weeks before May 1998. (B07-050)
- **Reported.** Mrs Longbottom says she was the last through and sealed it during the battle, after Aberforth left. How is not stated. (B07-078)

### Conflicts

- It looks as if it has been there for years, yet Neville says the Room opened it only about two weeks earlier. (B07-044, B07-050)
- Ron recalls seven passages, and this one is not on the Map. It is an eighth route, not one of the seven. (B07-044, B07-045)
- The Room’s door is on the seventh floor, but that night the refuge’s stair climbs a long way up to a fifth-floor corridor, so the tunnel’s end cannot be fixed to one floor. (B06-125, B07-051)

### Open questions

- How far it really runs. Crossing from the village to the castle should take far longer than the conversation narrated, as the Honeydukes tunnel does. It may not obey ordinary geometry.

## Secret passages out of the castle

The secret passages out of Hogwarts as a set, not one place. The Marauder’s Map shows seven, and many of them run into Hogsmeade. Filch knew four, whose routes the text does not give. The other three are the one behind a large mirror on the fourth floor, roomy but caved in from the winter of 1992–93; the Willow tunnel; and the one-eyed witch passage to Honeydukes. Those with known entrances start at a disguised opening: a statue, a mirror or a tree’s roots. Gregory the Smarmy’s statue and an unnamed statue of 1998 also hide passages, which may or may not be among the seven. The set was watched from 1996 and sealed, with guards at the far ends, in 1997–98. The Hog’s Head passage of 1998 is an eighth route.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** Seven passages in all are drawn on the Marauder’s Map. (B03-061, B03-062, B07-045)
- **Explicit.** Many of the seven run into Hogsmeade; the text does not say that all of them do. (B03-062)
- **Reported.** Filch is said to know four of them; the twins think they alone know the other three. (B03-062)
- **Reported.** The mirror passage stood behind a large fourth-floor mirror, and Sirius remembers it as roomy enough to practise jinxes in. (B05-097)

### Where it is

- **Reported.** The twins believe a passage out of school lies at the statue of Gregory the Smarmy, found in their first week. (B01-044)
- **Explicit.** In May 1998 a statue that has left its plinth reveals a concealed hole into a secret passage, guarded by Fred and others. (B07-076)

### Shape and structure

- **Inference.** They differ in form and in where they start: a statue’s hump, the back of a big mirror and a tree’s roots are all confirmed entrances. Because: Three of the seven have named entrances, each hidden differently, so no standard door or tunnel type should be assumed. (B03-062, B05-097)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The Hog’s Head passage is a separate, later route, not among the seven. (B07-045, B07-044)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Named instances: the one-eyed witch passage to Honeydukes, the passage under the Whomping Willow, and the fourth-floor mirror passage. (B03-062)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** The mirror passage is reported completely blocked by a cave-in since the winter of 1992–93. (B03-062, B05-097)
- **Explicit.** 1994: Filch boards up cracks and mouseholes along the corridors, but the one-eyed witch stays open. (B03-088, B03-090)
- **Reported.** 1996: Umbridge says Filch is watching every passage in and out. June 1997: McGonagall says all are covered. (B05-213, B06-193)
- **Reported.** 1997–98: Aberforth says both ends are watched; Neville says all seven were sealed before term, cursed at their mouths, with Dementors and Death Eaters posted at their far ends. (B07-041, B07-045)
- **Reported.** May 1998: groups are posted to defend the passage entrances during the battle. (B07-068, B07-076)

### Conflicts

- Hermione assumes Filch knows every passage; the twins say he knows four of seven. (B03-044, B03-062)
- All seven are said to be sealed in 1997–98, yet the Willow tunnel is open and unbarred in May 1998. (B07-045, B07-097)

### Open questions

- The routes and castle ends of the four passages Filch knew, and whether the two statue passages are among them.
