# Castle exterior, terrain and general protections

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/castle-and-terrain.md)

The castle, its cliff and the surrounding country, taken from the horizon inwards. Mountains surround the site and one overshadows Hogsmeade; caves up there have hidden Sirius, Hagrid and Grawp. Boundary walls enclose wide grounds of sloping lawns, with the lake below the castle and the Forest to one side. The castle stands on a cliff above part of the shore, a vast stone mass of towers and turrets, with at least eight storeys in its main body and tower storeys above the seventh floor. Astronomy is the tallest tower, with Ravenclaw and Gryffindor next, then West and North towers. Inside are courtyards, an east and a west wing, 142 staircases, and a pupil body of about 300 to 800 by estimate. Layers of ancient and dated protection cover the whole site, stopping Apparition at the boundary. No dimension of the castle or grounds appears in the text, so scale has to be built up from storeys, window counts, crowds and walking times.

- **Explicit.** The grounds fall away from the castle on the narrated sides, and every return is uphill. The lake lies below, and the greenhouses are behind. (B02-025, B04-056, B01-029)

## Castle exterior

Hogwarts Castle is a vast stone castle on a high cliff above the lake, with mountains around it, reading from any distance as a dense mass of towers and turrets. Five towers are named: Astronomy, the tallest; Ravenclaw and Gryffindor, which with it make the three highest; then West Tower and North Tower. The minimum stack is dungeons below ground, a ground floor, seven numbered floors, then tower storeys above the seventh floor, so the main body stands at least eight storeys high before any tower begins, all on top of the cliff. A seventh-floor frontage runs at least thirteen windows along from West Tower. An east wing reaching at least a fifth floor and a west wing are named. The front has stone steps and oak doors with the Astronomy Tower rising directly above them; the greenhouses lie behind. Walls are thick stone, windows mullioned, tower tops crenellated. The text gives no height for the cliff or any tower and no count of towers, so every height below is an estimate.

(B04-106, B05-110, B03-014, B03-111)
- **Inference.** Minimum stack to read on the exterior, bottom to top: cliff, at least one dungeon level, ground floor, first to seventh floors, then tower storeys. That is nine levels before any tower storey. Because: One dungeon level, the ground floor and seven numbered floors make nine. The dungeons being eight floors below the seventh puts one level under the ground floor (B05-110). Whether that level shows on the cliff side is not stated. (B04-106, B05-110, B01-006, B03-111)

Because: A search of the full text finds no mention of a sixth floor, while six floors down from the seventh (B04-106) and eight floors from the seventh to the dungeons (B05-110) both require one. (B04-106, B05-110)

(B03-111, B03-016, B03-155, B01-053, B02-016)

Not canon; the text gives no storey count for any tower. Because: Gryffindor needs seven year rooms each for boys and girls, 14 in all (assumes one room per year and sex, B02-016). Open-air courtyards in the castle where pupils spend breaks. The books never name one, count them or give a plan, so these scenes may be one yard or several. Between them the scenes show sheltered corners, a dripping balcony overhead, a stone step, a pillar, a side door and a corridor opening out from the staff-room area, with routes on down to the dungeons. A crowd from several year groups can stand there in separate huddles, so do not make it small.

## Castle interiors in general

School-wide conditions inside the castle, not one room, and the place to size everything else from. The interior is stone, lit by wall torches and candles, heated by fires and draughty in winter. It is very hard to learn: 142 staircases, trick doors and a layout that seems to move. An east wing and a west wing are named. Over a hundred house-elves clean at night. The pupil roll is never stated and the clues disagree. Class and dormitory clues (five beds, twenty cauldrons) point to about 280 pupils; crowd clues (a hundred coaches, a hundred-plus exam desks, two hundred in green at a match) point to 350 to 800. Plan on about 300 to 800, and size whole-school rooms for the top of that range. Everything seasonal or imposed, from Christmas trees to Umbridge's decrees, is listed under changes.

## Protections and security

The magical and practical security covering the whole school, not one room. Nobody can Apparate or Disapparate anywhere in the buildings or grounds, a rule that stops at the boundary, so people leave by the gates and vanish just outside. Enchanted gates, jinxed perimeter walls and ancient charms guard the site, and Muggles reportedly see only a ruin. Exceptions are real: house-elves Apparate freely, a phoenix can carry its owner out, Portkeys work in both directions, and the paired Vanishing Cabinets defeat everything in 1997. Most measures are reported by characters, not observed, and none has visible architecture. Dated layers change the picture: Dementors at every entrance in 1993 to 1994, Floo and owl surveillance in 1996, Secrecy Sensors and Aurors from 1996, and defensive spells and Mandrakes in the 1998 battle.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** The Apparition ban covers the whole site, buildings and grounds alike, not just the castle. (B04-042, B04-191, B06-004, B07-059)
- **Reported.** McGonagall reports an enchantment on every entrance to the castle and a guard on every secret way out, and Aberforth reports old passages watched at both ends. Plan for multiple entrances. (B06-193, B07-041)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The ban ends at the boundary: fleeing Death Eaters run for the gates in June 1997 and Snape Disapparates just beyond the school's limits. (B06-188)
- **Reported.** Perimeter protection is layered outward: Aurors are stationed in Hogsmeade in 1996, and in 1998 Harry treats the village as the place to test the defences. (B06-013, B07-034)

### Shape and structure

- **Reported.** Muggles reportedly see a decaying ruin with a sign warning of danger, according to Hogwarts: A History. (B04-002)
- **Reported.** Electricity, computers and radar reportedly fail near Hogwarts because of the density of magic. Do not give the school any Muggle technology. (B04-183)
- **Reported.** Snape says many ancient spells and charms guard the walls and grounds. Dumbledore calls the school a stronghold of ancient magic. Neither describes anything visible. (B05-166, B06-121)
- **Reported.** The site was reportedly chosen far from Muggle eyes when it was founded. (B02-053)

### Getting there

- **Reported.** Hogwarts: A History is cited for anti-stealth enchantments, with no Apparating or Disapparating inside. Snape repeats this flatly in 1994 and Hermione again in 1995. (B03-044, B03-158, B05-159)
- **Explicit.** House-elves are exempt: Kreacher and Dobby appear with a crack in the hospital wing and the common room, and Ron says elf magic works in and out of Hogwarts. (B06-117, B06-133, B07-010)
- **Explicit.** Portkeys work both ways: Dumbledore sends pupils out by kettle in 1995 and Harry in by statue head in 1996, and a doctored Cup takes Harry and Cedric out of the grounds in 1995. (B05-156, B05-297, B04-219, B04-221)
- **Explicit.** A phoenix can leave: Dumbledore vanishes from his office holding Fawkes's tail in 1996, and Umbridge insists he cannot have Disapparated. (B05-205)
- **Inference.** The perimeter walls and gates carry jinxes and Dumbledore's own enchantments. Harry's broom shudders crossing the wall in June 1997 as Dumbledore seems to lift them for entry at speed. Because: The shudder and muttering are observed; that Dumbledore is suspending his enchantments is Harry's interpretation. (B06-015, B06-176)
- **Reported.** Dumbledore barred Dementors from crossing the castle threshold in 1993. It was a rule rather than a barrier: Fudge brought one inside in June 1995. (B03-046, B04-236)
- **Reported.** Vanishing Cabinets defeat every ward in June 1997: Draco repairs the school's cabinet and Death Eaters step through from Borgin and Burkes. (B06-179)
- **Reported.** No check is complete: Secrecy Sensors miss a love potion repackaged as perfume or medicine, and enchanted coins carry messages past monitored post. (B06-080, B06-182)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** September 1993 to June 1994: Dementors at every entrance to the grounds. Lupin reports them resenting exclusion; they return to posts after searches and are withdrawn in June 1994. (B03-003, B03-012, B03-057, B03-137, B03-159)
- **Explicit.** February 1994: the front doors are taught to recognise Black's photograph and Filch boards up tiny cracks and mouseholes. (B03-088)
- **Reported.** 1996: fires are watched for Umbridge, with hers excepted, the Squad opens owl post, and Filch watches secret routes. Fires were also policed through a Floo Network contact. (B05-202, B05-213)
- **Reported.** From autumn 1996: new charms, counter-curses and a small Auror task force, with Dumbledore announcing stronger fortifications. No new masonry is described. (B06-001, B06-022)
- **Explicit.** From 1996 Filch screens arrivals and Hogsmeade trips with Secrecy Sensors, and the Ministry arranges a one-off Floo link for the January 1997 return. (B06-050, B06-053, B06-089)
- **Reported.** February 1997: Dumbledore lifts the Apparition ban for one hour, inside the Great Hall only, for lessons. Nobody can Apparate beyond its walls. (B06-104)
- **Reported.** By June 1997 the air is reportedly closed too: McGonagall says they knew nobody could fly in. Earlier flyers, such as the 1992 car, met no such barrier. (B06-193, B02-006)
- **Reported.** June 1997: Dumbledore says he has never left the school unprotected and adds cover the night he leaves. An unlocked front door that spring shows the routine could lapse. (B06-168, B06-136)
- **Explicit.** May 1998: Flitwick casts protective spells into the grounds from a broken classroom window, expecting to delay Voldemort, not stop him; Sprout's group carries Mandrakes to throw over the walls. (B07-062, B07-074)
- **Reported.** Before 1981: Hagrid reports Hogwarts as one of the few safe places during Voldemort's first rise. No specific measures are described. (B01-001)

### Conflicts

- Hermione says repeatedly that nobody can Apparate inside Hogwarts, yet house-elves do so in view and Ron explains it as a difference in elf magic. Treat her rule as applying to witches and wizards. (B03-044, B05-159, B06-117, B06-133, B07-010)

### Open questions

- The exact line of the Apparition boundary, and whether it follows the walls everywhere, including the Forest side.
- Whether any protection is visible, for example a shimmer on the wall tops. The text shows none.

## Grounds in general

The school grounds as a whole: lawns falling away from the castle on its narrated sides, with the lake, the Forbidden Forest, the Quidditch pitch, greenhouses and Hagrid's cabin set among them inside a boundary wall. Every route back to the castle climbs. The site is large: walking along the Forest edge far enough loses sight of both castle and lake, and Krum still thinks Durmstrang's grounds are bigger. Distances are rarely measured; the few figures are two hundred yards near Hagrid's cabin and a quarter of a mile from castle to the merpeople's square. Long views run from castle windows to the pitch and the cabin. The grounds change strongly with the seasons: frost in November, deep snow and a frozen lake by December, thaw and mud in February, hot cloudless June days.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** Krum says Durmstrang has grounds larger even than Hogwarts's, while its castle is smaller. (B04-126)
- **Explicit.** Walking along the Forest edge in November 1994, Hagrid's party goes far enough that the castle and the lake both pass out of sight. (B04-078)
- **Explicit.** The carriage from Beauxbatons parks two hundred yards from Hagrid's door, one of the few measured distances in the grounds. (B04-052)
- **Explicit.** From the merpeople's statue in the lake, Harry reckons his trunk in the castle to be a quarter of a mile away. (B04-168)
- **Estimate.** Treat the grounds as at least about a kilometre across. Not canon; no overall dimension exists. Because: A quarter-mile to the merpeople's statue is 400 m, the cabin and carriage add 200 yards near the Forest, and from the pitch the doors are distant. These spans do not lie end to end, but together with a route that loses the castle from view they rule out a compact site. (B04-168, B04-052, B04-078, B03-105)
- **Explicit.** Snow reaches several feet by mid-December 1991 and two feet in late 1995. (B01-069, B05-136)
- **Explicit.** Growth runs large: pumpkins reach garden-shed size in October 1992 and greenhouse flowers grow as big as cabbages in spring 1993. (B02-035, B02-116)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The ground falls from the castle: lawns slope down to the pitch, the cabin and the lake, and pupils return uphill from the Forest and greenhouses. (B02-025, B04-056, B05-143, B01-055)
- **Explicit.** Castle windows overlook the pitch, Hagrid's usual walks and his cabin frontage far off in the distance. (B01-061, B02-116, B06-090)
- **Explicit.** From the sloping lawns the Forest stands in the distance, and the flying lawn lies on the opposite side of the grounds from it. (B01-040)
- **Explicit.** Forest trees can be heard creaking from the Gryffindor dormitory in a storm, and Fawkes's lament carries across the grounds into the castle. (B03-049, B06-192)
- **Explicit.** There are lonely, secluded corners where pupils can go unobserved. None is named or placed. (B07-002)
- **Reported.** A hen-coop exists somewhere in the grounds, reported by Hagrid, with its position unstated. (B02-080)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Mostly grass lawn with flowerbeds. June lawns ripple satin-green; the lake reflects a cloudless sky. (B02-035, B05-252)

### Getting there

- **Reported.** Lupin says he and his school friends roamed the grounds and village at night, the knowledge behind the Marauder's Map. (B03-126)
- **Explicit.** Pupils cross the grounds freely between castle, pitch and cabin by day. At dusk or after dark the same routes feel empty. (B06-045)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Autumn: damp, days of heavy rain, a raised lake and flowerbeds turned to mud (1992). Hard frosts and first snow on the peaks by November. (B02-035, B01-061, B05-121, B03-058)
- **Explicit.** Winter: the lake freezes solid by December and pupils skate on it. Deep snow carries trodden channels; pupils toboggan, and snowballs are bewitched up at Gryffindor Tower's windows. (B01-069, B05-136, B04-118, B05-143, B04-100)
- **Explicit.** Late winter: endless rain, then thaw, cold rain and slippery mud in February; harsh drying winds in March 1995. (B01-085, B06-102, B05-174, B04-172)
- **Explicit.** Spring and summer: warm breezy Easter, then hot cloudless June days with pupils on the lawns and the giant squid at the lake's surface. (B05-223, B03-107, B03-160, B05-252)
- **Explicit.** Blizzards and sleet recur: grey snow darkens the castle in December 1992, sleet drives along the lane in October 1996. (B02-078, B06-055)
- **Explicit.** Temporary lessons and exams: Hagrid's bonfire of salamanders, January 1994, and Lupin's obstacle course of pool, potholes and marsh, June 1994. Neither is permanent topography. (B03-076, B03-109)
- **Inference.** June 1997: Fawkes's lament stops and Harry believes the phoenix has left for good. Because: The silence is observed; the permanent departure is Harry's conviction. (B06-201)

### Open questions

- Overall size and outline of the grounds: no dimension is given.
- How the boundary wall runs around the Forest and lake.

## Surrounding mountains and landscape

Mountains surround the school and rise beyond Hogsmeade, which lies in the shadow of one of them. None is named, measured or placed by bearing. They are seen from castle windows, the High Street and the railway, and one stands between the departing train and the castle. They turn icy grey and carry snow by November. The castle's own site is called a high mountain at first sight. The region lies far to the north, with moors and forest on the rail approach.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** At first sight the castle's own site is called a high mountain, and later a cliff, so the school stands in high country, not on a hill in a valley. Because: Both words are used of the same site across the lake. (B01-006, B02-005)
- **Inference.** A snow-capped mountain stands beside the railway on 1 September 1992, so peaks near the route hold snow in early autumn. Because: The date and the snow come from the same observation; the height is deduced, and that peak's distance from the school is unknown. (B02-004)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The narration speaks of the mountains around the school, and Harry notices their absence as soon as a Portkey takes him away. (B01-061, B05-121, B04-220)
- **Explicit.** One mountain overshadows Hogsmeade. A lane from the High Street thins out past cottages to a stile at its foot. (B04-175, B04-176)
- **Explicit.** Dark mountains stand beyond the village, seen from the High Street, which curves off towards the school. (B07-035)
- **Explicit.** A mountain stands between the railway and the castle: the school drops from view behind it soon after the train leaves. (B03-164)
- **Explicit.** Mountains and forest are visible from the train at dusk near arrival. (B01-004)
- **Explicit.** Ravenclaw's common-room windows look out on the mountains, and at dawn the sun rises over mountains seen from the Headmaster's office. (B07-054, B05-288)
- **Explicit.** Beyond Hogsmeade, Thestral riders pass over mountains, gullies, villages and winding hillside roads heading for London. (B05-287)
- **Reported.** The region lies far to the north: dawn has broken at Shell Cottage while Hogsmeade's street is still dark, and a man from Peebles reports the flying car on its way north. (B07-031, B02-168)

### Getting there

- **Reported.** Aberforth says that once out of Hogsmeade on foot and up into the mountains, Disapparating works again. He adds that Hagrid is hiding in a cave up there. (B07-040)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** They turn icy grey by November 1991 and are snowcapped by November 1995. (B01-061, B05-121)

### Open questions

- Names, heights and bearings of the mountains: the text gives none, and never names the country the school is in.

## Mountain caves and paths

Caves in the mountains beyond Hogsmeade, used as hiding places. These are separate reports and must not be merged into one cave. Only Sirius's refuge of 1995 is described: reached by a stile, a boulder-strewn slope and nearly half an hour's climb, then a narrow crack in the rock opening into a cool, dim cave. Hagrid's 1996 hiding place, Grawp's big cave from 1996 and the cave Hagrid shares with him in 1998 are known only from speech.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Sirius's cave lies nearly half an hour's climb up a steep, winding, stony path from the mountain's foot. (B04-176)
- **Explicit.** Inside there is room to sit and pace and to keep a tethered Hippogriff at the far end. (B04-177)
- **Reported.** Grawp's cave is called large by Hagrid, big enough to house a giant. (B06-023)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Reached from Hogsmeade's High Street by a winding lane to a stile, then scrubby rising ground and boulders at the mountain's foot. (B04-175, B04-176)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Sirius's cave has a rock floor, cool dim air and a large rock used as a tether post. The way in is a narrow fissure that people must squeeze through. (B04-177)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The way back runs down the path and over the boulders to the stile, through Hogsmeade and uphill to the castle. (B04-178)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** In 1995 the floor holds old newspapers and food scraps, with Buckbeak roped at the end. (B04-177)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** Later cave reports: Hagrid hides in one in 1996, Grawp is rehoused in one from September 1996, and Hagrid says he heard Voldemort's magnified voice in his cave in 1998. (B05-305, B06-023, B07-040, B07-067)

### Conflicts

- The cave entrance is a fissure that people squeeze through, yet a Hippogriff is tethered inside. The text does not explain how Buckbeak got in. (B04-177)

### Open questions

- Whether any of the later caves is the same as Sirius's.
