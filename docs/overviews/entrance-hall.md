# Entrance Hall and its connections

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/entrance-hall.md)

Walking in from the stone front steps, the oak front doors open straight into the Entrance Hall, a vast torch-lit stone room on the ground floor. It could take the whole of a four-bedroom suburban house, and its ceiling is too high to see from the floor. The marble staircase stands directly ahead and climbs to the first floor. On the right are the Great Hall’s double doors, and to the right of the staircase a door leads down to the kitchens and the Hufflepuff basement. On the staircase’s other side a door leads down to the dungeons and Slytherin, and a corridor on that side runs to classroom eleven. Small rooms open off the hall: the chamber where first-years wait before the Sorting, another empty chamber, and a broom cupboard. Giant house-point hourglasses stand in wall niches in one corner.

## Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: about 250 m² of open floor in the hall, plus the staircase and side rooms. Because: From the Entrance Hall overview: about 400 people at an assumed 2.5 per m² in a band round a clear middle, 16 m by 16 m = 256 m². (B05-232, B05-233)

## Where it is

- **Inference.** The Entrance Hall is the castle’s main junction: the grounds, upper floors, Great Hall, kitchens and dungeons all connect here. Because: The front doors, marble staircase, Great Hall doors, kitchen door and dungeon steps all open off this one room, and ordinary routes between them cross it. (B01-010, B04-007, B04-062, B05-302, B02-011)
- **Inference.** Doors on the Great Hall side lead to the rooms under the Great Hall; those on the other side lead to the dungeons. Because: The kitchen door is to the right of the stairs, on the Great Hall’s side, and the kitchens lie under the Great Hall. The door to Slytherin is on the other side of the stairs. (B04-062, B04-106, B04-108, B05-302)

## Entrance Hall

The Entrance Hall is the castle’s main junction, on the ground floor directly behind the oak front doors at the top of the stone front steps. It is a vast stone room with a flagged floor, wall torches and a ceiling lost above the torchlight, big enough to take a whole four-bedroom house. The marble staircase faces the front doors. Double doors on the right lead to the Great Hall, and a door to the right of the staircase leads down to the kitchens and the Hufflepuff basement. On the other side of the staircase a door leads down to the Slytherin dungeons, and a corridor runs to classroom eleven. Giant house-point hourglasses stand in wall niches in one corner. Most of the school has stood here at once in a ring round a clear middle, so allow at least 250 m² of open floor and 10 m to the ceiling, as modelling minimums rather than figures from the books.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The Dursleys’ entire four-bedroom house would fit inside it, on Harry’s first impression. (B01-010, B01-147)
- **Explicit.** Its ceiling is out of sight above the torchlight. (B01-010)
- **Explicit.** Peeves hovered twenty feet above arriving pupils in 1994; in 1996 two brooms rose fifteen feet inside it, level with him. (B04-006, B05-234)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 10 m from floor to ceiling. Because: Twenty feet (6 m) above pupils about 1.7 m tall puts Peeves near 8 m up, with the ceiling above that. A two-storey house with a pitched roof, assumed 8 to 9 m to the ridge, must also fit under it. A ceiling too high to see by torchlight suggests well over 10 m. (B04-006, B01-010, B01-147)
- **Explicit.** The 1994 Age Line ran ten feet out from the Goblet in every direction, a ring about 20 feet across at the centre of the hall, with people moving around it. (B04-049)
- **Explicit.** In 1996 apparently most of the school stood round the walls in a ring with a clear middle; an earlier packed ring overflowed on to the marble staircase. (B05-232, B05-233, B05-186, B05-187)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: about 250 m² of open floor, for example 16 m by 16 m, excluding the staircase. Because: Crowd: most of a school of 500 to 700 is about 400 people. At an assumed 2.5 people per m², a band 3 m deep around a 10 m clear middle holds 390 (156 m² × 2.5), which needs 16 m by 16 m = 256 m². Goblet: a 6 m ring plus an assumed 5 m each side for passing pupils also gives 16 m. House: a two-storey, four-bedroom house, assumed 8 m by 10 m in plan, fits inside. (B05-232, B05-233, B04-049, B01-010, B01-147)
- **Explicit.** Described as vast and cavernous. (B02-011, B03-009, B04-006, B06-017)
- **Explicit.** In 1996 Harry, coming down into the hall, judged the diners beyond the Great Hall doors to be about twenty feet away. (B05-278)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** On the ground floor, directly behind the oak front doors at the top of the stone front steps. (B03-009, B05-015, B06-017)
- **Explicit.** Seen from the front doors, the marble staircase stands straight ahead and the Great Hall’s double doors are on the right. (B01-010, B01-011, B04-007, B05-015)
- **Inference.** Seen from the front doors, the door to the kitchens and Hufflepuff basement is on the Great Hall side of the staircase, and the door down to Slytherin is opposite. Because: Cedric’s door is to the right of the staircase and is the one reached by turning left when coming down it, so it is on the right seen from the front doors. The Slytherin door is on the right when coming down, so on the left seen from the front doors. This puts the kitchen steps on the same side as the Great Hall, which fits the kitchens lying under it. (B04-062, B04-106, B04-184, B05-115, B05-302)
- **Explicit.** A corridor to classroom eleven leaves the hall on the side opposite the Great Hall. (B05-191)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Stone walls lit by flaming torches and a stone-flagged floor. Footsteps and shouts echo, and it feels cool or gloomy coming in from sunny grounds. (B01-010, B02-011, B04-029, B05-015, B01-121, B02-031)
- **Explicit.** A balcony overlooks the hall, and at least one window lies across the hall from the stairs. (B07-092)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The oak front doors have a sliding lock on the inside, and a centaur’s horse body has walked in through them. (B02-120, B05-188)
- **Explicit.** On Hogsmeade days Filch checks names at the front doors, from 1996 also with a Secrecy Sensor. (B03-035, B06-050, B06-053, B06-132)
- **Explicit.** Side chambers, a broom cupboard and at least one corridor door open directly off the hall. (B01-011, B02-088, B03-141, B07-070)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Giant house-point hourglasses stand in wall niches in one corner, holding rubies for Gryffindor, sapphires for Ravenclaw and emeralds for Slytherin. Gems fall as points are won and rise as they are lost. (B01-104, B05-208, B05-303)
- **Explicit.** A noticeboard, plus armour standing by the front door. (B02-075, B02-088)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Book 4: a large tournament notice stood at the stair foot in October 1994, then the Goblet of Fire on the Sorting stool at the centre of the floor. (B04-037, B04-049)
- **Explicit.** Damage: front doors blasted open and Gryffindor’s hourglass cursed, spilling rubies (June 1997); Slytherin’s hourglass shattered, doors burst by giant spiders, banisters broken, flagstones bloodied (May 1998). (B06-187, B07-092, B07-102)

### Open questions

- Plan shape and the position of each door along its wall. The text fixes only which side each is on, and gives no compass bearing.
- What the balcony connects to, and whether it is at first-floor level.
- Which wall holds the hourglasses. Slytherin’s is on the left when coming down the stairs, and Hufflepuff’s gem is never named.

## Marble staircase

The marble staircase is the castle’s main stair, rising from the Entrance Hall directly opposite the front doors. In most scenes it climbs from the ground floor to the first floor, where other stairs take over; from the seventh floor the way down is six floors and then this staircase. It has marble banisters and a balustrade and at least one landing, and it is the everyday route up for the whole school, carrying whole houses after feasts and hundreds of evacuees in 1998. No width, step count or plan shape is stated, so allow at least 4 m of width as a modelling minimum, not a figure from the books. It was badly damaged in the 1998 battle and no repair is described.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Carries large crowds: whole houses climb it after feasts, onlookers packed it in 1996, and hundreds of evacuees poured up it in 1998. (B03-013, B05-186, B07-069)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 4 m wide. Because: No width is given. Gryffindor and Ravenclaw, about half a school of 500 to 700, both live upstairs, so about 300 pupils climb it after a feast. At an assumed 0.5 people per metre of width per second, 300 people in 5 minutes need 2 m. Double that for traffic coming the other way, which also leaves room for Hagrid, at least three times normal breadth, who has run down it. (B03-013, B05-115, B05-186, B07-069, B04-012, B07-092)
- **Explicit.** A person at the foot can make out who is at the top: Harry saw Cedric and his friends up there from the bottom. (B04-084)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Stands across the Entrance Hall directly facing the front doors, and is the main way to the upper floors. (B01-010, B03-009)
- **Explicit.** Climbs from the ground floor to the first floor, where other stairs take over: from the seventh floor the route is six floors down, then this staircase. (B02-046, B03-111, B04-106, B05-268)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Marble steps and marble banisters with a balustrade; the banister ends can hold cakes set on them. (B02-088, B07-092, B07-132)
- **Explicit.** It has at least one landing: Filch passes a first landing on the way up, and a portrait of bewigged witches hangs on a landing. (B05-210, B06-185)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** A corridor reached by a concealed staircase and a tapestry joins a landing, from which the rest of the staircase drops to the Entrance Hall. (B06-186)
- **Explicit.** At the foot, coming down, the door on the left leads to the kitchens, the door on the right to Slytherin, and the front doors lie ahead. (B01-010, B04-106, B05-302)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Christmas 1994: everlasting icicles hung from the banisters. (B04-114)
- **Explicit.** May 1998: the battle left chunks missing from the staircase, parts of the banisters and balustrade gone, and rubble and blood on the steps. No repair is narrated. (B07-102, B07-132)

### Conflicts

- Most scenes end the staircase at the first floor, but Book 5 has Filch reach its first landing on the way up to Umbridge’s office, and in Book 6 the Carrows run down it above a landing with more of it below. That suggests a half-landing or a longer stair. (B02-046, B03-111, B04-106, B05-268, B05-210, B06-185, B06-186)

### Open questions

- Plan form: one straight flight, a divided stair, or flights with a half-landing. The text gives no shape or step count.

## Rooms off the Entrance Hall

A group, not one room: small spaces that open directly off the Entrance Hall. Known instances are the small empty chamber where first-years wait before the Sorting, an empty chamber used as a hiding place in 1994, and a broom cupboard of buckets and mops. The text does not say the two chambers are the same, and gives no wall for any of them.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The waiting chamber is small: the new first-years, all together, had to press in close. (B01-011)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** From that chamber the first-years cross back over the hall to reach the Great Hall’s doors. (B01-011)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** People hiding in the cupboard or the empty chamber can hear others crossing the hall; from the cupboard, even going down the front steps. (B03-113, B03-141)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** A broom cupboard opening off the hall holds buckets and mops, with room for two people and an upturned bucket as a seat. (B02-088, B03-141)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** In 1991 about twenty ghosts crossed the waiting chamber, in through the back wall and out through the opposite one. (B01-012)

### Open questions

- Which wall each room opens from, and whether the 1994 chamber is the 1991 one.
