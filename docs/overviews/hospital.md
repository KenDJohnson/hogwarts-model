## Hospital wing

The school's hospital on an upper floor: a ward with a distinct far end, also called a dormitory, off a corridor. Double doors open at the near end, and Madam Pomfrey's office opens off the far end. Beds with white linen and feather mattresses stand in lines with others opposite, so the ward has two sides and a way down the middle. High bed curtains and movable screens give privacy, and each bed has a cabinet or table and room for chairs. Windows with sills and curtains look outside, and the corridor beyond overlooks the Quidditch pitch and the lake. At least eight beds are implied, and six visitors at a bed is the matron's limit. The text never numbers the floor, and journeys disagree, though floors lie above and below it. The one counted journey, two floors down to the first-floor head of the marble staircase, gives the third floor. One Book 2 journey implies the first floor and one Book 6 journey the seventh or higher. Most journeys fit a floor above the second and below the seventh, and the third floor is the best-supported reading, but it is not settled.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** At least four beds stand in one line, and at least eight if both sides match; the text never counts them. Because: Colin's bed is three beds from Harry's (B02-069), and more beds stand opposite (B05-298). (B02-069, B05-298)
- **Inference.** It held at least four long-stay patients at once in 1993. Because: Colin, Justin, Penelope and Hermione were all petrified by spring 1993, and none is cured before the book's end. (B02-069, B02-082, B02-111)
- **Explicit.** The matron's limit is six visitors at one bed. (B06-110)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** The only journey that counts floors puts the ward on the third floor. Because: From the corridor outside the ward Harry goes two floors down to the top of the marble staircase, then along the first-floor corridor (B05-268). Two floors above the first floor is the third. (B05-268)
- **Explicit.** Floors lie above and below it: at the bell, which sounds just outside, pupils are heard in corridors over and under the ward. (B05-269)
- **Explicit.** The way up from the Entrance Hall is the marble staircase, portrait-lined corridors and then more flights of stairs. (B05-268)
- **Explicit.** It lies above the Great Hall, staff room, dungeons, Slughorn's room, McGonagall's first-floor study and the corridor of the December 1992 attack: people go up to it from all of them. (B01-036, B01-103, B01-142, B02-082, B02-134, B02-159, B04-050, B06-060, B06-111, B02-167)
- **Explicit.** A single flight of stairs is mentioned between the Headmaster's office and the ward. (B04-234)
- **Explicit.** From the West Tower top, with ten minutes allowed, the way down is a tight spiral, two more staircases and a new corridor. (B03-156)
- **Explicit.** A distant roar on the night Sirius left the seventh-floor office came from above the ward. (B03-157, B03-139)
- **Explicit.** Windows in the corridor outside show the Quidditch pitch and the lake. (B05-301)
- **Reported.** Match commentary from the pitch can be heard from a bed. (B06-215)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Only one ward is described, also called a dormitory, with beds near the doors and others at a distinct far end. (B03-138, B04-235, B06-110, B06-191)
- **Explicit.** The entrance is a pair of doors opening from a corridor. (B05-268, B06-110, B06-191)
- **Inference.** Beds stand on both sides with a way down the middle. Because: One bed faces others across the ward (B05-298), and people walk up the ward past beds to a far one (B03-138, B06-191). (B03-138, B05-298, B06-191)
- **Explicit.** Windows have sills and curtains, show the sky, and let moonlight fall across beds. (B01-140, B02-069, B03-138, B04-242, B05-298, B06-110)
- **Explicit.** Madam Pomfrey's office has its own door at the far end of the ward. (B03-138, B05-298, B06-116)
- **Inference.** She appears to sleep beyond that door. Because: She retires through it at bedtime (B06-116) and is fetched from that direction at night in her nightclothes (B02-069). (B02-069, B06-116)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The door can be locked by wand, and the matron can bar visitors altogether, as in spring 1993. (B02-117, B03-157)
- **Explicit.** House-elves arrive and vanish inside the ward with a crack. (B02-067, B06-117)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Beds have white linen sheets, pillows and feather mattresses, and each has a table or cabinet with room for a water jug, books and gifts. (B01-140, B02-066, B02-096, B04-235, B05-298, B06-110)
- **Explicit.** High curtains and movable screens can be drawn round a bed, and they block the view across the ward. (B02-066, B02-071, B02-096, B04-235)
- **Explicit.** Lamps light the ward; at bedtime they dim and the matron closes all the curtains with one wave of her wand. (B04-235, B06-115, B06-116)
- **Explicit.** Chairs are pulled up around beds, the matron conjures more when needed, and the ward keeps bedpans. (B03-050, B04-235, B06-110, B06-191)

### Conflicts

- Third floor against first floor. The one counted journey gives the third floor (B05-268). But in 1993, leaving the ward for Gryffindor, the boys climb to the floor above and find the corridor outside Myrtle's bathroom, narrated as the second floor (B02-097, B02-046). In 1997 three people walk along the corridor to the marble staircase with no stairs mentioned (B06-112), and in 1993 that staircase is the only climb named on the way from the pitch (B02-110). These suggest the first floor. (B05-268, B02-097, B02-046, B06-112, B02-110)
- Book 2 disagrees with itself: from Myrtle's bathroom the ward is up (B02-095), yet from the ward Myrtle's corridor is up (B02-097). McGonagall's first-floor study also has the ward above it (B02-159, B01-103), against a first-floor ward. (B02-095, B02-097, B02-159, B01-103)
- Seventh floor or higher. In 1997 Harry and Ron leave the ward going down to breakfast and turn into a seventh-floor corridor (B06-118), with no detour upwards stated. That sets the ward at or above the seventh floor, against a roar from a seventh-floor office coming from above it (B03-157, B03-139). (B06-118, B03-157, B03-139)
- Above the Defence office. In 1995 the matron is told to come down to it and later to go down to it (NEW-inner-2). That office is stated to be on the third floor (B05-055), which would put the ward on the fourth floor or higher, not the third. (B04-246, B05-055, B05-268)
- One flight below the Headmaster's office (B04-234). That office's gargoyle is approached towards the second floor in Book 4 (B04-188) and stands on the seventh floor in Book 6 (B06-035), so this relation fixes no floor. West Tower routes are no firmer: a spiral and two staircases down from the top (B03-156) cannot be turned into a floor count. (B04-234, B04-188, B06-035, B03-156)
- Weight of the journeys, not a settled answer: most put the ward above the second floor and below the seventh, floors lie above and below it, and the only journey that counts floors gives the third floor. The third floor is the best-supported reading. (B05-268, B05-269, B02-095, B02-159, B06-060, B06-111, B03-156, B03-157, B04-234)

### Open questions

- Which floor to model: the third is the best-supported reading, not a settled one.
- The number of beds and the ward's length are never given.
