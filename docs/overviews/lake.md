# Lake, shore, underwater world and tomb

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/lake.md)

Walking down from the front doors, the lawns reach the lake at muddy banks and shallows that deepen from knee to waist height. The Durmstrang ship lay anchored here from October 1994 until the end of that school year. The shore can be walked right round. Going one way, a large beech stands on the water's edge, in direct view of a Gryffindor dormitory; across the water from it lie the funeral bank and the white marble tomb, by the bank the judges used in 1995. Hagrid's side and the site of the 1994 Dementor attack face each other across what is probably a few hundred yards of water. A steep path from the station meets the shore opposite the castle, whose cliff rises from the water. Out from the shallows the bottom drops from sight into a dim country of weed and mud with ten-foot visibility, and over twenty minutes' swim from the far bank lies the merpeople village, a quarter of a mile from the castle. No size is stated. Model at least half a mile on the long line and at least 100 feet of water in the deep.

## Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** Allow the lake at least half a mile on the line from far bank to castle, narrowing to a few hundred yards at crossings. Because: A swim of over twenty minutes ends a quarter of a mile from the castle. At least 0.5 m/s gives 600 m, plus 400 m. Night sightlines show a much narrower crossing elsewhere. Minimum only. (B04-165, B04-168, B03-152)
- **Inference.** Three laps of the shore fit into one morning, so the shoreline is a few miles at most. Because: At walking pace a long morning covers six to nine miles. (B04-083)
- **Estimate.** Allow at least 100 feet of water over the deep basin, which is deep enough for a ship to rise from it. Because: A masted ship came up whole at the middle, and the water stays dark until close to the surface on the way up. Assumed from real lakes. (B04-045, B04-169, B04-170)

## Where it is

- **Explicit.** From the top of the lawns the surface is open to view as far as its centre; from inside the castle only parts are seen. (B04-041, B03-014, B04-153, B05-301)
- **Explicit.** The far-side bank is within a ten-minute sprint of the library. (B04-255)

## How it changes

- **Explicit.** Dated installations: the ship (1994 to 1995), second-task stands and judges' table (February 1995), funeral chairs and tomb (June 1997). (B04-045, B04-158, B06-206, B06-210)

## The lake

The lake is the large dark body of water in the Hogwarts grounds, lying between the station path and the castle. The castle's cliff rises from its far shore, and the front lawns slope down to it on another side. It can be walked right round: three laps fit into one morning, which limits the shoreline to a few miles. The books give no width, length or depth. The clues suggest an elongated basin, a few hundred yards across where people watch each other over the water at night, at least half a mile on the line from the far bank to the castle, and deep enough in the middle to hide a masted ship. Ron takes it to lie south of the castle. No window is said to show all of it: a high landing shows a piece, classroom windows a wide sheet. The water is usually still and dark, freezes in hard winters, and holds a giant squid, merpeople and Grindylows.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Narration calls it great at first sight, and from classroom windows it reads as a wide grey expanse. (B01-006, B04-153)
- **Reported.** Before he has entered it, Harry pictures it as huge and extremely deep, with merpeople living on the bed. (B04-254)
- **Estimate.** Allow at least half a mile (about 800 m) on the line from the far bank to the castle. Because: The merpeople square is a quarter of a mile (400 m) from the castle, reached by a swim that felt like over twenty minutes from the far bank. Assuming only 0.5 m/s, twenty minutes is 600 m, so about 1,000 m if straight; rounded down because the swim was not straight. A minimum, not a survey. (B04-168, B04-165)
- **Inference.** Between Hagrid's side and the site of the Dementor attack the water is probably only a few hundred yards across. Because: By moonlight the watchers on one bank could see Snape conjure stretchers and load bodies on the other, and a Dementor lower its hood, yet a figure there was too distant to make out properly. (B03-136, B03-150, B03-152, B03-170)
- **Explicit.** The whole shore can be walked: two pupils circle it three times one Sunday after breakfast, then go to the library. (B04-083)
- **Inference.** One lap is probably no more than two or three miles. Because: Three laps fit between breakfast and a session in the library. At walking pace a long morning covers six to nine miles. (B04-083)
- **Inference.** The middle must be deep enough to cover a masted ship, which came up there from under the surface. Because: Mast, rigging and hull rose in turn at the centre of the lake until the ship floated clear. This holds unless the whirlpool was a magical gateway; the text does not say. (B04-045)

- **Explicit.** Indoor views differ: a small piece from a landing seven flights up, a wide stretch from classrooms, swimmers from a corridor near the hospital wing, the shore beech from a dormitory. (B03-014, B04-153, B05-301, B05-227)
- **Inference.** Treat castle views as partial: no passage shows the whole lake, and Moody's classroom window shows only the top sail of the moored ship. Because: Every indoor view is described as a part. The hull of a ship anchored in the shallows is not mentioned from that window, so terrain or angle hides the near shore there. (B04-194, B03-014, B04-153)
- **Reported.** Reports put the Slytherin common room and the Chamber tunnels beneath it, and Myrtle says a flush can carry her down to it. (B07-028, B02-141, B04-145)

- **Explicit.** The surface is normally calm: black at night, steel or iron grey in cold weather, blue or clear green in summer sun. (B01-007, B01-061, B03-151, B04-153, B02-116, B05-252, B06-208)

### Conflicts

## Lakeshore and beech tree

The lakeshore is the band of bank, shallows and lakeside grass around the lake, reached from the castle down the lawns and from the station by a steep path through pines. The banks are soft and muddy, and the water deepens gradually from knee to waist height over silt and slick flat stones. The chief landmark is a large beech at the water's edge, tall enough to pick out directly from a Gryffindor dormitory window, with dense bushes nearby and open grass where pupils sit. Other bushes and shrubs stand along the bank, one at the very edge on Hagrid's side. Banks are fixed only as pairs: the beech looks across to the funeral site, and a spot near Hagrid's looks straight across to where Sirius collapsed in 1994. The far-side bank is within a ten-minute run of the library.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** By the shore route the far-side bank is probably under a mile from the front doors. Because: After waking at 9.20, talking, and running through the castle, Harry had perhaps seven minutes on open ground. A flat-out run, ending with a stitch, covers about a mile at most in that time. (B04-255, B04-158)
- **Explicit.** A short walk round from the lawns reaches a bank hidden from passers-by behind shrubs. (B05-306)
- **Explicit.** Wading out, the water passes the knee and reaches the waist before there is any need to swim. (B04-160)
- **Explicit.** The beech is large and, seen from a dormitory, towering; its shade takes a group of four sitting on the grass. (B05-086, B05-227, B05-221)

## Underwater lake terrain

The underwater terrain is the lake floor beyond the shallows, known only from the swim of the second task in Book 4. The bottom drops out of sight soon after the wading margin. Heading for the middle, a swimmer passes tangled black weed and wide mud plains strewn with dull stones, then a light-green weed bed two feet deep that runs beyond sight, then great stretches of black mud that cloud when stirred, ending at a large painted rock near the merpeople village. Visibility is about ten feet. The light is a failing grey, and the water overhead stays dark until close to the surface. No depth is stated: an aside says fathoms, and every clue points to a basin far deeper than a swimmer's dive, so model at least 100 feet.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** No depth figure is given. An aside calls the lake fathoms deep, and Harry thinks of it as very deep. (B04-013, B04-254)
- **Explicit.** Swimming out, Harry soon loses sight of the bottom even with improved eyesight, then dives and keeps going deeper towards the middle. (B04-161, B04-162, B04-163)
- **Explicit.** Rising from the village with two passengers is slow work, and he judges himself still very deep because the water above is so dark. (B04-169)
- **Explicit.** Daylight shows only near the top, and Harry's breath fails with about ten feet left to climb. That is the last stretch, not the depth. (B04-170)
- **Estimate.** Allow at least 100 feet (30 m) of water over the deep basin. Because: A whole masted ship rose from under the middle, and a mast alone is likely to exceed 60 feet. Daylight fails well above the bottom; water with ten-foot visibility goes dark at roughly 60 to 100 feet. Assumptions from real lakes, not text. (B04-045, B04-169, B04-170)
- **Explicit.** Visibility is about ten feet, so obstacles appear with little warning. (B04-162)

## Merpeople village

The merpeople village is a settlement of stone dwellings on the lake bed, in deep water well out from the second-task starting bank and about a quarter of a mile from the castle. It is reached across black mud past a large rock painted with a hunting scene. Rough stone houses streaked with algae have dark windows and doors, and some are also called caves. They grow more numerous further in, some with weed gardens, and they line a central square. There a gigantic merperson statue, roughly cut from a boulder, rises behind an open space where a choir sang during the second task of Book 4. Loose rocks litter the floor. It is big enough to have a corner, a square and a crowd of residents, but no count is given.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** A whole crowd floats in the square; one merman is seven feet tall, and about twenty escort the swimmers on the surface. (B04-167, B04-168, B04-170)
- **Explicit.** The statue is a gigantic merperson cut from a single boulder. (B04-167)

## Durmstrang ship

The Durmstrang ship is the visiting school's vessel, moored in the lake shallows from 30 October 1994 until the end of that school year. It is a dark masted sailing ship with rigging, black sails, portholes and a deck, looking like a wreck brought back up, and it serves as the delegation's lodging. It arrived by rising whole out of the middle of the lake, then glided to the bank and anchored with a plank to the shore. It lay in sight of the top of the lawns and was passed on the walk from the front doors to the gates. Its sails show from the Owlery, and its top sail from Moody's classroom. Its length is never given, but about a dozen students and their headmaster lived aboard for most of a year, so it cannot be small.

- **Explicit.** Its sails can be seen from the Owlery, and its top sail from Moody's classroom window. (B04-180, B04-194)

## Dumbledore’s tomb and funeral site

Dumbledore's tomb is a white marble tomb beside the lake, permanent from June 1997. It stands near the water's edge, by the bank the judges used for the second task, and is reflected in the lake, so that it shows from the beech across the water. It formed on the day of the funeral, when white flames around the body and its marble table left a marble tomb encasing both. For that one day hundreds of golden chairs stood in rows with a central aisle, all facing the table, on the bank beside the water, with a view across the lake to the Forest. Outside mourners already filled half of them when the school arrived. In March 1998 Voldemort split the tomb lengthwise, and no repair is described.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** It is larger than a tall man lying down, since it encases a long thin body and the table under it. Because: No dimensions are given. It replaced the table with the body on it, and it splits along the full length of the body. (B06-210, B07-033)
- **Estimate.** Allow at least 800 chairs for the funeral; that is still hundreds. Because: Mourners held half the chairs before the school sat. If 400 pupils and staff took the rest, even with some pupils gone home, the total is 800. At 0.5 m a chair and 1 m a row, twenty rows of forty fill about 21 m by 20 m. (B06-221, B06-206)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It lies across the lake from the beech, and the two are linked by a walk round the bank. (B06-211)
- **Explicit.** The funeral seats were very close to the bank where Dumbledore spoke with the Merchieftainess in February 1995. (B06-208)
- **Explicit.** From the site the view across the water runs to the Forest edge. The route from the castle leaves the front doors and heads down to the lake. (B06-209, B06-205)
