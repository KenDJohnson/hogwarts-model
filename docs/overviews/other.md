# Other rooms and school-wide subjects

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/other.md)

A mixed group of one room and several school-wide subjects. The trophy room is a display room with a door at each end, on the third floor by the walked journey and the sixth by a Map reading. The Marauder's Map is a parchment plan of every floor. Portraits line corridors and stairs, and their subjects move between frames. Armour and statues stand on plinths throughout the castle, with a long gallery of armour near the trophy room. Ghosts move through walls with no need of openings. Unnamed one-off rooms, including a laundry, cupboards and a vanishing room of chamberpots, complete the group.

- **Inference.** Portraits, armour and statues recur in corridors across many floors, so bare corridors would be wrong. Because: Armour and statues are a general corridor fitting (B01-027, B07-063), and portrait-lined corridors recur from the first night on (B01-021). (B01-027, B01-021, B07-063)

## Trophy room

A display room on an upper floor, the third or the sixth as the text disagrees, with a door at each end, another room beyond one door and a long gallery of armour beyond the other. Its crystal cases hold silver and gold cups, shields, plates and statues, about a hundred cups by one pupil's guess, including the Quidditch Cup, special awards, medals and a list of past Head Boys. Malfoy claims it is always unlocked. A 1991 night journey goes to the third floor, while a 1995 reading of the Marauder's Map puts it one floor above the fifth. The walked journey supports the third floor, but this is not settled.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** Ron guesses about a hundred cups, and a detention polishing them runs until late at night. (B02-032)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Another room lies beyond one door, and a long gallery of armour beyond the other. (B01-048, B01-049)
- **Explicit.** Pupils think of it as a very long way from the Charms classroom. (B01-049)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** It has a door at either end, so people can pass straight through. (B01-048)
- **Inference.** It has windows, since moonlight reaches the cases. Because: At night the cases shine where moonlight catches them (B01-048); no window is mentioned directly. (B01-048)

### Getting there

- **Reported.** Malfoy claims it is always unlocked, and the pupils do enter at midnight. (B01-045, B01-048)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Its crystal cases display silver and gold cups, shields, plates and statues. (B01-048)
- **Explicit.** Awards include the Quidditch Cup and a Special Award for Services to the School. (B02-032)
- **Explicit.** A burnished gold shield sits in a corner cabinet; the room also holds a Medal for Magical Merit and a list of past Head Boys. (B02-099)
- **Reported.** Its shields carry names: Riddle searched them for his father. (B06-097)

### Conflicts

- Third floor against sixth. In 1991 the pupils go up a staircase to the third floor and on towards the trophy room (B01-047). In January 1995 Harry, in the corridor outside the fifth-floor Prefects' bathroom, reads Peeves on the Map one floor up, in the trophy room, which makes the sixth floor (B04-139, B04-131). (B01-047, B04-139, B04-131)
- The third-floor claim has a flaw of its own: the pupils set out from the seventh-floor Gryffindor entrance (B01-053), yet they go up a staircase to reach the third floor (B01-047). (B01-047, B01-053)

The escape runs through the armour gallery, corridors and a tapestry passage to the Charms corridor, whose end door opens on the forbidden third-floor corridor, with no stairs mentioned (B01-049, B01-050). The sixth floor rests on one glance at the Map. (B01-047, B01-049, B01-050, B04-139)

## Marauder’s Map

A magical parchment, not a place: a map of the castle and grounds with a detailed plan of every floor, showing people as moving labelled dots. It matters here as evidence for the castle's layout. It shows many shortcuts and secret passages, and the twins count seven passages to Hogsmeade on it. It shows Gryffindor and Ravenclaw in separate towers, Slytherin in the dungeons and Hufflepuff in a basement near the kitchens. It reaches only to the school boundaries, reveals people under invisibility, and never shows the Room of Requirement. Positions on the sheet are not compass bearings.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** It shows the castle and grounds in detail, with a plan of every floor. (B03-061, B06-107)
- **Reported.** It reaches only to the school boundaries. (B04-191)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It shows Gryffindor in one tower, Ravenclaw in another on the west side, Slytherin in the dungeons and Hufflepuff in a basement near the kitchens. (B05-115, B06-129)
- **Explicit.** Dumbledore's study lies in its top left corner and Snape's office in its bottom left corner. (B03-061, B04-139)
- **Inference.** Sheet positions are not compass bearings. Because: The text never says which way up the sheet lies or how the floors are arranged on it. (B03-061, B04-139)

### Shape and structure

### Getting there

## Portraits and paintings

Paintings hang throughout the castle, along corridors, up staircases and on landings. This is a subject, not a place. Their subjects move, talk, whisper at passers-by and visit neighbouring frames, so a frame can stand empty or crowded. Whole staircases are picture-lined. Known examples include Sir Cadogan's large landscape on a seventh-floor landing, women in crinolines and monks on the North Tower stair, a landscape with trees on the fourth floor, a map of Argyllshire on the second floor, drunken monks near Charms, and bewigged witches on a marble-stair landing. Former heads have other frames elsewhere and can travel between them. Pictures were scrubbed in 1994, damaged in 1996 and 1997, and packed with shouting figures during the 1998 battle.

## Suits of armour and statues

Suits of armour and statues stand throughout the castle, usually on plinths in corridors. This is a group, not a place. A long gallery near the trophy room is full of armour. Statues range from smaller to larger than life and include animals; several are big enough to hide a person. Named or located examples are Boris the Bewildered on the fifth floor, Lachlan the Lanky on the seventh, the humpbacked one-eyed witch halfway along a third-floor corridor, Gregory the Smarmy, Wilfred the Wistful, a bust of Paracelsus, a large stone griffin and a muttering stone head in a recess. Suits creak, turn their helmets and laugh, and in December they glow or sing. In May 1998 all were animated and sent to the boundaries, leaving empty plinths.

## Ghosts

The castle's ghosts: translucent pearly-white figures that move through walls, doors and tables. They are a subject, not a place. About twenty crossed a chamber together in 1991, and hundreds gathered at a dungeon party in 1992. For a modeller the point is that ghost routes need no openings, and that ghosts drift a few inches off the floor and all but vanish in bright sun. Myrtle also travels by plumbing.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** About twenty ghosts crossed the antechamber together in 1991. (B01-012)
- **Explicit.** Hundreds crowded a dungeon dance floor at the 1992 party. (B02-043)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Myrtle travels by plumbing: she dives into her toilet and surfaces elsewhere in the castle's bathrooms. (B02-059, B06-130)
- **Reported.** Nick says the Bloody Baron likes to go up the Astronomy Tower and make a noise there. (B06-147)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** They are translucent and pearly white, and barely visible in strong sun. (B01-012, B06-207)
- **Explicit.** The Grey Lady is tall, with long hair and a floor-length cloak, and glides a few inches above the floor. (B07-070)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** They pass through walls, tables and blackboards, so their routes need no openings. (B01-012, B03-039, B05-032, B05-309)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** A petrified Nick turned black and smoky, hung six inches above the floor and had to be fanned upstairs. (B02-081, B02-082)

## Unnamed one-off rooms

One-off rooms the text mentions without naming or placing. They are listed together only for convenience, and they are not one room. Known instances are an empty room with a closing door, a school laundry, a student store-cupboard for potion ingredients, a lockable broom cupboard between Defence and the staff room, a dungeon cupboard where the young Aragog lived, a quiet room used during exams, a vanishing room of chamberpots, and a Vanishing Cabinet on the first floor.

- **Explicit.** A lockable broom cupboard lies round a corner from the Defence classroom, on the way to the staff room. (B03-028)
