# Chamber of Secrets and underground access

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/chamber-of-secrets.md)

The route to the Chamber of Secrets descends from Moaning Myrtle’s bathroom, on the second floor by most evidence, to below the dungeons. A sink that opens only to Parseltongue exposes the largest pipe of a branching system, which slants and twists down past the first and ground floors and the dungeon level, then levels out into a wet stone tunnel high enough to stand in. That tunnel bends repeatedly past small bones, a shed skin and a rockfall to a solid wall carved with two serpents. Behind it is the Chamber: one very long pillared hall ending at a statue of Slytherin as tall as the room, with a dark cavity behind its mouth that housed the basilisk. Nothing here has a stated dimension except the snake, at twenty feet or more. Harry’s miles under the school and Ron’s under the lake are guesses; the narrated fact is only that the way goes deeper than the dungeons.

## Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The one narrated depth: the pipe descends further below the school than the dungeons. (B02-141)
- **Reported.** Harry’s miles under the school and Ron’s under the lake are characters’ guesses at the foot of the pipe, not surveyed depths. (B02-141)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: if the hall lies below the dungeons its floor is about 30 m under the bathroom, since its 15 m height must fit beneath them. Because: Three storeys to the dungeon floor at an assumed 4 m, the estimated 15 m hall and some rock over it. If the hall lies clear of the castle, under the grounds or lake, it could be shallower. (B02-141, B02-147, B05-110)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: the walk from the foot of the pipe to the back wall of the Chamber is about 250 m, most of it tunnel. Because: Adds about 200 m of tunnel and about 45 m of hall, both assumed minimums. (B02-154, B02-146)

## Where it is

- **Inference.** Top to bottom: second-floor sink, pipe past the first and ground floors and dungeon level, tunnels at the bottom, then the Chamber at the far end. Because: Floor numbering places the dungeons one level under the ground floor, and the narrated order of pipe, tunnel and door gives the rest. (B02-057, B02-141, B02-145, B05-110)

## How it changes

- **Reported.** The route was used about fifty years before 1993 by report, again in 1992–93, and once more in 1998, when the rockfall was evidently passable. (B02-094, B02-148, B07-077)

## Chamber entrance and pipe

The way down to the Chamber of Secrets starts at a sink in Moaning Myrtle’s girls’ bathroom, on the second floor by most evidence. A tiny snake scratched on one copper tap marks it. Spoken Parseltongue makes the tap glow and spin and the whole sink sinks away, exposing a pipe a grown man could fit into. The pipe plunges steeply and twists, past smaller branches, down through the castle to below the dungeons, then levels out and ends in a stone tunnel. Nobody can climb back up it on foot; Fawkes carried four people out in 1993.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The exposed pipe is large: a grown man can slide into it. (B02-140)
- **Explicit.** It is the largest pipe in view; smaller ones branch off in every direction. (B02-141)
- **Explicit.** The ride goes deeper under the school than the dungeons before the pipe levels out, and feels endless to Harry. (B02-141)
- **Reported.** At the bottom Harry guesses they are miles under the school, and Ron guesses they are under the lake. Both are characters’ guesses. (B02-141)
- **Inference.** From the second floor the pipe must pass the first and ground floors and the dungeon level, so it descends more than three storeys inside walls and then rock. Because: The dungeons lie one level under the ground floor, and the narration takes the descent below them. (B02-141, B02-057, B05-110)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: a vertical drop of at least 16 m, and about 30 m if the Chamber lies under the dungeons. The pipe itself is much longer, because it slants and twists. Because: Three storeys to the dungeon floor at an assumed 4 m is 12 m, and going deeper than that means at least 16 m. The Chamber’s estimated 15 m height, plus some rock over it, gives about 30 m if it lies directly beneath the dungeons. (B02-141, B02-147, B05-110)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The entrance is a sink in Moaning Myrtle’s bathroom, the one in front of her toilet. (B02-140)
- **Explicit.** That bathroom is on the second floor by narration and by its place beside the second-floor writing corridor, three floors under the fifth-floor Prefects’ bathroom. (B02-057, B04-115, B04-145, B04-131)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** A tiny snake is scratched into one copper tap, the tap Myrtle says has never worked. (B02-140)
- **Explicit.** Dark, slimy and steep, it bends repeatedly, then levels off and throws riders out on to wet stone. (B02-141)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** English has no effect. Parseltongue makes the tap shine white and spin, and the sink drops out of sight. (B02-140)
- **Reported.** In 1998 Ron opens it by copying Harry’s hissing after several tries, so no born Parselmouth is required. (B07-077)
- **Explicit.** It cannot be climbed unaided: from the bottom Harry sees only a long dark shaft. Holding Fawkes’s tail, four people fly up in a chain, and the sink slides shut behind them. (B02-155, B02-156)
- **Reported.** By Harry’s reasoning the basilisk reached the upper floors through this plumbing, moving inside the walls. (B02-133)

### How it changes

- **Inference.** Ron returns from the Chamber in 1998 carrying a broomstick, which suggests a way back up the pipe. Because: The text does not say how they got out, but the pipe cannot be climbed and the broom is the only aid mentioned. (B07-077)

### Conflicts

- Hermione says Myrtle haunts the toilet on the first floor, and the 1993 walk from Lockhart’s second-floor office goes down stairs to reach it. Other scenes put it on the second floor. (B02-045, B02-138, B02-033, B02-057, B04-115, B04-145)

### Open questions

- How the system reaches other floors. Every branch seen is narrower than the main pipe, yet a snake as thick as an oak trunk travelled through the castle walls.

## Chamber tunnels

The stone tunnels between the foot of the pipe and the serpent doors of the Chamber of Secrets. They lie below the dungeons, unlit, wet and high enough to stand in, and they bend repeatedly. In 1993 they held small animal bones, a shed basilisk skin from a snake of twenty feet or more and, after Lockhart’s failed spell, a rockfall that blocked the way until Ron cleared a gap.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** High enough to stand in. Width is not given. (B02-141)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: about 200 m from pipe to Chamber. From the serpent doors it takes a few minutes to come within earshot of the rockfall, and the pipe is further on. Because: Three minutes at an assumed 65 m a minute is about 200 m, before the stretch from the rockfall back to the pipe. No length is stated. (B02-154, B02-155)
- **Explicit.** A shed snake skin lies coiled right across the tunnel, vivid green and huge; the narration judges the snake that shed it at twenty feet or more. (B02-143)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Runs from where the pipe levels out to the solid serpent wall of the Chamber. (B02-141, B02-145)
- **Inference.** The Chamber end may be the lower: the walk back to the pipe is described as going up the tunnel. Because: The wording is the only evidence of slope, and it may be loose. (B02-154, B02-155)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Dark stone with wet, slimy walls and a wet floor; footsteps and voices ring in it. (B02-142)
- **Explicit.** The tunnel keeps bending: one bend comes before the skin, and it turns again and again beyond it. (B02-142, B02-145)
- **Explicit.** No light of its own: only wand light, and in 1993 the glow of Fawkes’s wings. (B02-142, B02-155)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Small animal bones, including a rat’s skull, litter the floor. (B02-142)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** In May 1993 Ron’s damaged wand explodes and large chunks of ceiling come down, forming a solid rock barrier and leaving huge cracks overhead. (B02-144)
- **Explicit.** Ron later shifts enough rock by hand to leave a gap that Ginny, Fawkes and Harry can squeeze through. Nothing says the fall was ever fully cleared. (B02-144, B02-154)
- **Inference.** The way was still passable in 1998, since Ron and Hermione reach the basilisk’s skull beyond it. Because: They return with fangs from the Chamber, which lies past the rockfall. (B07-077)

## Chamber of Secrets

A very long pillared stone hall hidden far below Hogwarts, deeper than the dungeons and reached by the pipe from Moaning Myrtle’s bathroom and a winding tunnel. Its door is a solid wall carved with two serpents that parts at a hissed command. Inside, a dim space runs between paired rows of towering stone pillars wound with carved serpents, up to a vaulted ceiling lost in shadow. At the far end a statue of Salazar Slytherin, as tall as the hall itself, stands against the back wall with enormous feet on the smooth floor. Its mouth opens on command into a dark cavity of unknown depth, from which the basilisk emerges. The hall gave a snake as thick as an oak trunk room to rear, thrash and turn among the pillars, so it is far larger than any dungeon classroom. No figure for its length, width or height is given. The dead basilisk was left lying there in 1993 and its skull was still there in 1998.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** A single very long, dim hall. The statue at its far end comes into sight only once Harry reaches the final pair of pillars. (B02-146, B02-147)
- **Explicit.** Towering stone pillars stand in pairs down its length and rise to a vaulted ceiling that is lost in darkness. (B02-146, B02-147)
- **Explicit.** A statue as high as the Chamber stands against the back wall. Harry must crane to see its giant face; Ginny lies between its enormous feet, and the diary by its toes. (B02-147)
- **Explicit.** The basilisk is thick as an oak trunk, with fangs as long as Harry’s sword and a mouth that could take him whole. (B02-152)
- **Explicit.** It rears high, its head moving among the pillars, while its tail sweeps the floor and its body strikes pillars as it turns. (B02-152)
- **Explicit.** Fawkes, the size of a swan, appears on top of a pillar and circles the snake’s raised head. (B02-150, B02-152)
- **Inference.** Pillars must stand far enough apart for that snake to turn between them, with space along the walls for a boy to dodge it. Because: The snake rears, coils round and lunges among pillars and at the wall while Harry runs and dodges there. (B02-152)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: statue and ceiling at least 15 m high. A mouth 0.7 m wide for the snake suggests a face over 2 m tall, and a figure eight faces high. Because: Human proportions are assumed: a face about three mouth-openings tall, a figure eight faces tall. The 0.7 m comes from an oak-thick body that can still be stepped over when dead. (B02-151, B02-152, B02-154)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: at least 45 m long and 15 m wide. A very long room should be several times its height, and the rows need about 8 m between them plus side aisles. Because: Three times the 15 m height is an assumption, as is the row spacing for a snake to turn. No length or width is stated. (B02-146, B02-152)
- **Estimate.** Modelling allowance: a snake from 6 m (the text’s twenty-foot floor) up to about 15 m. Its oak-trunk thickness would make twenty feet oddly stout. Because: Assumes a body 0.6 m thick and a large snake’s length of about 25 times its thickness. The text gives only twenty feet or more. (B02-143, B02-152)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** At the end of the tunnels, below the dungeons. The only route ever seen is the bathroom pipe followed by the tunnels. (B02-141, B02-145, B02-154)
- **Reported.** By legend Slytherin built it secretly in the castle, unknown to the other founders; repeated searches by scholars and heads of the school found nothing. (B02-054, B02-055)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** The door is a solid wall closing the tunnel, carved with two intertwined snakes whose eyes are set with big emeralds. (B02-145)
- **Explicit.** Pillars are wound with carved serpents, and other stone snakes have hollow eye sockets. (B02-146)
- **Explicit.** Smooth, dusty stone floor and dark walls; footsteps and laughter ring loudly. (B02-146, B02-147, B02-151)
- **Explicit.** An odd greenish half-light fills the hall and throws long shadows, with no source named. (B02-146)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** A hissed command splits the wall and its halves slide away out of sight; the doors shut by themselves, with a soft hiss, behind people leaving. (B02-145, B02-154)
- **Reported.** By legend only Slytherin’s heir could unseal it. Riddle says finding the entrance took him five years. (B02-054, B02-149)
- **Explicit.** Addressed in Parseltongue, the statue’s stone face moves and its mouth gapes into a huge black hole; the basilisk climbs out and drops to the floor. (B02-151)
- **Inference.** Behind the statue’s mouth is a hollow, passage or lair of unknown depth, wide enough for the snake’s body. Because: The snake rises from somewhere inside the statue, and it must pass through the mouth. (B02-151, B02-152)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** The statue shows an old wizard with a monkey-like face and a long thin beard reaching nearly to the hem of sweeping stone robes. (B02-147)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** Reported openings: fifty years before 1992–93, when a girl died in a bathroom and a pupil was expelled; then in 1992–93, when Riddle says Ginny opened it through the diary. (B02-094, B02-103, B02-127, B02-148)
- **Explicit.** After May 1993 the dead basilisk stays on the floor among blood and ink. (B02-153, B02-154)
- **Reported.** In 1998 Ron and Hermione tear curved yellow fangs from its skull, so the remains are still there five years later. (B07-077)

### Open questions

- Where the hall lies in plan: under the castle or, as Ron only guessed, under the lake.
- How many pairs of pillars stand in it.
- What lies behind the statue’s mouth, and whether it joins the pipes.
