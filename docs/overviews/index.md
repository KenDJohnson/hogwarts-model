# Hogwarts: consolidated overview

The top-level description of the castle, built from every catalogue record. Read this before generating an image or model, then the page for the area you are working on. The [generation brief](../generation-brief.md) says what any attempt must meet. Each fact cites catalogue records, and [overviews.json](../../research/overviews.json) holds the same data.

Hogwarts is a vast stone castle on a cliff above a lake. Its main body has eight storeys, a ground floor and seven numbered floors in British numbering, with a dungeon level below the ground floor. No floor above the seventh is ever numbered: everything higher stands inside a tower. Five towers are named. The Astronomy Tower is the tallest. Ravenclaw and Gryffindor are the next highest. West Tower, with the Owlery, and North Tower, with the Divination classroom, are lower but still rise above the seventh floor. Gryffindor Tower starts on the seventh floor with dormitories stacked above its common room, so the best modelling figure for its top is the fourteenth floor, 15 storeys up. The castle has 142 staircases, about 20 between each pair of floors. It houses hundreds of pupils, and its shared rooms are built to match: a Great Hall that took about 1,200 seats at one ball, kitchens of the same size beneath it, and a library of hundreds of rows. The books give no overall dimensions, so its size has to be built up from these counts. It should read as far larger than an ordinary school or an ordinary castle.

## Conventions

- **Floors are British.** The ground floor comes first, and the first floor is one flight above it. The seventh floor is therefore the eighth storey. The books agree: from the seventh floor it is six floors down and then the marble staircase, seven staircases link the ground and seventh floors, and the dungeons are eight floors below the seventh.
- **Floor numbers and storeys differ.** A floor number leaves out the ground floor and a storey count includes it, so the fourteenth floor is the fifteenth storey.
- **Evidence labels.** Explicit: narrated as fact. Reported: a character says or believes it. Inference: deduced from the text, with the reasoning shown. Estimate: a modelling minimum resting on an assumption the books do not give, with the arithmetic shown.
- **Estimates are not canon.** Estimates exist to stop a model being made far too small. Treat them as lower limits to design above, and never present them as facts from the books.
- **Only the novels count.** Films, games, wikis and companion material are not evidence here. Anything known only from them is left out, including their castle plans.
- **Dated states.** Keep the permanent building separate from dated states such as decorations, tournament set-ups, classroom changes under each teacher and battle damage.

## The castle as a whole

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** The main body has eight storeys: the ground floor and seven numbered floors. Because: From the seventh floor it is six floors down and then the marble staircase; seven staircases link the ground and seventh floors; no higher floor is numbered. (B04-106, B04-060, B01-053)
- **Explicit.** A dungeon level lies below the ground floor: the dungeons are eight floors below the seventh. (B05-110)
- **Explicit.** It is described from the first as vast, with many turrets and towers, and later as a towering mass of turrets. (B01-006, B05-014)
- **Reported.** Durmstrang, by one of its own pupils, is not as big and has only four floors. (B04-126)
- **Explicit.** There are 142 staircases, of many forms: wide and sweeping, narrow and rickety, some with a vanishing step, some going elsewhere on a Friday. (B01-027)
- **Estimate.** That is about 20 staircases per gap between floors, or about 16 after setting aside 30 for towers and shortcuts. These are separate flights spread across a wide plan, not a stair stack counted floor by floor. Because: 142 / 7 gaps = 20. (142 - 30) / 7 = 16. Any one climb from ground to seventh uses seven of them. (B01-027)
- **Explicit.** Five towers are named. Astronomy is the tallest; Ravenclaw, Astronomy and Gryffindor are the three highest; West and North are lower. (B06-175, B01-102, B07-068, B04-034, B03-014)
- **Estimate.** Gryffindor Tower's best modelling height is a top floor of 14, 15 storeys counting the ground floor: seven dormitory levels above the seventh-floor common room. The strict minimum from the text is top floor 9. Because: Seven year groups, one room per year on its own level: 7 + 7 = 14. The text proves at least two doors below the top room: 7 + 2 = 9. (B02-016, B04-021, B03-086, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)
- **Estimate.** At 4.5 to 5 m per storey the seventh floor stands 32 to 35 m above the ground floor. Gryffindor's top room is then about 56 to 60 m up, and the Astronomy Tower must clear it. Because: 7 x 4.5 to 5 m = 31.5 to 35 m. Adding 7 dormitory levels at 3.5 m gives 56 to 59.5 m. All storey heights are assumptions. (B04-106, B04-021, B06-175)
- **Inference.** Seen from outside, the main body should show at least eight rows of windows, with far more up the towers. Because: Eight storeys of rooms, with windows recorded up to the seventh floor, where at least thirteen run along from West Tower. (B03-139, B01-006)
- **Estimate.** Plan a main block at least about 200 m long. Because: A 50 by 24 m Great Hall, a library of 1,500 m² and eight storeys with about 20 staircases per gap will not fit in much less, and Ron's ten minutes to the top of North Tower implies several hundred metres of corridor. Rough, and not a stated figure. (B03-014, B04-124, B01-073)
- **Estimate.** Pupils are always counted in hundreds, never given as a number. Plan on about 300 to 800. Because: About a hundred coaches at three to five pupils each gives 300 to 500. One five-bed room per year and twenty-place classes point to about 280; more than a hundred exam tables point higher. Size shared rooms for the top of the range. (B01-151, B04-248, B05-315, B06-217, B07-140, B04-004, B05-219)
- **Explicit.** Great Hall: about a hundred tables of about a dozen people each at the 1994 ball, more than a hundred exam tables on another occasion, and hundreds of sleeping bags across its floor. (B04-124, B05-219, B03-042)
- **Estimate.** The Great Hall needs at least 1,200 m² of floor, about 50 by 24 m, and at least 12 m to the ceiling. Because: About 1,200 seats at about 1 m² a seat. All spacing is assumed. (B04-124)
- **Explicit.** The kitchens directly under the Great Hall are as large as it is, with at least a hundred elves at work. (B04-108, B04-109)
- **Explicit.** The Entrance Hall could take a whole four-bedroom house, and its ceiling is too high to see. (B01-010, B01-147)
- **Explicit.** The library has hundreds of narrow rows, thousands of shelves and tens of thousands of books. (B01-073)
- **Estimate.** The library needs at least 300 m of row spacing and about 1,500 m² of floor, as one hall or a suite of rooms. Because: 200 rows at an assumed 1.5 m per row = 300 m. Rows about 4 m long plus aisles give about 1,500 m². (B01-073)
- **Explicit.** The Room of Hidden Things is the size of a large cathedral, with walls of objects up to fifty feet high. (B06-156, B07-081, B07-084)
- **Explicit.** The Owlery holds hundreds upon hundreds of owls, on perches rising to the top of the tower. (B04-035)
- **Explicit.** The Prefects' bathroom has a bath like a swimming pool with about a hundred golden taps around it. (B04-142)
- **Explicit.** The Quidditch hoops are fifty feet high, and the whole school fits in the stands. (B01-054, B01-156, B02-170, B03-171, B06-220)
- **Reported.** Ron allows ten minutes from the Great Hall to the top of North Tower. (B03-014)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** The seventh floor is a full storey: it carries the Gryffindor entrance, the Room of Requirement's wall, the Headmaster's gargoyle, Flitwick's office and the foot of the North Tower stair. Because: Each is placed on the seventh floor separately, and the gargoyle is said to be on the far side of the castle from the Room of Requirement. (B01-053, B05-108, B06-035, B03-139, B03-111)
- **Inference.** Rooms are not sorted by type. Teachers’ offices, classrooms and bathrooms are scattered through every floor. Because: Offices are placed on the first, third and seventh floors and in the dungeons. (B01-103, B03-139, B05-055)
- **Inference.** The largest states of the Room of Requirement cannot fit behind a seventh-floor wall, so treat them as a separate volume reached by the door. Because: A cathedral-sized room would not fit on one upper floor of the castle. (B05-108, B06-156)

### Conflicts

- Hospital wing: never numbered. The only journey that counts floors gives the third; other journeys read as the first, and one as the seventh or higher. (B05-268, B02-097, B06-118)
- Defence teacher's office: second floor in Book 2, third in Book 5, and fourth on one Book 4 route. (B02-033, B05-055, B04-147)
- Moaning Myrtle's bathroom: first floor by Hermione in Book 2, second floor by the narrated climb and in Book 4. (B02-045, B02-046, B04-115)
- Trophy room: third floor by the one walked route, sixth by a reading of the Marauder’s Map. (B01-047, B04-139)
- Headmaster's gargoyle: seventh floor in Book 6, while routes in Books 2 and 4 read much lower. (B06-035, B04-188, B02-082)
