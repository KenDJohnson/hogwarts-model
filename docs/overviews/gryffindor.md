# Gryffindor Tower, common room and dormitories

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/gryffindor.md)

Gryffindor Tower is a round tower holding one House's common room and dormitories. You reach it on the seventh floor, the highest floor the books number, along a dead-end corridor closed by a large portrait of the Fat Lady. Given the password, the painting swings out like a door over a round hole set above floor level, and you climb through into the common room: a cosy, shabby, round room with a fireplace, sagging armchairs, rickety tables and noticeboards, big enough for the whole House. Across the room two doors open on separate stone spiral stairs, boys' and girls'. Dormitory doors open along the stairs. At the head of the boys' stair, at the top of the Tower, is a circular room with five four-poster beds and high narrow windows. Its boys kept it from 1991 to at least 1996 while the year sign on the door changed, so rooms stay with a year group. Only this room is described. A round room cannot hold both stairs, so they rise outside the rooms, and the rooms need two stacks or alternating levels. No book gives the Tower's height. With one room for each of seven years on its own level the top floor is the fourteenth, 15 storeys counting the ground floor. That is the best modelling figure, not a stated one. Only the Astronomy Tower is known to stand higher.

## Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The whole House can crowd into it at once. About fifty at one 1997 party is not its limit. (B06-162, B02-165, B03-167, B04-244)
- **Estimate.** It should be at least 13 m across, and about 17 m if two dormitory stacks stand on it. Because: Assumes 0.75 m2 a person among furniture and a House of 175: 131 m2 is a circle 13 m across. Two 8.5 m stacks need 17 m. (B05-219, B02-165, B03-167, B04-244)
- **Inference.** Rooms stay with a year group: the five boys of 1991 took the top room as first-years and still held it in 1996, with only the door sign changing. Because: It is the same room at the stair's head in 1991, 1992 and 1994, with dormitories below it in 1996. (B01-024, B02-016, B04-021, B05-217)
- **Inference.** No book states the Tower's height. The strict minimum from the text is top floor 9, which is 10 storeys counting the ground floor. Because: The text proves two or more doors below the top room. If they share a level, that is 7 + 2 = 9. (B03-086, B05-217)
- **Estimate.** Read as a top floor, 10 to 12 means 3 to 5 dormitory levels and 11 to 13 storeys. It is a sound lower limit, but needs rooms sharing levels. Because: Seven rooms stacked 3+3+1 give 10, 2+2+2+1 give 11, 2+2+1+1+1 give 12. Read as storeys from the ground, the top floor would be 9 to 11. (B02-016, B03-086, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)
- **Estimate.** One room per year group on its own level gives floors 8 to 14: top floor 14, which is 15 storeys counting the ground floor. This is the best single figure. Because: 7 + 7 = 14; adding the ground floor gives 15. Assumes every year has one room per stair and each fills a level. Only Harry's year is shown. (B02-016, B04-021, B05-217, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)
- **Inference.** A round room cannot hold both spiral stairs side by side, so the stairs must rise outside the room circles. Because: Assuming rooms at least 7 m across and stairs 2.5 m across, two shafts would fill most of the width at every level. (B02-016, B05-048, B05-088)
- **Estimate.** Two round stacks side by side stay at top floor 14 but need a plan 17 m wide or two turrets. One stack with boys and girls alternating reaches top floor 21, 22 storeys. Because: 2 x 8.5 m per stack = 17 m. 7 + 14 = 21. Top floor 21 looks too high, as the Astronomy Tower would have to clear it. (B02-016, B05-088, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)
- **Estimate.** At a modest 3.5 m per storey, the top room's floor is about 49 m above the ground floor and the climb from the common room about 120 steps. Because: 14 x 3.5 = 49 m; 7 x 3.5 = 24.5 m; at 0.2 m per step about 120 steps. Both heights are assumptions. (B04-021, B05-088)

## Getting there

- **Explicit.** A corridor ends at a large portrait of the Fat Lady. On the password it swings out like a door over a round hole in the wall. (B01-022, B03-040, B05-025)
- **Inference.** The hole is a climb, not a doorway: its lower edge sits above the floor and it passes one person at a time. Because: Everyone climbs or scrambles through, a short first-year needed a leg up, and pupils queue at it. (B01-022, B06-024)
- **Inference.** Across the room from the portrait hole, two separate doors open on two separate stairs, so neither stair rises inside the room. - **Explicit.** It is circular, with five four-poster beds with deep red velvet hangings, a trunk at each foot, bedside cabinets, and high narrow windows. (B02-016, B02-102, B04-021)

## Conflicts

If so, some years need several rooms, which can add rooms per level without adding levels. (B02-016, B03-106, B05-219)
- Common room and top dormitory are called round or circular, yet both are given corners. (B01-023, B02-016, B03-166, B04-120)

## Gryffindor Tower

Gryffindor Tower is the round tower holding the Gryffindor common room and dormitories. It is entered on the seventh floor, the highest floor the books number, through a portrait at the end of a dead-end corridor, and everything above that level lies inside the tower. Its windows face the side of the grounds with Hagrid's cabin, the Forest edge and the lakeside beech. Its height is never stated. A 1998 battle plan names it with Ravenclaw and Astronomy as the three highest towers, and only Astronomy is known to be higher. Counting dormitories gives the modelling figure. The top room stands at the head of a stair with other dormitory doors below it, and a year group seems to keep one room for its whole school career. One room per level for seven years puts the top floor at the fourteenth, which is 15 storeys counting the ground floor. The strict minimum from the text alone is the ninth floor, and a single shared stack for boys and girls would reach the twenty-first. Treat 14 as an estimate, not canon.

- **Inference.** No book gives the Tower's height, its number of levels or its number of steps. Every height figure here is derived. Because: No passage counts them. The only count on a dormitory stair is the sixth step of the girls' stair, which measures nothing overall. (B04-021, B05-088)
- **Inference.** Rooms stay with a year group: the boys of 1991 took the top room as first-years and still held it in 1996, with only the door sign changing. Because: Same room at the stair's head in 1991, 1992 and 1994, with dormitories below it in 1996. So rooms are not handed up by seniority, and pass to new pupils when a year leaves. (B01-024, B02-016, B03-013, B04-021, B05-217)
- **Inference.** Seven year groups with one boys' room and one girls' room each would make 14 dormitories, seven per stair. Only Harry Potter's year is shown to fit this. Because: Seven years are confirmed and all five boys of Harry's year share one room, but no other year's rooms are described. (B02-016, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)
- **Inference.** Strict minimum from the text: top floor 9, which is 10 storeys counting the ground floor. Because: The text proves at least two doors below the top room. If they share one level, that is two dormitory levels above the common room: 7 + 2 = 9. (B03-086, B05-217)
- **Estimate.** A top floor of 10 to 12 means 3 to 5 dormitory levels and 11 to 13 storeys counting the ground floor. It needs two or three rooms sharing a level. Because: Seven rooms stacked 3+3+1 give floor 10, 2+2+2+1 give 11, 2+2+1+1+1 give 12. Round rooms pack badly, so lower rooms would be wedges. If 10 to 12 meant storeys from the ground, the top floor would be 9 to 11. (B02-016, B03-086, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)

(B02-016, B04-021, B05-217, B07-068, B01-146, B05-313, B06-213)

Because: 14 x 3.5 = 49 m; 7 x 3.5 = 24.5 m; at 0.2 m per step about 120 steps. Storey height and step height are assumptions; the text gives neither. (B04-021, B05-088)

## Fat Lady portrait entrance

The Fat Lady portrait is the only entrance to Gryffindor Tower that the books describe. It hangs on the seventh floor at the very end of a corridor that stops there: a large framed oil painting of an extremely fat woman in pink. Given the current password, the painting swings out into the corridor like a door and uncovers a round hole in the wall. The hole is a climb rather than a doorway, passes one person at a time, and opens straight into the common room. The painted occupant acts as the lock: if she is away nobody gets in, and she can refuse or waive the password at will. The canvas was slashed in October 1993, Sir Cadogan's portrait stood in until February 1994, and security trolls then patrolled the corridor.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** It passes one person at a time, so pupils queue to leave, but adults such as Professor McGonagall get through. Because: A queue forms at it on a school morning, and McGonagall steps out and back through it. No dimension is given. (B03-087, B06-024)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The portrait hangs on the seventh floor at the very end of a corridor that stops there. (B01-022, B01-053, B03-040, B05-025, B06-070)
- **Inference.** The corridor in front is hard-floored and takes moonlight, so it has outside windows along it or close by. Because: Harry finds its floor hard to sit on, and it is described as moonlit at night. (B05-149, B06-145)

### Shape and structure

## Gryffindor common room

The Gryffindor common room is a round tower room on the seventh floor, entered by climbing through the portrait hole. It is cosy and shabby but not small: the whole House has packed into it at once. Across the room from the portrait hole are the doors to two separate stairs, boys' and girls', and the noticeboard. The fire, in a brick fireplace with a worn hearthrug, is the room's focus, with the best armchairs round it. The rest is filled with soft, sagging armchairs, a sofa, rickety movable tables, a chest of drawers, a tapestry, two clocks, a lamp and candles. Glazed windows open onto a view of the grounds, Hagrid's cabin and the Forest edge, and owls land on their outside sills. The room is cleaned overnight by house-elves.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The whole House has gathered in it at once, in 1992 standing on the furniture, in 1994 waiting up overnight and greeting a new champion. (B02-165, B03-167, B04-244)
- **Explicit.** Fifty people watched a moment at one 1997 party, and it is called crowded or packed on many other nights. Neither sets its limit. (B01-060, B02-119, B06-061, B06-162, B06-200)
- **Inference.** It is wide enough for far corners: in dim light a large boy asleep in a distant chair went unnoticed by someone sure the room was empty. Because: A person in a chair can only be missed like this if the room is well over a few paces across. (B06-214)
- **Estimate.** It should be at least 13 m across, and about 17 m if two dormitory stacks stand on it. Because: Assumes 0.75 m2 a person among furniture. A House of 70 needs 52 m2 (8 m across); a House of 175 needs 131 m2 (13 m across). Over 100 candidates a year, over four Houses and seven years, gives 175. Two 8.5 m stacks need 17 m. (B05-219, B02-165, B03-167, B04-244)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It is entered directly from the seventh floor through the portrait hole, so it occupies the seventh-floor level of the Tower. (B01-022, B01-023, B01-053, B06-024)
- **Inference.** The Defence Against the Dark Arts classroom is four floors below it, which makes that classroom third-floor. The staff room, McGonagall's office and the dungeons also lie below. Because: The text says four floors below; seventh minus four is third. (B01-064, B06-027, B06-031, B06-058)
- **Explicit.** The windows overlook the dark grounds, Hagrid's cabin and teaching at the Forest edge, and a low sun over the Forest treetops can be judged from the room. (B03-024, B05-178, B05-259, B06-135, B06-203)
- **Inference.** Portrait hole and hearth share one half of the circle; both stair doors and the noticeboard share the other. Because: The boys' door lies opposite both hole and fire, the noticeboard opposite the fire, the girls' door by the noticeboard, and pupils cross the room before dividing. (B02-016, B03-013, B04-082, B05-026, B05-088)
- **Explicit.** Sound carries from above: the closing of two dormitory doors, early-morning movement, and a yell down the boys' stair are heard from the room. Storms sound louder here than upstairs. (B02-119, B03-049, B05-060, B03-168)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** The room is round, called circular in Books 2, 4 and 5 and a tower room. (B01-023, B02-015, B04-020, B05-026)
- **Explicit.** Its windows are glazed and can be opened, with sills that take a perching owl. Owls fly in across the room, and a table stands at one window. (B01-063, B01-099, B04-032, B04-119, B05-070, B05-077, B05-128, B06-151)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The door to the boys' stair is across the room from the portrait hole. It stands behind anyone facing the fire, with a clear view from it back to the flames. (B02-016, B02-025, B02-129, B03-085, B04-082, B05-048, B05-098, B05-149, B05-169, B05-308, B06-051, B06-106, B06-200)
- **Inference.** A separate door leads to the girls' stair, close to the noticeboard: a boy thrown down the slide stopped at the feet of a friend standing there. Because: Harry is at the noticeboard, looks round at the door, and is not said to move before the slide delivers Ron to him. (B01-024, B01-086, B03-085, B04-020, B04-116, B05-048, B05-087, B05-088)

- **Explicit.** The fire serves as a remote link: a head can appear in the flames to talk, as in 1994 and 1995, and a searching hand reached through in October 1995. (B04-082, B05-071, B05-098)
- **Reported.** House-elves clear it overnight and can appear in it. From October 1995 only Dobby cleaned the Tower, the others refusing because of hats hidden around the room. (B05-049, B05-060, B05-104, B06-133)
- **Explicit.** Dated dressings include Dungbombs (1993), party food and banners (1994), practice clutter (1994), an enlarged talking magazine cover on a wall (1996) and careers leaflets (1996). (B02-040, B03-070, B04-090, B04-099, B05-136, B05-178, B05-225)

- The number of windows, and their compass bearing.

## Gryffindor boys’ dormitories

The boys' dormitories lie up a stone spiral stair behind a door in the common room, with doors opening off the stair. Only one room is described: the top one, at the head of the stair and of the Tower. It is a circular room with five four-poster beds set against the walls, one for each boy of Harry Potter's year. The beds have deep red velvet hangings, with a trunk at each foot and a bedside cabinet or table beside each. High narrow glazed windows look over the lawn, the Whomping Willow, the lakeside beech, Hagrid's cabin and the Forest edge. The same five boys kept this room from 1991 to at least 1996, while the year sign on the door changed. Lower rooms exist but are never seen.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** It holds five four-poster beds against the walls, for all five boys of one year. (B01-024, B02-001, B02-016, B03-013, B04-021, B07-136)
- **Estimate.** Allow at least about 7 m across inside. Because: Assumes beds 2.1 m long with heads to the wall and a 0.5 m trunk at each foot: 2 x 2.6 m, plus 1.8 m of central floor, is 7 m. (B04-021, B05-148)
- **Inference.** Rooms stay with a year group: the boys of 1991 were still in this room in 1996, and only the door sign changed. Because: The room recurs as their old, familiar room at the stair's head each year, with dormitories below it in 1996. (B01-024, B02-016, B03-013, B04-021, B05-217)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The described room stands at the head of the boys' spiral stair and at the top of the Tower. (B02-016, B02-108, B04-021)
- **Explicit.** One window is beside Harry's bed and another beside Neville's, which Harry can see from his own bed. Ron's bed is next to Harry's, on his right. (B05-027, B05-148, B05-183)
- **Explicit.** The windows overlook Forest treetops, the Whomping Willow and a lawn. The lakeside beech is directly ahead from Neville's window, with Hagrid's cabin and the Forest edge in view. (B03-104, B05-227)
- **Explicit.** Other dormitories lie below the top room: doors opened along the stair in 1994, and pupils were heard in rooms beneath it in 1996. (B02-119, B03-086, B05-217)
- **Explicit.** A shout from the upper rooms carries down the stair and silences the common room. (B03-168)
- **Reported.** Another dormitory also has a window on the grounds: a younger boy reports watching the lawn through one in June 1996. (B05-267)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** It is a circular room with a door from the stair, which carried a year sign reading second-years in 1992. (B01-039, B02-016, B03-013, B05-169)
- **Explicit.** The windows are high and narrow, glazed, curtained and rattled by storms. A boy can climb onto a sill, and an owl can get in. (B02-016, B02-086, B03-049, B03-104, B05-183, B06-052)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** A door off the common room opens on a stone spiral stair, cool and quiet, with walls tight enough for a running boy to hit. (B02-025, B02-129, B03-085, B05-048, B05-149, B05-169, B05-308, B06-051, B06-106, B06-135, B06-200)
- **Explicit.** The door and stair serve the boys of all years: a first-year in 1992 and a seventh-year in 1995 use them. (B02-164, B05-314)
- **Explicit.** Girls can enter these rooms, unlike the reverse. (B02-086, B05-088)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** The beds have canopies, posts and closable deep red velvet hangings, called crimson in Book 4. (B01-024, B04-021, B04-063, B05-060, B06-071, B06-106)
- **Explicit.** Each bed has a lockable trunk at its foot and a bedside cabinet or table with a drawer. Headboards and nearby walls take posters and rosettes. (B02-016, B02-102, B02-108, B03-103, B03-130, B04-021, B05-027)
- **Explicit.** The room also has wardrobes, a long mirror, and a silver water jug with a goblet under a window. (B01-076, B01-144, B03-104, B04-120, B05-183)
- **Explicit.** Light comes from a lamp, candles and the moon. No fireplace is mentioned. (B03-086, B05-028, B05-183)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Dated damage: Harry's belongings were ransacked in spring 1993, and one side of Ron's hangings was slashed away in February 1994. (B02-108, B03-086)

### Conflicts

- The room is circular, yet in December 1994 a long mirror stands in its corner. (B02-016, B04-120)

### Open questions

- The plan, bed count and door positions of every room other than the top one. Do not assume five beds elsewhere.
- Whether any bathroom lies inside the Tower. None is described, and the nearest named one is a boys' bathroom on the Room of Requirement corridor.

## Gryffindor girls’ dormitories

The girls' dormitories lie up a separate stone spiral stair behind their own door in the common room. No interior is ever described, because the narrative never enters them. The stair guards itself: when a boy reaches the sixth step an alarm sounds and the steps merge into a long smooth stone slide that drops him back into the common room. Girls of more than one year ride it down, so several rooms open off it. A window opens to the outside, since books summoned from the Headmaster's study flew straight in through it in 1997.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** Several rooms open off the stair, and the slide runs some distance. Because: Fourth-year girls and a fifth-year separately ride the slide down, and on an alarm girls come down their stair in nightclothes. (B03-085, B05-088)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** If they share the Tower, no girls' room stands higher than the top boys' room, which is at the top of the Tower. Because: The top boys' room is placed at the top of the Tower. Two turrets could stand level, but the text mentions neither. (B04-021)
- **Inference.** Hermione's first-year room may be low on the stair: she fetched a book from it in moments in early 1992. Because: The boys barely exchange a look before she is back. This is narrative pace, so a weak hint. (B01-086)
- **Explicit.** The Marauder's Map marks at least one girls' dormitory by name. (B07-022)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** The stair is a stone spiral. (B03-085, B05-088)
- **Reported.** A dormitory window opens to the outside: in June 1997 books summoned from the Headmaster's study flew in through it. (B07-005)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** A door of its own off the common room, separate from the boys', leads to the girls' stair and dormitories. (B01-024, B04-020, B04-116, B05-048, B06-203)
- **Reported.** Hermione attributes the rule to the founders, who she says trusted boys less. Girls may enter the boys' rooms. (B05-088)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** When a boy reaches the sixth step an alarm wails and the steps merge into a long smooth stone slide, which ends on a rug in the common room. (B05-088)

### Open questions

- Everything inside: room shape, bed count, furnishings and how many doors open off the stair. Do not copy the boys' top room without flagging it as assumed.
