# Library and Restricted Section

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/library.md)

The school library and its Restricted Section. The books give its size in three counts: hundreds of narrow rows, thousands of shelves and tens of thousands of books. It is reached up the marble staircase and on through corridors, with no stated floor, though the best clue points to about the fourth. One door opens from a corridor. Inside, reading tables and lamps stand by mullioned windows and among the shelves, and the rows run back to the Restricted Section, behind a rope at the very end. At an assumed narrow 1.5 m per row, even 200 rows need 300 m of row spacing and at least about 1,500 m² of floor, as one great hall or a suite of rooms. This is far larger than an ordinary room. Read literally, the counts also keep the collection under 100,000 books, which suits rows about 3 to 4 m long.

## Library

The school library, a very large upstairs collection entered by a door from a corridor. No floor is stated. Routes climb the marble staircase and go on, it lies above McGonagall's first-floor study, and Harry's guess of five floors above the kitchens puts a nearby corridor at about the fourth floor. The books run to the tens of thousands, the shelves to the thousands and the narrow rows to the hundreds. At an assumed 1.5 m per row, 200 rows need 300 m of row spacing, so this is a hall of at least about 1,500 m² or a suite of rooms, not one ordinary room. Read literally, the same counts keep the collection under 100,000 books and 10,000 shelves, which suits rows about 3 to 4 m long holding thick volumes. Shelves are tall enough to hide people. Tables with lamps stand by mullioned windows and among the shelves at the back. The Restricted Section lies at the far back behind a rope. The library closes in the evening, and its librarian polices noise and food.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The books number in the tens of thousands, the shelves in the thousands and the narrow rows in the hundreds. (B01-073)
- **Inference.** Limits for any model: 20,000 to 99,999 books, 2,000 to 9,999 shelves, 200 to 999 rows. Because: Each phrase is a plural of its unit, so it means at least two and fewer than ten: tens of thousands stops short of 100,000, thousands short of 10,000, hundreds short of 1,000. (B01-073)
- **Estimate.** Row spacing alone comes to at least 300 m, far beyond any one ordinary room. Because: At an assumed narrow 1.5 m per row (0.9 m walkway plus a 0.6 m double-sided case), 200 rows × 1.5 m = 300 m. Even at a very tight 1.2 m it is 240 m. At 300 rows it is 450 m. (B01-073)
- **Estimate.** The whole library needs at least about 1,500 m²: for example one hall about 50 m by 30 m, or three rooms 50 m by 10 m. Because: 200 rows × 1.5 m × 3 to 4 m = 900 to 1,200 m² of shelving floor. Folded into six banks of 33 rows with 2 m cross-aisles: 50 m deep and 28 to 34 m wide. Tables and the Restricted Section are extra. Check: a 3-minute fetch at 1.2 m/s is 216 m; if half goes on finding the book, about 50 m each way. A minimum, not a measurement. (B01-073, B02-166)
- **Estimate.** The counts allow 2 to 50 books per shelf on average; at a realistic 25 to 30, only about 3,300 to 4,000 shelves fit under the book limit. Because: 20,000 ÷ 9,999 = 2 and 99,999 ÷ 2,000 = 50. Taking one shelf as one 1 m board of ordinary books: 99,999 ÷ 30 = 3,333 and 99,999 ÷ 25 = 4,000. (B01-073)
- **Estimate.** Rows cannot be long: about 4 m is the limit at six shelves high with shelving both sides, or 200 rows pass 10,000 shelves. Because: Each metre of such a row adds 2 × 6 = 12 shelves (1 m shelf assumed). 200 rows × 4 m × 12 = 9,600 shelves, just under the ceiling. With shelves on one side only the limit doubles to about 8 m. The counts allow 2 to 50 shelves per row in all (2,000 ÷ 999 and 9,999 ÷ 200). (B01-073)
- **Estimate.** Rows 3 to 4 m long fit all three counts only at about 10 to 14 books per shelf, so stock must be thick volumes or shelves part-empty. Because: 200 rows × 3 to 4 m × 12 shelves per metre = 7,200 to 9,600 shelves. 99,999 ÷ 9,600 = 10.4 and 99,999 ÷ 7,200 = 13.9. At 30 books a shelf those rows would hold 216,000 to 288,000 books, over twice the ceiling. The text does show large, heavy volumes (B01-080, B02-061). (B01-073, B01-080, B02-061)
- **Estimate.** Long full rows overshoot badly: 200 rows 20 m long would hold about 1.4 million books. Because: 200 × 20 m × 12 shelves per metre = 48,000 shelves, almost five times the ceiling. At 30 books each that is 1.44 million, 14 times the ceiling. (B01-073)
- **Estimate.** Rows are at least about 3 m long, since six or seven girls lurk together in one. Because: Single file in a narrow row at an assumed 0.5 m each: 6 to 7 × 0.5 m = 3 to 3.5 m. (B04-105)
- **Explicit.** Shelving at the back runs in long, tall ranks, and elsewhere the shelves are called lofty. (B02-079, B02-061)
- **Explicit.** Fetching one restricted book took the librarian several minutes. (B02-166)
- **Inference.** One reading area holds at least sixteen people in sight of one another. Because: Fourteen witnesses saw a pupil jinxed from behind as she worked: fourteen, plus the victim and her attacker (B05-120). (B05-120)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** No floor is stated; the best single clue is about the fourth floor. Because: Just after fleeing the library, with no stairs narrated, Harry reckons he is five floors over the kitchens (B01-081). The kitchens lie one flight below the Entrance Hall (B04-106), so five floors up is the fourth floor. It is a panicked guess about a corridor, not the library itself. (B01-081, B04-106)
- **Reported.** It lies above McGonagall's first-floor study: pupils are taken down from it to her. (B04-155, B01-103)
- **Explicit.** It is reached up the marble staircase and on through corridors, and left by corridor and stairs down to the Entrance Hall. (B02-109, B05-163, B04-158)
- **Explicit.** It is about five minutes' walk from Myrtle's bathroom and within fifteen of Gryffindor, and Harry judges it nearer the Room of Requirement than the dormitories. (B02-062, B04-152, B05-198)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Windows are mullioned and show open sky, and one faces a reader seated at a table. (B01-092, B05-168)
- **Inference.** Shelves stand taller than the pupils, who spy and listen unseen behind them, and books rise above seated readers. Because: Pupils repeatedly hide behind shelves while standing or moving about (B02-079, B04-073, B04-105), someone stays unseen close to a table until the librarian rounds a corner (B06-079), and Ron looks upward at the books around him (NEW-inner-5). (B02-079, B04-073, B04-105, B06-079, B01-149)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The only entrance mentioned is one door from a corridor. (B01-073, B01-080, B04-152)
- **Explicit.** It shuts at night, with the lamps out by eight in February 1995, yet Harry twice walked in after hours and no lock is described. (B01-079, B04-152, B06-079)
- **Explicit.** Talk is kept to whispers and food is banned; in 1996 enchanted books drove two offenders out. (B06-079, B05-224)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Tables and desks stand near windows and deep among the shelves at the back, and the librarian stamps loans several shelves from them. (B01-092, B02-079, B04-152, B05-168, B05-224)
- **Explicit.** Lamps stand at the tables and candles can be moved about; at night it is pitch dark and needs a carried lamp or wand. (B01-079, B04-152, B05-168, B06-079)
- **Explicit.** An Invisibility section stands towards the back, and Hermione reports a whole collection of old Daily Prophets. (B02-079, B06-163)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** Dumbledore is reported to have taken every book on Horcruxes off the shelves at some unknown date. His private library went to Hogwarts in 1997; nothing says it joined this collection. (B07-004, B07-007)

### Conflicts

- Shelving at the back reads as long ranks (B02-079), while the counts cap a row at about 4 m (B01-073, by the estimates above). The two fit if short rows stand end to end across cross-aisles, making lines of 30 m or more; the text does not say so. (B02-079, B01-073)

### Open questions

- One great room or a suite: the text mentions only a back, corners and one door.
- Whether a shelf means one board, as assumed here, or a whole bookcase. As bookcases six boards high, even 2,000 could hold only about 8 books per board, so the board reading fits better.
- How the height of tall shelves is reached: ladders and upper galleries are not mentioned.
- Whether a row means a walkway lined on both sides, as assumed here (Ron walks down one and girls lurk in one), or a single face of shelving.

## Restricted Section

The restricted part of the library, at its very back, marked off from the rest only by a rope low enough to step over. It holds books of powerful Dark Magic, and a signed note from a teacher is needed to read or borrow them. The bar is a rule, not a lock: no magical barrier is described. Its size is not given, but the librarian needed several minutes to fetch a book from it and one pupil claims to have gone right through it. Volumes include old works with peeling gilt titles in unknown languages, some with no title, and large heavy books that cry out when handled.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** A fetch from it took the librarian several minutes, so it stands well away from where she took the note. Because: She walks off among the tall shelves and is gone several minutes (NEW-inner-1), though some of that time went on finding the book. (B02-166, B02-061)
- **Inference.** It is much smaller than the main stacks, since one pupil reports going right through it. Because: Hermione claims to have been through all of it for one subject (B06-101), whereas three pupils searching the open shelves for a fortnight made little headway (B01-073). (B06-101, B01-073)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It lies at the very back of the library. (B01-079)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Only a rope separates it from the other shelves, low enough to step over. (B01-079)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Reading or borrowing needs a teacher's signed note, which the librarian inspects before fetching the book herself. (B01-074, B02-060, B02-061, B04-152)
- **Inference.** The bar is a rule, not a lock. Because: Harry simply stepped over the rope at night and took a book down (B01-079). The alarm was the book itself (B01-080). (B01-079, B01-080)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** It holds books of powerful Dark Magic, read by older pupils in advanced Defence. (B01-074)
- **Explicit.** Titles are in peeling, faded gold, in languages Harry cannot read, or absent; one book has a stain resembling blood. (B01-079)
- **Explicit.** Known volumes include a heavy black-and-silver book on a bottom shelf and the large, mouldy Moste Potente Potions. (B01-080, B02-061)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Some books react aloud: one screams without stopping when opened, another wails when slammed shut. (B01-080, B06-101)
- **Explicit.** At night its shelves are unlit, and a reader must carry a lamp in. (B01-079)
