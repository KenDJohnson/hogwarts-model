# Great Hall and adjoining rooms

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/great-hall.md)

The Great Hall is the castle's main dining and assembly room, on the ground floor, with the kitchens directly beneath it and matching it in size. Thousands of floating candles light four long house tables, which run up the room from the double doors to a staff table on a raised platform. It has to hold everyone at once: about a hundred tables of about a dozen people each stood here at the 1994 ball, more than a hundred exam tables in another year, and the whole school has slept on its floor. Behind the staff table, at the end where Hagrid sits, a door leads to a smaller room with paintings, a fire and a low chandelier; other small chambers beside the Hall are mentioned, but the text does not say they are that room. As a modelling minimum rather than a figure from the books, the Hall needs at least 1,200 m² of floor.

## Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 1,200 m² for the Great Hall, plus its side chambers. Because: From the Great Hall overview: about 100 tables × about 12 seats × an assumed 1 m² per seat, checked against a span of fifty yards (46 m). (B04-124, B07-129)

## Where it is

- **Inference.** The Hall and the kitchens form a two-level block of matching plan, the kitchens’ four tables lying exactly under the four house tables. Because: The kitchens are stated to be as large as the Hall above them, and their tables are placed exactly beneath the house tables, so the two plans have to line up. (B04-108, B04-109)
- **Inference.** The side chamber door is behind the staff table at Hagrid’s end, opposite the Hall’s main doors at the back of the room. Because: The main doors open at the back of the Hall on the Slytherin side, while the side door is at the top, at the end nearest Gryffindor, so the two lie at opposite corners. (B04-058, B04-057, B04-008, B01-017)

## Great Hall

The Great Hall is the school’s main dining and assembly room, on the ground floor to the right of the Entrance Hall, with the kitchens directly beneath it. It is a stone room with high windows and an enchanted ceiling that shows the sky. Four long house tables with benches run up the room to a fifth, the staff table, on a raised platform along the top wall. The books never state its dimensions, but every clue points to a very large room: about a hundred dining tables, roughly 1,200 seats, stood in it at once for the 1994 ball, the whole school has slept on its floor, more than a hundred exam desks have filled it, and two duels have been fought fifty yards apart inside it. As a modelling minimum, not a figure from the books, allow at least 1,200 m² of floor, roughly 50 m by 24 m, and at least 12 m to the ceiling. All the furniture is movable, and the room is repeatedly cleared or rearranged for exams, duelling, Apparition lessons and the 1998 battle.

### Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 1,200 m² of floor (about 13,000 sq ft), for example 50 m by 24 m. Anything much smaller fails several scenes at once. Because: No dimensions are stated; all spacing here is assumed. Seats: about 100 tables × about 12 seats = about 1,200 seats; at an assumed 1 m² per seated diner including aisles, 1,200 m². Grid check: a 1.8 m table, 0.5 m of chairs each side and a 0.6 m aisle makes a 3.4 m grid, and 100 × 3.4 m × 3.4 m = 1,156 m². Length: fifty yards is 46 m between two duellists; an assumed 2 m each end for the crowd along the walls gives 50 m, and 1,200 ÷ 50 = 24 m wide. Width check: four tables at 3 m with benches and diners, plus five 1.6 m aisles, is 20 m, which fits. Sleeping check: 500 to 700 pupils × 1.6 m² per bag (2.0 m by 0.8 m) = 800 to 1,120 m² of bags alone, before walkways and the long tables at the walls. Bench check: 700 pupils ÷ 8 bench sides × 0.5 m each = 44 m of table, which fits a 50 m room. Exam desks (100+ at an assumed 3 m² = 300+ m²) and Apparition spacing (100 pupils on an assumed 2 m grid, five clear feet plus a body, = 400 m²) need less, so they do not set the limit. (B04-124, B07-129, B03-042, B06-018, B05-219, B06-103)
- **Inference.** The Hall must hold the whole school at one sitting, about 500 to 700 pupils plus staff, and must also physically take the roughly 1,200 seats laid out for the 1994 ball. Because: No head-count is ever stated. Coaches: about 100 carry everyone above first year; the observed loads of 4 (1994) and 5 (1995) give 400 to 500 pupils in six years, so about 470 to 580 in seven. Exam desks: more than 100 desks × 7 years gives 700 or more if they held one year group, though in 1996 fifth- and seventh-years were called in together, which could halve that. The ball’s 1,200 counts seats, not people: only fourth-years and above plus invited partners could come, so on these figures at most about 800 attended. Size the room for the largest stated furniture load, because a big room can be under-filled but a small one cannot take the tables. (B05-008, B04-004, B05-012, B05-219, B05-256, B04-124, B04-112)
- **Explicit.** Yule Ball, 1994: about a hundred small lantern-lit tables of about twelve seats each stood in the Hall at once, with a large round table for the judges at the top. (B04-124)
- **Explicit.** O.W.L. exams: more than a hundred one-person desks, all facing the same way with aisles between them (Snape’s year, about 1976). The same layout was used in 1996. (B05-219, B05-256)
- **Explicit.** Hallowe’en night 1993: all four houses slept here together, with hundreds of sleeping bags across the floor and the long tables stood against the walls. (B03-042)
- **Explicit.** May 1998: two duels ran fifty yards apart inside the Hall, while hundreds of people lined the walls and everyone still on their feet pushed in. (B07-129)
- **Explicit.** February 1997 Apparition lesson: tables removed, each sixth-year stood with five clear feet ahead and a wooden hoop on the floor. The anti-Apparition enchantment was lifted inside the Hall only. (B06-103, B06-104)
- **Explicit.** Duelling Club, December 1992: with the dining tables gone, the bulk of the school crowded in standing, then paired off across the floor. (B02-076)
- **Explicit.** Narration calls the room huge and the seated school hundreds; in 1994 the two visiting schools added only some twenty. Harry’s walk up to the staff table seemed endless. (B02-017, B01-013, B06-018, B04-058, B04-250)
- **Explicit.** Floating candles number in the thousands on feast nights and at the Duelling Club; Book 4 gives hundreds and hundreds. (B01-013, B02-076, B03-011, B04-008)
- **Explicit.** Owl post arrives in one flock: about a hundred owls in Books 1 and 4, hundreds in Book 5, circling above the tables. (B01-033, B04-022, B05-031)
- **Explicit.** Twelve Christmas trees, called towering, stand around the room every year; one tree alone could hide Hagrid in a corridor. (B01-071, B01-072, B02-087, B03-073, B04-114, B06-077, B07-024)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 12 m from floor to ceiling. The text gives no height and never describes the real roof. Because: A fir that can hide Hagrid (about twice normal height, so about 3.5 m) and is called towering once stood up is likely 6 m or more. Grawp, at about 16 ft (4.9 m), can put his face to one window from outside, while the windows in general are called high or upper, so their heads must stand well above him: allow about 10 m, plus 2 m above the window heads to the ceiling. A room 50 m long under a low ceiling would contradict all of this. (B01-071, B01-072, B04-012, B07-131, B05-253, B05-016, B05-031, B06-025)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** On the ground floor, entered through double doors on the right of the Entrance Hall as you come in from the front doors. The kitchens lie directly beneath it. (B01-011, B03-009, B04-007, B05-015, B04-108, B04-109)
- **Inference.** The doors open at the back of the Hall on its Slytherin side: you pass the other tables to reach Gryffindor, the table furthest from the Entrance Hall. Because: In 1994 the walk in passes Slytherin, Ravenclaw and Hufflepuff before Gryffindor. In 1993 Harry skirts the rear of the Hall to get to his table. Book 6 calls Gryffindor the furthest table. One Gryffindor bench faces the doorway and the other has its back to it, which could not happen if the doors lay on the line of the tables. (B04-008, B03-011, B06-018, B04-046, B05-255)
- **Explicit.** From outside, a lit window near the foot of the front steps looks into the Hall across the tables as far as the staff table. (B02-010)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Stone walls and floor, a raised platform across the top end, winter fires, and high mullioned windows that open for owls and catch the dawn sun (May 1998). (B02-017, B05-256, B07-129, B07-066, B07-103, B01-070, B05-016, B05-031, B06-025, B04-022, B06-042, B07-130)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Double doors from the Entrance Hall give a view out from the benches. A door behind the staff table at Hagrid’s end leads to a side chamber. The text mentions further entrances in 1993. (B01-011, B05-255, B04-012, B04-057, B04-058, B03-042)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Four long house tables with benches run up the room side by side, in the order Slytherin, Ravenclaw, Hufflepuff, Gryffindor, with aisles between, laid with gold plates and goblets. (B01-013, B04-008, B04-057, B04-058, B05-023, B05-138, B06-018, B07-066, B07-131)
- **Explicit.** The staff table (the High Table) runs along the top wall, staff on one side facing the pupils. The Headmaster’s high-backed gold chair is central; Hagrid’s huge chair is at one end. (B01-017, B04-008, B04-009, B05-017, B06-041, B06-204)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** The ceiling shows the sky outside, stars, cloud or storm, though a real one exists: a Howler shook dust from it. Food appears and clears itself unseen, by spoken order at the 1994 ball. (B01-014, B01-015, B02-017, B04-010, B05-016, B05-312, B06-025, B01-018, B04-014, B04-125, B05-019, B06-020)
- **Explicit.** At each start of term a stool and the Sorting Hat stand before the staff table, with first-years lined up facing the school; both are removed afterwards. (B01-016, B03-011, B04-011, B05-018, B07-107)
- **Explicit.** Furniture moves by magic: tables vanish or line the walls. One table served twelve at Christmas 1993; a stage stood on one wall in 1992, a music platform on the right-hand wall in 1994. (B02-076, B03-042, B03-074, B04-124, B04-128, B05-256, B06-103, B07-131)
- **Explicit.** Decorations follow the calendar: 2,000 live bats (1991), hundreds of pumpkins, some able to hold three men, house-colour hangings, huge silk banners (1994), black mourning drapes (1995), Valentine flowers (1993). (B01-056, B02-041, B03-039, B04-051, B01-143, B03-163, B04-039, B04-238, B02-101)
- **Explicit.** Battle of Hogwarts, May 1998: house tables removed, the injured treated on the platform, the dead laid in a row down the middle, the floor cracked, a window smashed; tables later restored. (B07-103, B07-129, B07-131)

### Conflicts

- House-table order. Book 1 has Ravenclaw second from the left and Hufflepuff on the right, seen from the top of the Hall with Gryffindor far left. From Book 4 on, Hufflepuff sits next to Gryffindor with Ravenclaw beyond it. The later order recurs in at least four scenes. (B01-016, B04-008, B04-057, B04-058, B05-023, B05-138)
- School size. Two-house classes of twenty imply about 40 pupils a year and about 280 in all. About 100 coaches imply about 500, more than 100 desks up to 700, and 1,200 seats for the top four years alone imply more. These cannot all be right; the room must fit the largest. (B01-041, B02-073, B05-008, B05-219, B04-124, B04-112)
- The Sorting stool has four legs in Book 1 and three in Books 3 and 4. (B01-016, B03-011, B04-011)

### Open questions

- Exact door position along the back of the Hall, and how that wall relates to the Entrance Hall’s right-hand wall.
- Window heights. Most are called high, yet boys on the ground near the front steps can see in (1992) and Grawp’s face reaches one (1998). Decide sill heights and how the ground falls along that wall.
- Which walls face the castle front and the sunrise. The text gives no compass bearing for the Hall.
- Whether the raised platform is a permanent feature. It is only named in Book 7.

## Chambers beside the Great Hall

A group of small rooms next to the Great Hall, which the text does not say are one room. The best described is the champions’ room, through the door behind the staff table at Hagrid’s end: smaller than the Hall, hung with paintings, with a fire facing the door and a low candle chandelier. Fifth-years waited for practical O.W.L.s in a small chamber beside the Hall in 1996, and Voldemort’s body was laid in a chamber off the Hall in 1998; either may or may not be that same room.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** It is smaller than the Hall, yet held at least eleven people in 1994, and twelve in June 1995 in separate family groups. (B04-058, B04-206)
- **Explicit.** A chandelier of candles hangs low enough that Madame Maxime, at full height, brushed it with her head. She is not measured, so this gives only a relative clearance. (B04-059)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: about 50 m² if a full year group must wait in the 1996 chamber, despite its being called small. Because: A year group of up to about 100 (see the Great Hall head-count) standing at an assumed 2 people per m² = 50 m². On the smaller class-based figure of 40 pupils it would be 20 m². (B05-256, B05-257)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The champions’ room is reached through the door behind the staff table, at the end where Hagrid sits. (B04-057, B04-058)
- **Explicit.** In 1996 the fifth-years waited for practical O.W.L.s in a small chamber beside the Hall, went straight into the Hall when called, and did not come back. (B05-256, B05-257)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Paintings of witches and wizards line the walls, a roaring fire with a mantelpiece faces the door, and parts of the room stay in shadow beyond the firelight. (B04-059)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The door shuts out the noise of the Hall; the voices beyond carry only while it stands open. (B04-058)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Violet’s portrait hangs here next to that of a walrus-moustached wizard. Harry reckons she ran through pictures on seven staircases to reach the Fat Lady’s frame. (B04-060, B04-206)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** May 1998: Voldemort’s body was laid in a chamber off the Hall, apart from the fallen defenders. (B07-131)

### Open questions

- Whether the 1996 waiting chamber and the 1998 chamber are the champions’ room or separate rooms.
