# Hagrid's cabin, garden and nearby installations

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/hagrid.md)

Walking down the sloping lawns from the castle for about five minutes brings you to Hagrid’s cabin, a one-room wooden house on the Forest edge, built for a man twice normal height. Before it lies open lawn used for lessons and, in spring 1995, a dug plot. Behind it, through the back door, is the fenced garden and pumpkin patch, ending only yards from the first trees, with Aragog’s grave mound beyond the patch from 1997. Two hundred yards from the front door, from October 1994 to June 1995, stood the house-sized Beauxbatons carriage with its paddock for twelve elephant-sized horses, and trees begin a short way past that paddock. Care of Magical Creatures uses all this ground and more: crates by the cabin, trestle tables ten yards from the door and behind the walls, the Forest-edge lawn, and a separate fenced paddock five minutes’ walk round the trees. No stable, barn or classroom building is ever described here.

## Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** The carriage stood about half as far from the cabin as the cabin stands from the castle front doors. Because: Two hundred yards is about 180 m, against 330 to 420 m for a five-minute walk. (B04-052, B01-155)
- **Inference.** Everything here runs oversized against pupils, so ordinary cottage and garden proportions will read far too small. Because: The householder is twice normal height, the visiting carriage is house-sized with elephant-sized horses, and the pumpkins reached garden-shed size. (B01-154, B03-169, B04-251, B05-316, B04-043, B02-035)

## Where it is

- **Inference.** Order from the castle: sloping lawn, cabin front, cabin, back garden, first trees. Because: Every approach descends the lawn to the front door, the garden lies behind, and the rescue of Buckbeak runs from the patch straight to the trees. (B03-020, B03-115, B03-145, B04-024)
- **Explicit.** Lawn links the area to the stadium; the school vegetable patch is a detour from the direct way down and leads on to the greenhouses. (B05-242, B04-187, B06-137, B05-053)

## Shape and structure

- **Inference.** Animals here are tethered, crated or fenced in the open; no stable or barn is described. Because: Hippogriffs, Skrewts, horses and Buckbeak are all kept on chains, in crates, in paddocks or at tethers outdoors. (B03-021, B04-067, B04-052, B06-046)

## Getting there

- **Inference.** About five minutes on foot from the front doors to the cabin, and about five more round the trees to the 1993 paddock. Because: The paddock walk is timed in the text; the castle walk rests on Harry leaving at five to three for tea at about three. (B01-155, B03-020)

## Open questions

- Hagrid’s hen-coop exists (B02-080) but has no stated position.

## Hagrid’s cabin

Hagrid’s cabin is a one-room wooden house where the sloping lawns below the castle meet the Forbidden Forest, roughly five minutes’ walk from the front doors. The books call it small, but its owner stands about double normal height and three or more times normal breadth, and everything in it is built for him, so it must be far larger than an ordinary hut. The single room has an open fire in one corner, a gigantic patchwork-covered bed in another, an enormous central table with chairs, and a wardrobe, dresser, chest of drawers and cupboards. Hams, birds and pots hang from a ceiling his head can just brush. A wooden front door with bolt and keyhole faces the lawns; a back door opens into the fenced pumpkin garden, which ends only yards from the first trees. There is a chimney, curtained glass windows and a water barrel outside. It has held a Hippogriff, six raiders at once and most of a class. Fire wrecked it in June 1997, yet it stands in May 1998. The roof is never described.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Hagrid stands about double a normal man’s height and three or more times his breadth; other passages simply say twice normal size. (B01-154, B03-169, B04-251, B05-316, B03-072, B05-130)
- **Explicit.** Against people: Umbridge barely reaches his navel, Harry and Slughorn only his elbow; bent double he tops a stand by four feet; a photograph shows him seven or eight feet tall at eleven. (B02-169, B05-317, B06-219, B04-138)
- **Explicit.** When he jumps up from his seat his head brushes the ceiling. (B02-114)
- **Estimate.** Give the room at least 3.4 m (11 ft) of clear height, about one and a half times a normal cottage’s. Because: Twice a 1.7 m man is 3.4 m, and his elbow would then sit near 2.1 m, within Harry’s reach. He grazes the ceiling only on leaping up, so it sits close to his height. A cottage room is about 2.3 m. (B01-154, B03-169, B04-251, B05-316, B02-169, B05-317, B06-219, B02-114)
- **Estimate.** Make both doors at least 1.4 m wide and about 3 m high; a normal door would not pass him. Because: Three times a 0.45 m shoulder is 1.35 m. At 3.4 m tall he needs about 3 m with a slight stoop. No passage has him forcing his way through here, and a Hippogriff has gone through one door at a canter. (B01-154, B03-169, B04-251, B05-316, B03-149, B05-129)
- **Explicit.** Capacity: most of a two-house class shelters inside at once (1994); six raiders enter with Hagrid and Fang within (1996); a Hippogriff lives indoors in winter 1993–94, on bed and floor. (B04-103, B05-264, B03-072, B03-092, B03-149)
- **Estimate.** Allow a rectangular floor of at least 8 m by 6 m, despite the word small. Because: Umbridge walks the cabin’s length, and corners hold bed, fire and barrel. A 3.7 m bed, a central 3 m by 1.5 m table with chairs and a 1 m walk all round (about 6.5 m by 5 m), plus hearth, wardrobe and dresser, need no less. (B05-133, B05-131, B04-054, B06-047, B02-029, B06-048)
- **Estimate.** Site the cabin at least about 350 m from the front steps. Because: Five minutes at 4 to 5 km/h is 330 to 420 m. Cabin and castle steps are each called distant from the other, and Fang looks tiny from the Astronomy Tower. (B01-155, B03-114, B05-264, B06-137)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The cabin stands on the edge of the Forest at the foot of the sloping lawns: every visit goes downhill from the castle and every return climbs. (B01-037, B02-001, B01-096, B03-020, B04-024, B02-031, B04-056, B05-014, B03-024, B03-071, B03-091, B03-113, B03-116, B05-134, B05-196, B01-100, B02-129, B03-117, B04-077)
- **Inference.** Its front looks towards the lawns and castle approach; the back garden lies towards the trees. Because: Officials walking down from the castle reach the front while pupils leave unseen by the back, and a rear window overlooks the patch beside the trees. No compass bearing is given. (B03-115, B03-114, B03-144, B03-145, B05-243)
- **Inference.** From the castle front doors the walk takes roughly five minutes; Harry allows about half an hour from Gryffindor Tower under the Cloak. Because: Harry and Ron leave the castle at five to three for tea at about three. Officials seen on the steps knock soon afterwards. Harry sets out at half past eleven for a midnight meeting. (B01-155, B04-253, B03-115, B03-143)
- **Explicit.** It is in view from Gryffindor common-room and dormitory windows, the Owlery, the top of the Astronomy Tower and corridor windows; one cabin window shows the far-off castle steps. (B03-024, B05-227, B04-180, B05-065, B05-263, B05-045, B05-090, B06-090, B03-114)
- **Explicit.** It is Harry’s closest refuge from the stadium; Hagrid says he watched a match from it by binoculars. The lake is a run from its door, and people heading for the gates pass nearby. (B02-028, B05-242, B01-067, B03-150, B06-188, B05-265, B04-135, B04-187, B06-045)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** A wooden house of one room, called small, little or a hut throughout. (B01-037, B01-038, B02-029, B04-024, B04-054, B02-114, B01-002)
- **Explicit.** The front door is wood with a bolt and a keyhole; a back door opens straight into the garden, and people can walk right round the building. (B05-129, B03-149, B03-115, B03-116, B04-103, B05-304, B06-046)
- **Explicit.** Windows are glazed, curtained and can open: one beside the front door, one with the water barrel outside it and a view of the carriage, and one at the rear over the garden. (B05-132, B04-055, B06-139, B03-144, B04-103, B01-098, B01-093, B01-097, B01-107, B04-134, B05-134)
- **Explicit.** Outside stand a chimney, a water barrel, a bush some twenty feet off, a crossbow and galoshes by the door, in summer an armchair, and from 1996 Buckbeak on a tether in front. (B05-065, B05-129, B05-227, B04-180, B03-025, B04-055, B01-037, B01-118, B02-028, B06-003, B06-046, B06-090)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** A gigantic bed under a patchwork quilt stands in one corner, with space to peer underneath; a Hippogriff with folded wings has lain on it. (B01-038, B02-029, B04-054, B05-133, B03-092)
- **Explicit.** An open fire with a grate and mantelpiece occupies another corner, heating a copper kettle. (B01-038, B02-029, B01-093, B02-121, B06-047)
- **Explicit.** An enormous scrubbed wooden table, which once held a dragon steak bigger than a car tyre, stands mid-room before the fire, with equally huge chairs and a walkway round it. (B04-054, B05-131, B02-029, B03-025, B06-047, B06-048)
- **Explicit.** Storage comprises a wardrobe, a chest of drawers beside the bed, a dresser with drawers and cups, and more than one cupboard. (B03-092, B04-054, B04-138, B04-237, B03-114, B05-133)
- **Explicit.** Cured hams and dead birds hang from the ceiling over the table; by 1997 brass pots and a long skein of unicorn hair hang there too. (B01-038, B04-054, B06-143)
- **Explicit.** Household goods match him: bucket-sized mugs and cups, an enormous kettle and cooking cauldron, Fang’s cushioned basket, a haversack fit for several small children, and in 1996 a large barrel of foot-long grubs. (B03-025, B04-237, B05-130, B06-047, B06-142, B05-133, B02-114, B05-132, B06-048)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Shut up and dark in January 1995 and from September to November 1995, raided at night in June 1996 when its door burst open, and closed to pupils’ evening visits in 1994 and 1996. (B05-014, B05-065, B05-264, B03-024, B03-099, B05-173, B04-134)
- **Explicit.** A spell sets it alight in June 1997; water jets leave a smoking wreck. It is reported in use by spring 1998 and still stands that May, with no rebuilding described. (B06-189, B07-027, B07-118)

### Conflicts

- Breadth differs: five or more times normal width in Book 1, three or more times normal breadth in Book 4. Use the smaller figure as a minimum. (B01-154, B03-169, B04-251, B05-316)
- Height readings differ: twice a normal man implies about 3.4 m, but head and shoulders above a street crowd, or Umbridge at his navel, read closer to 2.2 to 2.6 m. (B01-154, B03-169, B04-251, B05-316, B02-169, B05-317, B06-219)

### Open questions

- Roof form and covering are never described; the only fixed points are a chimney and a ceiling that clears Hagrid.
- The number of windows and the fire’s corner relative to the doors are unstated.

## Hagrid’s garden and pumpkin patch

Hagrid’s garden is the fenced plot behind his cabin, entered by the back door and overlooked by the rear window, running towards the first trees of the Forest. It holds the pumpkin patch, runner beans and a compost heap, and from spring 1997 Aragog’s grave mound just beyond the patch. Its pumpkins reached boulder size and then shed size in 1992, so even a plot called small needs real ground.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** In 1992 the plot, called small, held about a dozen pumpkins the size of large boulders; rain and magical help swelled them to garden-shed size by October. (B02-030, B02-035)
- **Explicit.** Carved for that Hallowe’en, they became lanterns that three men could sit inside. (B02-041)
- **Estimate.** Allow at least about 7 m by 10 m for the pumpkin bed. Because: Twelve pumpkins 2 m across with 0.5 m gaps, in a three-by-four grid, take 7 m by 9.5 m. (B02-030, B02-035)
- **Explicit.** Aragog’s grave, dug in spring 1997 just beyond the pumpkin patch, was a massive pit with dug earth heaped ten feet high beside it; the ten feet is the spoil, not the spider. (B06-139, B06-140)
- **Estimate.** Allow a pit of at least about 50 m³, for example 5 m by 4 m and 2.5 m deep. Because: A conical heap 3 m high and 8 m across holds about 50 m³. (B06-140)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The garden lies behind the cabin, reached through the back door and overlooked by the rear window. (B02-030, B03-115, B05-304, B06-139)
- **Explicit.** In June 1994 a spot ten feet short of the Forest was still in plain view of the back door, and a tether lay a few yards from that door. (B03-115, B03-145)
- **Inference.** The patch’s Forest side lies only yards from the first trees, close enough for sounds at the back door to carry into the wood. Because: Harry darts from the trees to vault the fence, and is soon only ten feet from the Forest while leading Buckbeak away at a walk. (B03-143, B03-144, B03-145)
- **Reported.** A 1997 remark calls the ground before the cabin his front garden, apparently the plot dug for the Niffler lesson. (B07-003, B04-231, B04-181)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** A fence surrounds the patch, low enough to vault and to lean on; a Hippogriff’s rope has been tied to it, and an axe was apparently swung into it in 1994. (B03-144, B04-103, B05-304, B03-146, B03-116)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Crops include pumpkins and runner beans; a compost heap stands near the grave site, and at least one tree grows behind the patch. (B02-030, B05-304, B06-141, B03-115)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Blast-Ended Skrewts were boxed here: about twenty nearly three feet long in October 1994, then ten nearly six feet long, which burst enormous boxes that December and withered plants. (B04-053, B04-102)
- **Explicit.** Once filled, the grave remains as a smooth mound of earth in the back garden from 1997. (B06-140)

### Conflicts

- Buckbeak’s tether in June 1994 is a tree behind the patch in one chapter and the patch fence in another. (B03-115, B03-144)
- That tether lies only a few yards from the back door, beyond the patch, which leaves little depth for a bed that held a dozen shed-sized pumpkins in 1992. (B03-115, B02-035)

### Open questions

- The garden’s overall size and fence line are unstated; keep the patch, then the grave, then the first trees in that order from the back door.

## Outdoor creature-lesson grounds

These are the outdoor sites used for Care of Magical Creatures, not one place. There is no classroom or stable: lessons for two houses together happen on open ground around Hagrid’s cabin and along the Forest edge. Known instances are the fenced Hippogriff paddock five minutes round the trees from the cabin (1993); crates and lawn by the cabin and a rear trestle table (1994); an edge tree beyond the horse paddock and a muddy foal site (early 1995); a dug Niffler plot before the cabin (spring 1995); a trestle table ten yards from the door (1995); and the Forest-edge lawn of the 1996 examination. The sites of the Flobberworm and salamander lessons are unstated.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** A lesson is a joint class of two houses, such as Gryffindors with Slytherins, so each site must take both at once. (B03-020, B05-051)
- **Explicit.** It held twelve Hippogriffs on long chains and a class spread among them; wings are given as twelve feet, and one animal flew a full circuit of it. (B03-021, B03-022)
- **Estimate.** Make that paddock at least 40 m across. Because: A Hippogriff with twelve-foot wings spans up to 7 m and flies one circuit before landing, while a dozen animals and a class spread out inside. The text gives no size. (B03-021, B03-022)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The Hippogriff paddock is about five minutes’ walk from the cabin, round the edge of the trees without entering them. (B03-020)
- **Explicit.** In September 1995 a long trestle table stood about ten yards from Hagrid’s front door, with pupils working on the grass around it. (B05-051, B05-076)
- **Explicit.** In December 1994 pupils worked at a trestle table behind the cabin, using the building as shelter. (B04-113)
- **Explicit.** In January 1995 the class passed the horse paddock to a unicorn tied at an edge tree, with the boys left at the paddock fence. Muddy ground served for foals early that year. (B04-133, B04-154)
- **Explicit.** The June 1996 practical examination, with a Knarl among a dozen hedgehogs, filled the lawn at the Forest edge in sight of Hagrid’s window; lessons there can be seen from Gryffindor’s common room. (B05-260, B05-259)
- **Explicit.** Flobberworm lessons (1993) and a salamander bonfire (January 1994) have no stated site. (B03-031, B03-076)

### Shape and structure

- **Inference.** No classroom or animal house is used; every described lesson is in the open or at a temporary table or crate. Because: All lessons narrated are outdoors, and no stable or roofed outbuilding is ever mentioned. (B03-021, B04-025, B04-113, B05-051, B05-260)
- **Explicit.** That paddock is a fenced grass enclosure with a gate; the fence takes tether chains and pupils climb over it. (B03-021, B03-023)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Classes climb back uphill to the castle steps, or cross the vegetable patch to the greenhouses; the school bell carries faintly across the grounds to them. (B03-023, B03-100, B05-053, B05-052)

### What is inside

- **Reported.** Hagrid says he has raised a special herd for lessons over the years; it proves to be the Thestrals, shown inside the Forest. (B05-135, B05-140)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Skrewts: crates of about a hundred six-inch hatchlings stood by the cabin in September 1994; by November pupils walked leashed three-foot Skrewts over the lawn, each blasting several yards at a time. (B04-025, B04-067)
- **Explicit.** In spring 1995 a large patch of freshly dug earth before the cabin held a hundred buried coins for Nifflers, within sight of the carriage window; crates were stacked at the cabin wall. (B04-181, B04-231, B04-182)

### Open questions

- The position of the 1993 paddock relative to the carriage site and Hagrid’s garden is unknown; it should not be merged with the Beauxbatons horse paddock.

## Beauxbatons carriage and paddock

The Beauxbatons carriage is a powder-blue flying vehicle as big as a large house, drawn by twelve elephant-sized winged palominos. It stayed at Hogwarts from 30 October 1994 to June 1995, standing two hundred yards from Hagrid’s front door near the Forest edge, with a makeshift horse paddock alongside, and served as sleeping quarters for Madame Maxime and about a dozen pupils. It came back briefly in June 1997. Treat it as a dated visitor, not a fixture.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Powder-blue and as big as a large house (simply a house in 1997), it looked far bigger than a hundred broomsticks in the sky. (B04-043, B06-202)
- **Estimate.** Make the body at least 12 m long and 8 m high. Because: A large house is about 12 m by 10 m over two or more storeys, and inside it must clear a headmistress of about Hagrid’s height, taken as 3.4 m. (B04-043, B04-252)
- **Explicit.** Twelve winged palomino horses draw it, each the size of an elephant, with hooves larger than dinner plates. (B04-043, B06-202)
- **Explicit.** Madame Maxime is within an inch of Hagrid’s height, with a shoe like a child’s sled, and her size is said to explain the carriage and horses. (B04-252)
- **Inference.** It must work as a dormitory for about thirteen for eight months, with a door, steps and rooms that admit a person of Hagrid’s size. Because: Pupils are seen climbing in to sleep and the party numbers Maxime plus about a dozen, from late October to June. The interior is never described. (B04-052, B04-252)
- **Estimate.** Make the paddock at least 40 m square. Because: Twelve elephant-sized horses at 8 m by 4 m each need about 380 m² just to stand, about 20 m square. Grazing, wings and fence walking at least double each side. (B04-043, B04-052)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** It stood two hundred yards (about 180 m) from Hagrid’s front door, near the edge of the Forest. (B04-052)
- **Explicit.** It is in view from a cabin window and the Owlery, and from the castle entrance the harnessing is seen far across the grounds. (B04-055, B04-180, B04-240)
- **Explicit.** Forest trees begin a short way beyond the paddock; an edge tree used for a lesson and the spot where Crouch appeared both lie just past it. (B04-133, B04-187, B04-190)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** It has vast wheels, a door bearing two crossed golden wands each giving off three stars, fold-out golden steps, and windows lit from within at night. (B04-043, B04-182, B04-077, B04-187)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The walk to the dragon enclosure starts by rounding the paddock’s edge from the carriage door. (B04-077)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** A makeshift fenced paddock lies alongside for grazing, with a trough of single-malt whisky in one corner. (B04-052, B04-044, B04-101, B04-133)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** It flew in over the Forest canopy on 30 October 1994 and landed before the castle; by next day it stood near the cabin. It left in June 1995. (B04-043, B04-052, B04-240)
- **Explicit.** It returned on the afternoon before the June 1997 funeral, landing at the Forest edge; its distance from the cabin then is unstated. (B06-202)

### Conflicts

- The measured two hundred yards sits uneasily with other cues: Harry hears Maxime inside it from Hagrid’s door, in snow it seems to stand next to the cabin, and whisky fumes reach Hagrid’s class. Keep the measured figure. (B04-052, B04-077, B04-118, B04-101)

### Open questions

- No interior is ever described; layout, rooms and storeys must be designed.
- Which side of the cabin it stood on is unstated, beyond being near the Forest edge and in view of one cabin window.
