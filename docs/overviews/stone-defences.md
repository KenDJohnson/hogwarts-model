# Third-floor forbidden corridor and Stone defences

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/stone-defences.md)

The Stone defences begin on the third floor and end far below it. From the locked door closing off the Charms corridor the way leads past Fluffy to a trapdoor in the corridor floor, then straight down: a drop past the second, first and ground floors to the level of the dungeons or lower, into a chamber of Devil’s Snare. A stone passage slopes down to the high key chamber; a door opens into the huge chess chamber; a passage climbs to the troll chamber; doors lead on to the potion room; and black fire bars the last chamber and the Mirror of Erised. The vertical drop is the feature to plan first. The shaft is a sealed column through or beside every lower floor and the dungeons, and the chambers need a large block of deep space that no other scene shows. The text gives no dimensions. Hermione’s miles under the school is a guess and Dumbledore’s dungeons a passing phrase. Everything here is dated to 1991–92.

## Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: 15–20 m from trapdoor to plant chamber, then lower again down the sloping passage, so the deepest rooms are well over 20 m below the third-floor corridor. Because: Stamp arithmetic gives 12–24 m; four storeys at an assumed 4 m give 16 m. The passage’s fall is not stated, so any further depth is open. (B01-125, B01-127, B05-110)

## Where it is

- **Inference.** Top to bottom: third-floor corridor, shaft past three lower floors, dungeon level (four floors under the third), the plant chamber there or lower, then a downward slope to the key chamber. Because: Floor numbering and the dungeons’ position come from the eight-floors-below note; the chamber order and slopes are narrated. No scene fixes a chamber to a named level. (B01-124, B01-125, B01-127, B05-110)
- **Inference.** The shaft cannot sit over an ordinary room: whatever is drawn under the trapdoor on the lower floors must leave a closed vertical column, wide enough for a rider on a broom. Because: A shaft from a third-floor floor must displace what is below it, and Harry assumes brooms can use it. No lower-floor scene shows it, so it has no doors or windows. (B01-124, B01-125, B01-135)
- **Inference.** Under the castle the chain needs a tall void for the key chamber and a wide one for the chess room, clear of the dungeon rooms or below them. Because: Both rooms are too big to fit unnoticed among named dungeon rooms, and no scene shows a connection to them. (B01-128, B01-130, B01-138)

## Getting there

- **Inference.** The route is one-way in practice: nothing lets anyone climb the shaft, so the only way back up that the text names is by flying. Because: There is no ladder or steps at the trapdoor, and Harry’s escape plan depends on brooms. (B01-124, B01-135)

## How it changes

- **Inference.** Set up for the 1991–92 year. After June 1992 no text says what became of any stage. Because: The ban was announced for that year only, the Stone was destroyed, and no later book returns. (B01-020, B01-138)

## Forbidden third-floor corridor

A corridor on the third floor, shut behind a locked door and out of bounds to everyone during 1991–92, when it held the first guard on the way to the Philosopher’s Stone. The door closes off the end of the Charms corridor, a few seconds from the head of a carpeted staircase. Behind it a three-headed dog, Fluffy, stood floor to ceiling on a ringed trapdoor let into the corridor floor. The trapdoor lifts upward on to a black shaft with no ladder or steps and no visible bottom, the only known way down to the Stone chambers. Because that shaft starts three storeys above the ground and ends at dungeon level or deeper, it needs a clear vertical run through the second, first and ground floors, at a spot no other scene mentions. The corridor’s length, width and ceiling height are unstated, and nothing is recorded of it after June 1992.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Ceiling height is tied only to the dog: standing in 1991, Fluffy reaches from the floor right up to the ceiling. (B01-051)
- **Inference.** The ceiling is not cathedral height: asleep, the dog is low enough for Ron to see past its back and step across its legs. Because: A body that an eleven-year-old can look across when slumped cannot stand many times his height. (B01-124, B01-051)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** On the third floor. Dumbledore’s 1991 notice places it on the right-hand side, without saying whose right or seen from where. (B01-020, B01-028, B01-050)
- **Explicit.** Its door is at the far end of the Charms corridor. (B01-050)
- **Inference.** The Charms corridor must therefore also be on the third floor. Because: That corridor’s end door opens straight into the forbidden corridor, with no stairs between them. (B01-050)
- **Explicit.** The door is only seconds from the top of a carpeted staircase climbing to the third floor. (B01-123)
- **Explicit.** Pupils pass the door on everyday journeys: in spring 1992 they stop to press an ear to it and hear the dog growling. (B01-091)
- **Explicit.** The door opens directly into the dog’s space: in 1991 the pupils shut it and took the place for a room before turning to find Fluffy. (B01-050, B01-051)
- **Inference.** The floor at the trapdoor cannot lie over ordinary rooms: the shaft under it needs a clear run down past the second, first and ground floors. Because: The opening is in a third-floor floor and the pupils land far below it, at least four floors down if they reach dungeon level. (B01-124, B01-125, B05-110)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** It is a true corridor, not a room, with the trapdoor set in its floor. (B01-051, B01-124)
- **Explicit.** The trapdoor has a ring; pulled, it swings up and open. (B01-124)
- **Reported.** Hermione alone notices in 1991 that the dog is standing on a trapdoor. (B01-052)
- **Explicit.** Under the opening is only blackness: no bottom in sight and no ladder, steps or other way of climbing down, so entrants have to hang and drop. (B01-124, B01-125)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The door was locked in September 1991, resisted forcing, gave to Alohomora, and stood ajar on the night in June 1992. (B01-028, B01-050, B01-123)
- **Reported.** Hagrid claims only he and Dumbledore know how to get past the dog. (B01-094)
- **Reported.** Harry’s way out from below is to fly: brooms from the key room, up through the trapdoor and past the dog. (B01-135)
- **Reported.** Meeting Hermione and Ron in the Entrance Hall, Dumbledore rushes straight to the third floor, which fits the trapdoor being the way in. (B01-141)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** In 1991–92 a three-headed dog guards the trapdoor: Hagrid’s Fluffy, on loan to Dumbledore. His thunderous growl carries through the shut door. (B01-068, B01-051)
- **Explicit.** Music puts Fluffy to sleep and he stirs the moment it stops; his three noses still follow people hidden under a Cloak. (B01-120)
- **Explicit.** A harp lay at the dog’s feet on the night of June 1992. (B01-124)
- **Reported.** Closed for the 1991–92 school year, with Dumbledore warning pupils of a painful death. (B01-020)

### Open questions

- Whether this is the same corridor as the third-floor corridor with the one-eyed witch. Both are on the third floor, but no text links them.
- Its length, and whether it continues beyond the trapdoor or stops there.
- Whether the dog and trapdoor stayed after June 1992. No later scene returns here.

## Trapdoor shaft and Devil’s Snare chamber

The chamber at the foot of the trapdoor shaft, under the forbidden third-floor corridor and at dungeon level or deeper. The drop is long enough to shrink the open trapdoor to no bigger than a postage stamp. In June 1992 a mass of Devil’s Snare covered the floor to break the fall, and one stone passage led on.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The drop is a long free fall through cold, damp air; from the landing the open trapdoor is a speck of light no bigger than a postage stamp. (B01-125)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: a drop of about 15 m. An opening 1 m wide that looks like a 2.5 cm stamp held 30–60 cm from the eye is 12–24 m away. Because: The text gives only the stamp comparison; trapdoor width, stamp size and viewing distance are assumptions. Four storeys at an assumed 4 m each give a matching 16 m to dungeon level. (B01-125, B05-110)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** The shaft falls from the third floor past the second, first and ground floors to dungeon level or below, so it needs a sealed vertical run through or beside each of them. Because: The trapdoor is in a third-floor floor, the pupils land far below, and the dungeons lie four floors under the third floor (eight under the seventh). No lower floor ever shows the shaft, so it must be walled off. (B01-124, B01-125, B01-138, B05-110)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** In June 1992 long snaking Devil’s Snare covers the floor and tightens on whatever struggles. The room otherwise offers damp walls and a single stone passage as the only way on. (B01-126, B01-127)

### Open questions

- Where the shaft and this chamber fall in plan relative to the rooms on the floors above.

## Winged-key chamber

A tall stone chamber at the bottom of a sloping, dripping passage from the Devil’s Snare chamber, with a heavy wooden door in the opposite wall leading to the chess chamber. The vault is high enough for three people on brooms to chase a key through the air, and Ron still manages to ride into it. In June 1992 hundreds of winged keys filled the space. Its light source is never described.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The ceiling arches high above; the room is called high, and a flying Ron can still reach and hit the ceiling. (B01-128)
- **Explicit.** In June 1992 hundreds of keys fly here, enough to read as one whirling cloud. (B01-129)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: about 10 m high and 15 m across. Three riders above, level with and below a dodging key need about 3 m of height each; the sprint across takes seconds. Because: No dimensions are given. Rider spacing and a dash of 3 seconds at 5 m/s are assumptions. (B01-128, B01-129)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Entered from a stone passage that slopes downward from the plant chamber, with water trickling down its walls. (B01-127)
- **Explicit.** The heavy wooden door to the chess chamber is directly across the room from the entrance. (B01-128, B01-130)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Stone walls, brilliantly lit throughout, with no lamps, torches or windows mentioned. (B01-128)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** The door has a silver handle and a lock that defeats force and Alohomora; only the one big key opens it. (B01-128, B01-129)
- **Reported.** Harry names this room’s brooms as the way back up and out through the trapdoor. (B01-135)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Broomsticks are left in the room, at least three. (B01-128)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** The June 1992 flock has rainbow feathers. The right key is large and silver, with bright-blue wings, one already bent. Once used, it flies off again. (B01-129)

## Giant chess chamber

The chamber beyond the key room, filled by a huge chessboard with stone pieces taller than the pupils. Entrants arrive behind the black men and the door onward stands behind the white men at the far side, so the board has to be crossed by playing. It is dark until someone steps in, then light floods it.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** A huge board: the black men all stand taller than the pupils, the white men tower a long way off across the room, and knights ride stone horses. (B01-130, B01-131)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: a board 16 m square in a room about 20 m across. Eight squares at 2 m each hold a mounted stone knight, plus a strip by the wall for taken men. Because: Pieces obey real chess rules, so a standard eight-by-eight board is assumed; the text never counts squares. The 2 m square and the wall strip are assumptions. (B01-130, B01-131)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Entered from the key room on the black side; another door stands behind the white men on the far side, opening on to a passage that climbs. (B01-130, B01-132)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Entry floods the dark room with light. In June 1992 the stone men wake, take orders, strike down captured men and drag them to the wall, then part to clear the door. (B01-130, B01-131)

## Troll chamber

A foul-smelling chamber between the chess room and the potion room, reached up a passage from the chess chamber and through a door. In June 1992 it held a troll bigger than the twelve-foot Hallowe’en troll, already knocked out, with one huge leg lying in the way to the next door.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The troll here is larger than the Hallowe’en troll, which stands twelve feet tall. (B01-132, B01-148)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: a ceiling above 4.5 m and a floor about 6 m long. A troll over 3.7 m tall must have stood here, and it lies flat with room to step past. Because: Twelve feet is 3.7 m and this troll is larger; headroom and floor margins are assumptions. The text gives the room no size. (B01-132, B01-148)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Lies at the top of the climbing passage from the chess chamber, behind a door; a further door leads straight on to the potion room. (B01-132, B01-133)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** In June 1992 the troll lies unconscious with a bleeding swelling on its skull, and Quirrell claims the work. Its stench drives the pupils straight on. (B01-132, B01-139)

## Potion chamber

A room between the troll chamber and the last chamber, with a doorway at each end. In June 1992 a table held seven different bottles in a line with a written riddle beside them, and crossing the threshold lit purple fire in the doorway behind and black fire in the doorway ahead.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** No size is given. The room needs only the table, two doorways and floor enough for Hermione to walk up and down beside the bottles. Because: Those are the only physical demands the scene makes on the room. (B01-133, B01-134)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Entered by the door out of the troll chamber; a second doorway leads on to the last chamber. (B01-133)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** A table holds seven differently shaped bottles in one line, beside a roll of paper with the riddle: three poisons, two nettle wines, two passage drinks. (B01-133, B01-134)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** In June 1992 fire fills both doorways once the threshold is crossed: purple behind, black ahead. The tiny smallest bottle passes the black, a round bottle at the right-hand end the purple. (B01-133, B01-134)

## Last chamber of the Stone

The last underground chamber, behind the black fire at the end of the Stone defences. It held the Mirror of Erised, free-standing, with room to walk all round it and more open floor beyond.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Quirrell walks right round the Mirror and behind it, and Harry gets a few paces clear of it, so there is open floor on all sides. No dimensions are given. (B01-136)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The end of the chain, entered only through the black flames from the potion room; that flame doorway is also the only exit named. (B01-134, B01-136)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** The Mirror of Erised stands here in June 1992, a free-standing framed mirror brought from a disused classroom. No pedestal, steps or pit are described. (B01-136)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** The Stone lay hidden by the Mirror’s rule: it would come only to someone who sought it with no wish to use it. It reached Harry’s pocket; Dumbledore later reports it destroyed. (B01-137, B01-138)

## Stone defences as a whole

The chain of seven guarded stages below the forbidden corridor, set up for 1991–92 to protect the Philosopher’s Stone: Fluffy, Devil’s Snare, winged keys, giant chess, a troll, the potion fires and the Mirror of Erised. The route starts at the third-floor trapdoor, drops at least four storeys to dungeon level or lower, then runs in single file through the plant chamber, down a sloping passage to the key and chess chambers, up another passage to the troll chamber, and on through the potion room to the last chamber. Two big rooms sit in that run: the key chamber needs a high vault and the chess chamber a wide floor, so the whole chain takes a large block of space that must lie beneath, or out to the side of, the dungeon rooms. No link to the dungeons, or to any other floor, is described.

### Size and numbers

- **Reported.** Seven stages in all: Hagrid’s dog, then work credited to Sprout, Flitwick, McGonagall, Quirrell, Snape and Dumbledore. (B01-094, B01-132)
- **Reported.** On landing at the foot of the shaft, Hermione guesses they must be miles beneath the school. It is a guess, not a measurement. (B01-125)
- **Inference.** The chain is not literally miles down: Harry’s shout from the bottom reaches Ron at the trapdoor, and the flute above is faintly audible below. Because: Voice and music both cross the whole height of the shaft, and all three pupils survive the jump. (B01-125)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum: chambers end to end run about 55 m, and about 70 m with passages. They need not lie straight, but the block is far bigger than a cellar. Because: Adds assumed minimums: plant 5 m, key 15 m, chess 20 m, troll 6 m, potion 4 m, last 5 m, passages about 15 m. No length is stated anywhere. (B01-127, B01-128, B01-130, B01-132)

### Where it is

- **Reported.** Dumbledore afterwards speaks of the last events as taking place down in the dungeons. (B01-138)
- **Inference.** The chain sits at dungeon level or deeper, but apart from the working dungeons: no scene joins it to the Potions rooms or any dungeon corridor. Because: Every entry and planned exit in the text uses the third-floor trapdoor; the only underground label is Dumbledore’s passing remark. (B01-135, B01-138, B01-141)
- **Explicit.** Levels shift along the way: the passage after the plant chamber slopes down, and the one after the chess chamber goes up. Other stages connect by doors. (B01-127, B01-132)

### Getting there

- **Inference.** A single-file chain with no side branches: each stage has one way on, and the trapdoor is the only known way in. Because: The passage from the plant chamber is called the only way on, and every later room shows just one onward door. (B01-127, B01-128, B01-130, B01-132, B01-133)
- **Reported.** The exit used in fact is undescribed: Hermione says only that she got back, revived Ron and was heading for the owlery when they met Dumbledore in the Entrance Hall. (B01-141)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** All this is dated to 1991–92. Quirrell admits scouting the guards at Hallowe’en 1991; the Stone is destroyed in June 1992, and no later scene visits the chambers. (B01-138, B01-139)

### Conflicts

- Hermione guesses miles under the school; Dumbledore says the dungeons. Neither is a survey, and they imply very different depths. (B01-125, B01-138)
- Harry’s plan was to fly out through the trapdoor and past Fluffy, but Hermione’s account has the pair meeting Dumbledore in the Entrance Hall on the way up to the owlery, with no mention of brooms or the third floor. (B01-135, B01-141)

### Open questions

- How the shaft is hidden on the second, first and ground floors, and where it falls in plan.
- Whether the chambers lie directly under the third-floor corridor or run off beneath another part of the castle or the grounds.
