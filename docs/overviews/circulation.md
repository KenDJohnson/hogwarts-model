# Corridors, stairs, landings and general circulation

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/circulation.md)

The castle's connecting fabric: 142 staircases, stone corridors on every floor, and concealed shortcuts between them. Walking down from the top, the seventh floor holds the Fat Lady's corridor, Sir Cadogan's landing at the start of the North Tower stair, and the Barnabas tapestry. The fifth floor holds the Prefects' bathroom corridor and the east-wing corridor of the 1996 swamp. The fourth has a large vase halfway along one corridor, a blocked mirror passage and the head of a two-floor tapestry stair. The third holds the forbidden corridor, the one-eyed witch and, by inference, the Charms corridor. The second holds the disputed attack corridor outside Myrtle's bathroom and a tapestry shortcut towards Gryffindor. The first floor is the head of the marble staircase, and the ground floor is the Entrance Hall. With seven gaps between the ground and seventh floors, the staircases average about 20 per gap, so each floor is crossed at many separate points and no single stair stack will do. Routes are long: Ron allows ten minutes from the Great Hall to the top of North Tower. Windows along them look over the grounds.

## Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** 142 staircases serve eight main levels, about 20 per gap, or about 16 after allowing for tower spirals and shortcuts. Because: 142 ÷ 7 gaps = 20.3. With an assumed 30 set aside: 112 ÷ 7 = 16. (B01-025)
- **Estimate.** Each floor needs at least about 400 m of corridor, and one long route covers about 450 m on the level. Because: 16 staircases at an assumed 25 m apart: 400 m. Ron's ten minutes less about 4 minutes on stairs, at an assumed 1.25 m/s: 450 m. (B01-025, B03-014)

## Where it is

- **Explicit.** Floors count in British style along the main route: six floors down from the seventh, then the marble staircase to the Entrance Hall. (B04-106)
- **Explicit.** The dungeons lie eight floors below the seventh floor. (B05-110)

## Getting there

- **Explicit.** Concealed shortcuts behind tapestries, panels and false walls link floors, and the Marauder's Map shows many of them. (B04-139, B04-147, B01-021)

## Moving staircases

The castle's general staircases, scattered through the whole building rather than gathered in one stairwell. This is a group, not one structure. The text counts 142. Between the ground floor and the seventh floor lie eight levels and seven gaps, which averages about 20 staircases per gap, or about 16 after setting aside a generous 30 for tower spirals and shortcuts. A climb from the ground floor to the seventh floor uses seven staircases, one per gap, so any single route uses about 5 per cent of the total. Each gap must therefore be crossed at many separate points across a wide plan. Forms vary: broad curving flights, thin unsteady ones, narrow stone flights, carpeted stairs, stairs with banisters, portrait-lined flights and tight stone spirals in the towers. Some arrive somewhere else on Fridays, one is called swivelling, and many have a false step that traps a leg. No scene shows a general staircase moving in front of anyone.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Hogwarts has 142 staircases. (B01-025)
- **Inference.** A climb from ground level to the seventh floor takes seven long staircases, one for each gap between floors. Because: The seventh long staircase from the Great Hall ends on Sir Cadogan's landing (B03-014), which is later placed on the seventh floor (B03-089). Harry also reckons seven staircases from the ground-floor chamber to the Fat Lady (B04-060), and six floors plus the marble staircase make seven (B04-106). (B03-014, B03-089, B04-060, B04-106)
- **Estimate.** About 20 staircases per gap between floors on average, or about 16 after setting aside tower spirals and shortcuts. Because: 142 ÷ 7 gaps (ground to seventh floor, eight levels) = 20.3. Assuming a generous 30 of the 142 are tower spirals, dungeon stairs and shortcuts: 112 ÷ 7 = 16. A staircase spanning two floors (B04-147) counts once, so long stacked stairs raise the crossings per gap rather than lower them. (B01-025, B04-147)
- **Estimate.** One route uses only about 5 per cent of the staircases; spacing the rest needs at least about 400 m of corridor per floor. Because: 7 ÷ 142 = 4.9%. At an assumed minimum of 25 m between separate staircases, 16 staircases on a floor need 16 × 25 m = 400 m of corridor. A castle built around two or three stair stacks would be short by a factor of five or more. (B01-025, B03-014)
- **Explicit.** The way from the portrait hole down to the Entrance Hall is described simply as many staircases. (B05-129)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** One flight arriving on the seventh floor has a statue of Lachlan the Lanky to the right of its top. (B05-059)
- **Inference.** Floors are not perfectly level: a staircase lies on the walk along the seventh floor from Gryffindor to the blank wall. Because: Harry stops at the top of a last staircase after the three have set out along the seventh floor, and only then reaches the tapestry (B05-108). Short stairs within a floor would also help account for so high a total. (B05-108)
- **Explicit.** The flight from the seventh floor down to the sixth near the boys' bathroom is also called marble. (B06-152)
- **Explicit.** Landings carry windows or single large paintings; Sir Cadogan's seventh-floor landing has little else. (B03-014, B03-089, B02-160)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Some are broad and curving, others thin and unsteady. (B01-025)
- **Explicit.** Narrow stone flights rise towards the Fat Lady's passage, past portraits and armour. (B02-014)
- **Explicit.** The flight up to the third floor is carpeted. (B01-123)
- **Explicit.** Some flights have banisters, and the flight below the portrait hole is lined with pictures of old witches and wizards. (B05-042, B05-030)
- **Explicit.** Towers are climbed by tight stone spirals, such as the one from the West Tower battlements to a corridor at its foot. (B03-156, B03-015, B07-052)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** On Fridays some staircases arrive at a different place. (B01-025)
- **Explicit.** Many stairs are trick stairs: a step partway up gives way or disappears, and pupils learn to jump it. (B01-025, B04-019, B06-186)
- **Explicit.** One false step swallows a leg to above the knee and struggling sinks it deeper; someone else must pull the victim free. (B04-148, B04-019)
- **Reported.** One staircase is called swivelling and offers a way round the corridor to the Defence office. (B05-271)
- **Inference.** No scene shows a general staircase moving before anyone's eyes; the only stair seen in motion is the spiral behind the Headmaster's gargoyle. Because: A search of all seven books for staircases moving, shifting, swinging or turning finds only that spiral, the Friday remark and the one swivelling name. Harry's sense that everything moves about is his impression (B01-026). (B01-025, B01-026, B05-271, B06-035)

### Open questions

- How the 142 are shared between wings and towers is unstated: the modeller must place them, keeping the average of 16 to 20 per gap.
- The swivelling staircase is a name only; nothing says what it does or how far it turns.

## Corridors in general

Stone corridors and landings on every floor, the general fabric linking the castle's rooms. This is a group, not one place. They are long, branching and confusing: Ron allows ten minutes from the Great Hall to the top of North Tower, and two first-years once spent nearly an hour failing to retrace one route. Widths vary from a passage narrow enough for two men to touch a hidden boy to corridors that take crowds and, in 1996, flying firework wheels five feet across. Windows are high and frequent, and many look over the grounds to Hagrid's cabin, the pitch or the lake. Light comes from wall torches, lamps and the occasional chandelier. Contents include rugs, waste-paper baskets, armour, statues, vases on plinths, tapestries, portraits and cupboards. Classrooms open off both sides of some stretches. Some corridors have boarded floors. Damage is dated: fireworks in 1996, a half-collapsed ceiling in 1997, and broken windows in 1998.

### Size and numbers

- **Estimate.** The walk from the Great Hall to the top of North Tower implies at least several hundred metres of level corridor, roughly 450 m. Because: Ron allows ten minutes (B03-014). Assuming about 3 minutes for seven staircases and 1 minute for the tower spiral leaves 6 minutes; at an assumed 1.25 m/s that is 450 m. A rough figure, since Ron was guessing and the three then lost their way. (B03-014)
- **Explicit.** Two first-years wandered for nearly an hour trying to retrace one route. (B01-083)
- **Explicit.** One corridor near the library is so narrow that two approaching men would have knocked into an invisible boy. (B01-081)
- **Inference.** Corridors one floor below the Defence office exceed five feet in width and height. Because: Firework wheels five feet across flew along them in April 1996 (B05-214). (B05-214)
- **Explicit.** Doors line both sides of some corridors: classes are audible left and right, and door after door opens at once. (B02-081)
- **Explicit.** A corridor window, once smashed, took Hagrid's whole body thrown in from outside. (B07-072)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Windows on the Gryffindor route and near History of Magic look towards Hagrid's cabin. (B05-045, B05-090, B06-090)
- **Explicit.** Windows in the corridor outside the hospital wing show the Quidditch pitch and the lake. (B05-301)
- **Explicit.** A large vase stands halfway along the fourth-floor corridor on Harry's way up to the Owlery. (B04-034)
- **Explicit.** A corridor near the Owlery leads towards the west wing. (B05-064)
- **Explicit.** Two corridors joined by a corner link the Defence classroom to the staff room. (B03-028)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Walls are stone: stray curses crack them and blast out fragments. (B06-184)
- **Explicit.** Some corridors and stairs are floored with boards, which squeak under Harry and quake under Hagrid. (B04-147, B07-072)
- **Explicit.** High windows throw moonlight and sunlight in bands across the floor, and one route passes window after window. (B01-047, B04-196, B05-061, B07-052)
- **Explicit.** Light comes from torches in wall brackets, from lamps in places, and from a chandelier in one corridor. (B07-060, B02-081, B06-081, B06-083)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Fifth-years may stay out in the corridors up to nine in the evening. (B05-108)

### What is inside

- **Explicit.** Rugs, waste-paper baskets, suits of armour and statues on plinths stand along the corridors. (B01-027, B07-076)
- **Explicit.** Cupboards open off corridors, including lockable ones for brooms. (B03-028, B05-215)

### How it changes

## Second-floor attack corridor

The corridor outside Moaning Myrtle's bathroom, narrated in Book 2 as the last, empty passage of the second floor. It is stone-walled, with windows and torch brackets, and stairs serve both ends, so crowds from the Great Hall can pour in from either direction at once. Myrtle's door, marked Out of Order, stands a few paces past the spot where Filch kept watch. The corridor is the site of the 1992 wall message, in foot-high letters between two windows, and of a second message in 1993. Water from the bathroom has covered half its length. Its floor number is disputed within Book 2: most journeys support the second floor, but this is not settled.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** It must hold a crowd: pupils coming up from the feast pack it from both ends. Because: The text has hundreds of feet on the stairs at either end and the students massing in the passage around the hanging cat (B02-047). (B02-047)
- **Explicit.** The 1992 message stood in foot-high shining letters between two windows. (B02-047)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Reached by climbing the marble staircase to the first floor, a further flight, then going round the second floor to its last empty passage. (B02-046)
- **Explicit.** Myrtle's bathroom door stands a few paces past the place where Filch's chair stood. (B02-057)
- **Explicit.** McGonagall's office lies moments away along the corridor, with no stairs mentioned. (B02-157)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** The wall is stone, with at least two windows and torch brackets. (B02-047, B02-050)
- **Explicit.** The window beside the writing has a small crack in its topmost pane. (B02-056)

### Getting there

- **Explicit.** Stairs serve both ends: pupils leaving the feast enter from both directions at once. (B02-047)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Filch's cleaning fluid failed to remove the message, and he kept watch from a chair set against that wall. (B02-050)
- **Explicit.** After the first attack the floor showed scorch marks. (B02-056)
- **Explicit.** In early 1993 water from Myrtle's bathroom covered half the corridor. (B02-098)
- **Reported.** A second message appeared directly below the first in May 1993. (B02-136)

### Conflicts

- Floor number. Narration runs up to the second floor and round to this passage (B02-046). Hermione places Myrtle's toilet, beside it, on the first floor (B02-045). Lockhart's office is on the second-floor corridor (B02-033), yet it is just upstairs from here (B02-048), and the nearest stairs lead down from it to here (B02-138), which also implies the first floor. (B02-046, B02-045, B02-033, B02-048, B02-138)
- Book 2 also has this corridor one floor above the hospital wing (B02-097), though from Myrtle's bathroom the ward is said to be up (B02-095). (B02-097, B02-095)

A corridor on the fifth floor of the east wing, with classrooms beyond it, best known as the site of the Weasley twins' Portable Swamp in 1996. The twins call it Gregory the Smarmy's corridor, so that statue probably stands here. It lies above the Defence classroom and well away from the Defence office.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The swamp filled the corridor, which was roped off, and Filch punted pupils across it to classrooms beyond. (B05-235)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** The corridor is on the fifth floor of the east wing. (B05-235)

## Hidden stairs and shortcuts inside the castle

Concealed stairs, tapestry openings, wall doors and mirror or panel passages that stay inside the castle. This is a group of separate features, not one passage, and the Marauder's Map shows many of them. The common form is an opening behind a tapestry with a narrower staircase beyond, sometimes with a tapestry at each end. The best described drops two floors in one long flight, apparently from the fourth floor to the second, with a false step halfway. Known instances also include a sliding panel and a hanging tapestry above the marble staircase, a second-floor tapestry used as a regular way up to Gryffindor, a sideways shortcut on the third floor, a fourth-floor alternative route, a door posing as solid wall on the way to Potions, and a tapestry passage coming out near Charms. A roomy passage behind a fourth-floor mirror is reported blocked.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The Marauder's Map shows many shortcuts as well as secret passages. (B04-139)
- **Explicit.** One narrow tapestry staircase drops two floors in a single long flight. (B04-147)
- **Reported.** A passage behind a big fourth-floor mirror was roomy enough to practise jinxes, but is reported blocked by a collapse. (B05-097)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** That staircase apparently runs from the fourth floor to the second. Because: It opens halfway along the corridor one flight below the corridor of the fifth-floor Prefects' bathroom (B04-147, B04-131) and descends two floors. The second-floor tapestry shortcut (B06-070) may be its foot. (B04-147, B04-131, B06-070)
- **Explicit.** A concealed stair with a vanishing step ends at a tapestry, in a corridor leading to a landing of the marble staircase. (B06-186)
- **Explicit.** A tapestry on the second floor opens on the usual shortcut up to Gryffindor Tower, with a corridor behind it. (B06-070)
- **Explicit.** Above the marble staircase, the route to Gryffindor uses doorways hidden behind a sliding panel and a hanging tapestry. (B01-021, B05-024)
- **Explicit.** A shortcut opens sideways off a third-floor corridor, on the way up to the Fat Lady. (B06-145)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Tapestries hide both its top and bottom openings. (B04-147)
- **Inference.** The false-step stair of Books 4 and 6 is probably one staircase. Because: Each is a concealed narrow stair behind a tapestry, with a step halfway that pupils jump (B04-019, B04-148, B06-186). The text never says outright that they are the same. (B04-019, B04-148, B06-186)
