# Dungeons and lower corridors

[Castle overview](index.md) · [All records for this area](../areas/dungeons.md)

The dungeons occupy the level directly below the ground floor, reached by stone steps from the Entrance Hall. They are a maze of dim, cold stone passages lit by torches. From the foot of the steps the passages lead to Snape’s office door, the Potions classroom with its queueing space, numbered dungeon rooms, at least five of them, and the roomier dungeon used for the 1992 Deathday party. They run on deeper under the school to the blank wall hiding the Slytherin common room. Only one level is ever counted. The kitchens and Hufflepuff basement lie at the same depth, but are reached by a separate door and are broad and brightly lit.

## Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Two dungeon rooms are singled out for size: Potions is held in one of the large ones, and the 1992 party in one of the roomier ones. (B02-073, B02-042)

## Where it is

- **Inference.** The dungeon level lies exactly one storey below the ground floor: eight floors below the seventh floor in British numbering. Because: Seven floors down from the seventh is the ground floor, so the eighth is the first level under it. (B05-110, B04-106)
- **Inference.** The kitchens and Hufflepuff basement lie at the same depth but on the other side of the marble staircase, bright where the dungeons are gloomy. Because: Both are one run of stone steps below the Entrance Hall, reached by doors on opposite sides of the staircase. The text contrasts the kitchen corridor with the gloomy passage to Snape’s dungeon, and Book 6 lists the dungeons and the basement separately. (B04-106, B05-302, B05-115, B06-129)

## Conflicts

- Harry says the Slytherin common room is under the lake, yet the castle stands on a high cliff over the lake and the dungeons are one storey below ground. Both hold only if the passages descend a long way. (B07-028, B05-110, B02-005, B01-006, B02-091)

## Dungeon corridors

The dungeon corridors are the underground passages of the castle, a group rather than one corridor, lying exactly one storey below the ground floor and reached by stone steps from the Entrance Hall. They are dim, cold and rough-walled, lit by torches in brackets, and they link the Potions classroom, Snape’s office, the Slytherin common room and other dungeon rooms. Known stretches include the passage with Snape’s office door halfway along it, the waiting space outside the Potions door, the way to the 1992 Deathday party, and a maze of deserted passages leading to the Slytherin wall. Only one dungeon level is ever counted, though the way to Slytherin is said to run deeper under the school.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** The passages form a maze: in 1992 two boys walked deserted passages for about a quarter of an hour before meeting anyone. That time includes searching. (B02-091)
- **Reported.** In a diary memory, following footsteps from the Potions room took perhaps five minutes before a corner and a door. (B02-106)
- **Explicit.** The way down to Snape’s passage is a narrow stone staircase. (B02-011)
- **Explicit.** Stadium crowd noise dies away completely as you walk in from the steps. (B06-160)

### Where it is

- **Inference.** Eight floors below the seventh floor, in British numbering, is exactly one level under the ground floor, so that is where the dungeons lie. Because: The narrator places them eight floors below the seventh floor. In British numbering seven floors down from the seventh reach the ground floor (7, 6, 5, 4, 3, 2, 1, ground), so the eighth is the first level under it. British numbering is confirmed by the route of six floors plus the marble staircase from the seventh floor to the Entrance Hall, and by seven staircases from the ground floor to the Fat Lady. (B05-110, B04-106, B04-060)
- **Inference.** The text firmly supports only this one level. No second level is counted, though passages towards the Slytherin common room run deeper under the school, so parts may lie lower. Because: The eight floors is the only count, and each route described from the Entrance Hall is a single run of stone steps. The only hints of greater depth are the Book 2 walk towards the Slytherin wall and Harry’s later claim that the common room lies under the lake. (B05-110, B02-011, B02-091, B05-093, B07-028)
- **Explicit.** The ways in described are stone steps down from the Entrance Hall. A door on the right as you come down the marble staircase leads towards Slytherin; Snape’s stairs also open off the hall. (B02-011, B02-091, B03-027, B04-122, B05-072, B05-186, B05-302)
- **Explicit.** These are not the deepest spaces under the school: the pipe to the Chamber of Secrets drops well below them. (B02-141)
- **Explicit.** Snape’s office door stands halfway along a cold passage, and the Potions door has space outside it for a class to queue, at the foot of the steps. (B02-011, B05-034, B05-093)
- **Inference.** Harry’s flight from Snape’s office in 1996, three floors up, would end on the second floor. Because: One level below ground plus three floors is the second floor in British numbering. (B05-222, B05-110)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Walls of rough stone with bracket-mounted torches; dim, gloomy and colder than the main castle. (B05-002, B05-110, B04-069, B04-106, B02-011, B02-042, B01-034)
- **Explicit.** Side rooms and corners open off the passages, voices echo along them, and a stair rises close to the Potions exit. (B01-071, B02-091, B02-106, B04-069)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** Hallowe’en 1992: the passage to the Deathday party was lined with thin black candles burning blue. (B02-042)

### Conflicts

- Harry says the Slytherin common room is under the lake, yet the castle stands on a high cliff over the lake and the dungeons are one storey below ground. Both hold only if the passages descend a long way. (B07-028, B05-110, B02-005, B01-006, B02-091)

### Open questions

- How far the passages descend past the first level to reach the Slytherin common room.
- Whether the Slytherin door and the stairs to Snape’s office are one door or two.

## Deathday party dungeon

One of the larger dungeons, used for Nearly Headless Nick’s party on 31 October 1992, at the end of a passage from the dungeon steps. It took hundreds of ghosts on a dance floor, a thirty-strong orchestra on a platform and a long table across the room. No permanent fittings are described; everything on record is party dressing.

### Size and numbers

- **Explicit.** Called one of the roomier dungeons; in 1992 it held hundreds of ghosts, most of them on a crowded dance floor. (B02-042, B02-043)
- **Explicit.** It also took an orchestra of thirty musical saws on a platform, a chandelier of a thousand black candles, and a long table on the far side. (B02-043, B02-044)
- **Estimate.** Modelling minimum, not canon: at least 300 m² of floor, for example 20 m by 15 m. Because: Hundreds means at least 200 ghosts; at an assumed 1 m² per dancer, 200 m². Thirty players at an assumed 1.5 m² each, 45 m². A long table with walking space, assumed 10 m by 3 m, 30 m². A margin for the walkway round the dance floor brings it to about 300 m². Ghosts need no floor, but the room must read as holding this crowd. (B02-043, B02-044)

### Where it is

- **Explicit.** Reached along a passage from the dungeon steps and round a corner to a doorway; the way out returns up that passage to the steps into the Entrance Hall. (B02-042, B02-046)

### Shape and structure

- **Explicit.** Cold enough for living visitors’ breath to mist. (B02-043)

### How it changes

- **Explicit.** All dressing was for 31 October 1992: black velvet door drapes, draped platform, podium and velvet-covered table. A dozen ghost horses came in through the wall. (B02-042, B02-043, B02-044)

### Open questions

- Which numbered dungeon this is, and where it lies relative to Snape’s rooms.

## Other dungeon rooms

A catch-all for dungeon rooms the text does not connect to the named ones, known only from passing mentions, with nothing to show that any two are the same room. The instances are dungeon five, a room behind a door beyond the Potions room in Riddle’s memory, and the unnamed place where Harry’s Firebolt was held in 1996.

### Size and numbers

- **Inference.** Dungeons carry numbers, so at least five exist. Because: Nearly Headless Nick refers to dungeon five, which needs at least four others unless the numbering skips. (B02-036)

### Where it is

- **Reported.** A memory from about fifty years earlier shows a door round a corner beyond the Potions room, with a very large box beside it. (B02-106)

### How it changes

- **Reported.** In 1992 third-years coated dungeon five’s ceiling in frog brains, and Filch spent a morning cleaning it. (B02-036)
- **Explicit.** In May 1996 Filch took Harry’s Firebolt below to the dungeons. Umbridge said it was strictly guarded; a security troll watching it is only a rumour. (B05-236, B05-275)
